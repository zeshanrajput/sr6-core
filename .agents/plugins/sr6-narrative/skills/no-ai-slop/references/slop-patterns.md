# Detailed Catalog of 29 AI Slop Patterns & Anti-Patterns

This document provides complete descriptions, examples, and fixes for all 29 AI writing patterns flagged by the `no-ai-slop` skill.

---

## 1. Structural & Rhythmic Patterns

### Pattern 1: Binary Contrasts & Pivots
- **Symptom:** AI compulsively contrasts what something is *not* before stating what it *is*.
- *Flagged:* "This is not a clean extraction. It's a slaughter." / "The question isn't whether she could hack the host, but whether she could survive the trace."
- *Fix:* State the point directly. "The extraction turned into a slaughter." / "Surviving the trace was the only problem that mattered."

### Pattern 2: Throat-Clearing Openers
- *Flagged:* "Here's the thing," "Here's what I mean," "Let me be clear," "The uncomfortable truth is."
- *Fix:* Delete the setup phrase completely.

### Pattern 3: Faux-Insight Setups
- *Flagged:* "This is the part most people skip," "What most people get wrong," "The part everyone misses."
- *Fix:* Cut the setup and state the claim directly.

### Pattern 4: Colon Reveals & Fake Drama
- *Flagged:* "The detail that made it terrifying: the drone carried corporate transponders."
- *Fix:* "The drone carried corporate transponders." Use colons for lists, dialogue attributions, and data blocks.

### Pattern 5: Superficial Analysis & Trailing `-ing` Clauses
- *Flagged:* "He tightened his grip on the Ares, highlighting the growing danger."
- *Fix:* "He tightened his grip on the Ares." Let the physical action speak for itself.

### Pattern 6: Importance Puffery
- *Flagged:* "Stands as a testament," "marks a pivotal moment," "plays a vital role," "underscores its significance."
- *Fix:* State the concrete fact.

### Pattern 7: Weasel Attribution
- *Flagged:* "Fixers agree," "rumors suggest," "many runners argue."
- *Fix:* Name the specific contact or delete the hedge.

### Pattern 8: Fake-Strong Verbs
- *Flagged:* "The clinic serves as a safehouse."
- *Fix:* "The clinic is a safehouse."

### Pattern 9: Synonym Cycling
- *Flagged:* Cycling through "the runner," "the operative," "the infiltrator," "the specialist" to refer to one person.
- *Fix:* Use the character's name or a consistent identifier.

### Pattern 10: Negative Listing
- *Flagged:* "Not a corp drone. Not a street punk. A professional."
- *Fix:* "A professional."

### Pattern 11: Dramatic Fragmentation
- *Flagged:* "Cold. Methodical. Deadly." / "That's it. That's the whole run."
- *Fix:* Use complete, braided sentences with active verbs.

### Pattern 12: Robotic Rhythm
- *Flagged:* Identical sentence lengths, repeated subject-verb-object structures, or stacked punchy fragments.
- *Fix:* Vary sentence structure: combine short observations into braided paragraphs with rhythmic variety.

### Pattern 13: Rhetorical Setups
- *Flagged:* "What if I told you...", "Think about it:", "Plot twist:"
- *Fix:* Cut the setup entirely.

### Pattern 14: Fake-Profound Kickers
- *Flagged:* Ending scenes with aphorisms: "Full stop." / "Let that sink in." / "Some debts are paid in blood."
- *Fix:* End on the last concrete physical action or sensory detail.

### Pattern 15: Summary-Recap Endings
- *Flagged:* Final paragraphs summarizing what the scene meant: "Ultimately, Reiko had made her choice."
- *Fix:* Delete the summary paragraph. End immediately after the aftermath beat.

### Pattern 16: Formatting Slop
- *Flagged:* Emoji in headers, mid-sentence bolding for emphasis, unnatural bullet lists in narrative prose.
- *Fix:* Clean standard prose without typography gimmicks.

### Pattern 17: Em-Dash Density
- *Flagged:* Using em-dashes (`—`) in every other sentence as a crutch for rhythm and aside clauses.
- *Fix:* Replace with commas, periods, or braided compound sentences.

## 2. Sensory & Environmental Patterns

### Pattern 18: Olfactory Checklist Template
- *Flagged:* "The alley smelled of wet garbage, stale synth-coffee, and burnt ozone."
- *Fix:* Cut the formulaic list. Use barometric, acoustic, tactile, or thermal sensory cues: "Freezing drizzle pooled along the ferroconcrete, leaking steam from a broken heat exchanger."

### Pattern 19: Lore Preaching & Meta-Exposition
- *Flagged:* "Her dermal plating, a multi-million nuyen custom job from Mitsuhama Biotech, was designed to stop high-caliber rounds."
- *Fix:* Show the physical friction without lecturing the reader: "The round mushroomed against her forearms, the impact jolting into her elbow joints."

### Pattern 20: Thesis Monologuing
- *Flagged:* Characters explaining their thematic tragedy or psychological wounds to strangers.
- *Fix:* Replace with silence, deflection, professional brevity, and subtext.

### Pattern 21: Show-Then-Tell Redundancy (Distrusting the Reader)
- *Flagged:* "She checked the breach seal twice. She was paranoid about contamination."
- *Fix:* "She checked the breach seal twice." Cut the explanatory sentence.

### Pattern 22: Gratuitous Negatives vs. Significant Dramatic Negatives
- *Flagged:* "He did not look up from the commlink. He didn't blink. Without looking back, she stepped out."
- *Fix (Affirmative Staging):* "His eyes remained locked to the commlink display. She stepped into the rain."
- *Exception:* Allow negatives when the absence of a natural human response is itself the dramatic point (*"He's quiet. He doesn't cry."*).

### Pattern 23: Staccato / Single-Sentence Paragraph Habit
- *Flagged:* Stacking 4–6 single-sentence lines sequentially to simulate thriller tension.
- *Fix:* Braid into cohesive 3–6 sentence paragraphs weaving physical action, sensory cues, and consequences.

### Pattern 24: False Agency / Inanimate Anthropomorphism
- *Flagged:* "The cyberware yearned for connection." / "The alley swallowed them."
- *Fix:* "She keyed the interface." / "They stepped into the darkness of the alley."

### Pattern 25: Tricolon Fatigue (Rule of Two over Three)
- *Flagged:* "Fast, quiet, and deadly." / "The damp cedar, the distant siren, and the cold neon."
- *Fix:* Prefer a single sharp detail or an authentic pair.

## 3. Dialogue, Telemetry & Mindspeech Discipline

### Pattern 26: Weightless Radio Chatter vs. Somatic Co-Presence
- *Flagged:* In shared-mind or co-presence dynamics, characters verbally asking what the other sees (*"What's behind that door?" / "It's an automated turret!"*).
- *Fix:* Shared sensory feeds render telemetry directly. Characters speak only to regulate biology, interpret ontological meaning, or negotiate tactical consensus.

### Pattern 27: Anti-Thesis Sloganeering (Trust the Reader)
- *Flagged:* Characters announcing moral arcs as bumper-sticker dialogue (*"I will hold your humanity," "You gave me life, I will protect yours"*).
- *Fix:* Dramatize through sacrifice, tactile tradecraft, and subtext.

### Pattern 28: One Speaker Per Paragraph (MANDATORY)
- *Flagged:* Combining spoken lines from two different speakers into a single paragraph.
- *Fix:* Every speaker change requires a new paragraph. Braid dialogue with that speaker's physical actions (2–4 sentences per turn).

### Pattern 29: Mindspeech & Non-Acoustic Communication Discipline
- *Flagged:* Formatting direct DI/technomancer/Monad resonant mindspeech with double-quotes (`"..."`) or treating it as acoustic speech.
- *Fix:*
  - Pure non-acoustic mindspeech (DIs, sprites, resonant sparks): format in italics without quotes (`*...*`).
  - Spoken acoustic dialogue or simulated metahuman voice synthesis: format in double-quotes (`"..."`).
  - DIs code-switch to simulated voice (`"..."`) when communicating with metahumans to mask origin.
