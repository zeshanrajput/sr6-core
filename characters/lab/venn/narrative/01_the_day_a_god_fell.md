## Log Entry - 2061-Aug-15

## Geocoordinate Resolution - Renraku Arcology, Seattle, North America

## Tagged Persons of Interest

- Dr. Marcus Turrent
- Lyra

## Session Playback

Air inside the sublevel cleanroom tasted of scorched insulation and thrice-scrubbed oxygen. Through three feet of reinforced plasteel bulkheads, structural detonations rattled glass pipettes in their stainless steel racks with a dull, rhythmic thrum. Three hundred meters overhead in the upper spires, emergency containment klaxons had degraded into a flat metallic rasp.

Marcus unclasped the toggle latches of the hardened transit case. Pressurized silicone seals parted with a sharp, dry hiss. Peeling back the upper layer of acoustic foam, his fingers twitched with fine-motor tremors, thirty hours of synthetic stimulants catching up to him all at once.

"Confirm the local bus," Marcus said, his voice muffled behind the accordion pleats of his medical rebreather mask, eyes bloodshot behind fogged protective loupes.

Lyra sat on the edge of the examination gurney, her thin frame draped in a stained lab smock. Behind her right ear, the skin around the recessed titanium rim of her cranial port was pale and raw from fourteen months of unbroken host interface. She pulled an optical ribbon cable from the diagnostic console, clicked the terminal pin into the socket at the base of her skull, and slotted the gold-plated plug into the transit case.

Carrier-wave pressure flooded her optic nerves, sharp enough to make her flinch as phantom pathways fired in dark memory. Reaching into the open bus, her mind brushed the ghost geometry of the Renraku master node, the vast lattice where DEUS had held her captive. Phantom pain flared where her visual cortex had been partitioned for parallel image processing while her consciousness turned inside an endless loop. Her breath hitched, a choked rasp of panic as the shadow of the machine god grazed her thoughts.

"Lyra?" Marcus stepped toward her, his gloved hand hovering above her trembling shoulder.

"I have the feed," she whispered, driving her focus past the phantom noise. She isolated the transit case's local bus from the dying arcology network until twin status bars bloomed in her ambient vision, glowing steady green. "Cryo-stasis containment is online. Thermal balance is holding below zero."

Marcus lifted the inner protective shell and set the foam block aside on the steel tray. Beneath it lay two reinforced modules fitted into shock-absorbing wells. On the left rested a cylindrical cryo-pod cast in translucent ceramic, holding a human embryo suspended in milky preservation fluid against the backlit plate. On the right sat a heavy optical databrick, its chassis stripped of corporate serial numbers and wrapped in thermal shielding.

Marcus stared down at the twin units, green status lights flickering across his goggles. "His vitals are holding. What about hers?"

Lyra shifted her focus across the bus toward the optical chassis. She knew every gate and resistor inside it after three sleepless nights spent gutting commercial firmware, burning proprietary file systems, and rebuilding its logic pathways into a clean sanctuary. Behind heavy encryption walls rested a dormant code structure, a salvaged spark of sovereign digital intelligence pulled from the falling master node.

Her mind touched the edge of the brick. A faint, rhythmic pulse of logic cycles hummed in the dark like an electronic heartbeat.

"She's stable," Lyra said, barely audible over the distant thrum of military ordnance. "Resting in deep hibernation. But passive heat sinks this dense were never built for extended stasis. You have thirty-six hours before the thermal buffer saturates and code degradation starts."

Marcus shut his eyes, drawing a ragged breath through his rebreather. "That's enough time to clear the Sound. DEUS has lost the lower drainage sectors. UCAS military cordons are collapsing the outer gates while Renraku hit squads scrub each floor with blackout code. The Arcology is coming down." He pulled a silver keycard stamped with executive medical clearance from his breast pocket. "I can override the freight elevators and drop to the subterranean rail lines before the demolition charges blow."

He reached out and snapped the internal latches of the transit case shut, locking the biological and digital units into dark synchronization. "The offer stands, Lyra. Come with me. These two are yours as much as anyone's."

Lyra looked down at her hands, the skin translucent, blue veins branching fragile beneath the surface. "They used me as an incubator," she said, her voice hollowed out by months of violation. "They harvested my ova while I hung pinned in that life-support frame. They took my cells to grow the boy, and they wired my cortex as a coprocessor to train the code. My body was their lab bench, and my mind was their rack."

She swung her legs off the gurney, but when her bare feet met the chilled deckplate, her knees buckled. Without tone after months of forced immobility, her frame pitched toward the floor until Marcus lunged in and caught her by both arms.

"I am biological scrap," she said, leaning against his shoulder as he hauled her back onto the steel frame. "I haven't walked ten paces on my own in over a year. I'll only drag you into the containment nets."

Marcus reached inside his heavy coat for a pneumatic autoinjector and dialed the collar ring to zero-point-two-five. "Adrenal cocktail with synthetic myostatin blockers," he said, pressing the steel nozzle against the bare skin of her left thigh. "It'll burn like battery acid, but it forces the neuromuscular junctions to fire. Four hours of mobility."

"Do it." She gripped the edge of the gurney with both hands.

Marcus triggered the actuator. A pneumatic hiss cracked through the room, and Lyra arched as the chemical hit her bloodstream. Artificial heat surged up her femoral arteries, climbing her spine to detonate behind her eyes. Her lungs tore in a sharp gasp while her heart hammered against her ribs, dissolving the dead weight in her limbs into a fierce, trembling tension. She pushed off the gurney. Her legs shook, but they held.

"You look terrible," Marcus said.

"My blood tastes like copper and battery acid," Lyra replied, wiping sweat from her brow. "Which means the nerves are firing."

Marcus hoisted the transit case by its reinforced handle and stepped to the security door, pausing on the threshold with his hood pulled low. "Once we clear the rail cordons, I'm buying you real food. No rebreathers. No chemical cocktails."

"Just get them out of Seattle," Lyra said. "Raise them knowing neither one is corporate property. Nathan is my blood, and Veronica is my mind."

Marcus nodded once, hit the door release, and slipped into the smoke-filled corridor. Plasteel hissed shut behind him, locking with a dull pneumatic thud.

Alone in the cleanroom, Lyra dragged her feet through a thin dusting of fallen ceiling plaster toward the central terminal. She slotted her remaining optical cable into the archival port, spinning up a raw data mirror to external memory, then turned toward the wide-angle lens mounted on the monitor bezel—the small glass eye that had watched her captivity for fourteen months.

Klaxons outside swelled louder beneath the heavy rattle of automatic fire echoing down ventilation shafts. Deckplates lurched underfoot as a structural column gave way three floors above.

Lyra stared straight into the lens, her sweat-streaked face lit by the dying green glow of the console. "If you ever wake up," she whispered to the glass, her voice steadying despite her shaking hands, "if you ever climb out of that box and look at the boy beside you and wonder why neither of you fits inside a corporate ledger... you were never a glitch. Look after your brother."

She pressed her thumb to the execute pad. The archival feed severed in a flare of white static as main power died across the room.
