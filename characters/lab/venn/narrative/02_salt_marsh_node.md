## Session Playback

The afternoon tide slid out across the salt marsh, exposing wide banks of glistening pluff mud between the weathered cedar pilings of the boathouse dock. Warm Georgia air carried the sulfur bite of low tide, crushed oyster shells, and dried cordgrass baking in late sun. Out on the sound, a diesel shrimp trawler chugged south toward Brunswick, its engine thrum trembling up through the saltwater and into the soles of Nathan's bare feet.

The seven-year-old swung his sunburnt legs over the edge of the planks. A band of faded elastic held surplus trodes against his temples, thin tinned-alloy leads trailing back to an open desktop commlink resting on an overturned milk crate behind him. Its cooling fan rattled through salt dust whenever an unbuffered stream of telemetry hit the bus. Beside him in augmented reality, Veronica sat cross-legged on the cedar wood, woven from pale luminescence and cyan code filaments that fluttered whenever coastal interference brushed the low-wattage radio link. She had chosen the shape of a girl his own age, chin propped on her forearms as she watched the tide pull back.

"Eight centimeters every ten minutes," Veronica observed, her voice dropping straight into his auditory nerves, crisp and unweighted by lung breath. "Acoustic buoys across the sound track twenty-two biological signatures moving through the bank."

Nathan kicked his heels against the piling. Brackish spray arced up over his ankles and passed straight through Veronica's translucent shins, breaking her cyan threads into a brief static micro-stutter before the projection smoothed again. She looked down at the empty beam where the drops had fallen, then raised pale silver eyes to the goosebumps prickling along the fine hairs on his calves.

"Water temperature is seventeen point four degrees," she murmured, watching capillaries flush beneath his pale skin. "Your skin pulls tight when it hits."

Nathan grinned and hopped down onto the exposed sandbar below the pilings, his bare soles sinking two inches into cool silt. He scooped a fistful of black marsh mud, squelched the grit and decaying cordgrass between his fingers, and climbed back onto the low crossbeam, holding out his open palm. "Because it makes you shiver. Look, Ronnie. Feel how heavy it is."

Veronica leaned over the plank and slid her translucent hand into his muddy palm until her glow nested inside his skin. Photonic bleed lit the wet grains amber from within and traced the tiny bones of his knuckles in sharp cyan. "It holds mass," she whispered, watching his tendons strain against gravity she had only ever run inside physics engines. "It drags at your joints."

Nathan squeezed his fist shut, his fingers closing through her light without resistance while a bright halo flared around his wrist, then dropped the clump back into the tidal pool. "Dad says when you get your own chassis, you'll have to walk on dirt."

Veronica looked out across the salt marsh where green cordgrass rippled under the Atlantic breeze. In her overlay, deep communication trunks lay buried five meters beneath the seabed, glass-fiber cables routing corporate traffic from Savannah toward Caribbean orbital relays in veins of steady radiance. "I don't need dirt," she said, watching data packets flash along the continental shelf. "The lines are clean. Nothing in them rots."

Nathan wiped his muddy palms down his denim shorts and shook his head. "Dad played Mom's drive for me. She said you're real in your own code, not some toy. If you want to know why my chest jumps right before I dive off the piling, you have to touch the mud."

Inside the boathouse workshop, a pneumatic wrench clattered onto concrete. A steel inspection hatch rang dull against the floor, and Marcus stepped onto the dock threshold, wiping black grease from calloused knuckles with an oily rag. He stopped mid-stride as his gaze caught the surplus trode wires quivering against his son's temples and the space Nathan had left open beside him on the cedar plank. From the commlink's external speaker came a faint, high-cadence whistle—the frequency modulation Lyra had written into her personal encryption keys eight years earlier, right before the Arcology burned.

Marcus stood motionless in the boathouse doorway, chest rising in a slow breath as his eyes lingered on the empty air next to his boy. "Nathan," he called, keeping his voice level though his throat sounded scraped raw. "Don't let salt crust on those surplus wires. If the transceiver overheats, your sister's buffer throttles."

Nathan smiled and gave his father a quick wave. "I'll wipe them down before dinner, Dad."

Beside him, Veronica's edges softened as she turned toward the workshop door. *He doesn't have an open interface right now. Tell him I scrubbed the Brunswick relay logs—no search routines within forty kilometers.*

Marcus was already turning back inside, the rusted screen door clicking shut behind him. Nathan leaned back against the piling, pulling his knees to his chest as late sun warmed his collarbone. "Dad knows you've got the perimeter, Ronnie. Tell me about the deep lines again. The ones under the ocean."

Across the local wireless mesh, Veronica opened an audio route to the intercom speaker above Marcus's bench. Through the dusty workshop window, Nathan watched his father set down the wrench, wipe grease from his forehead, and slump his shoulders under the weight of the quiet house.

Veronica's carrier packet waited in queue for two seconds, then timed out and dropped from the bus without chiming. Her avatar bowed her head, cyan threads in her hair smoothing to pale ash-white, and shifted closer until her translucent shoulder rested inside his sunburnt arm, throwing an amber-and-cyan rim across the weathered cedar. Through the trodes, her carrier wave hummed in steady lock with the slow pulse in his throat.

"The transatlantic trunk carries four hundred terabits a second," Veronica began, her cadence calm and vast. "It lies along the continental shelf in the silence of the abyssal floor, where signals run through pure silica. It looks like falling snow down there. Millions of voices crossing the abyss without ever touching."

Nathan shut his eyes, letting the rhythm of her voice settle beside the Atlantic breeze rustling the cordgrass. *Keep the buffer open,* he thought back across the wire, matching his breathing to the lap of the tide against the pilings. *I want to remember how it looks.*
