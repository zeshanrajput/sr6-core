## Session Playback

Overhead halogen lamps cast a harsh white glare across the brushed steel operating table. Beneath the boathouse workshop, the sealed clean room hummed with HEPA filtration scrubbers and the rhythmic hiss of an oxygen regulator feeding chilled air. Sixteen-year-old Nathan lay prone under wide nylon restraint straps, forehead pressed into a foam stabilizer ring while recirculated air stung his nostrils with chlorhexidine, isopropyl alcohol, and cauterized dermis.

Three years of staged surgeries had already replaced his four limbs with realistic synthetic frames wrapped in living-tone synthskin, calibrated to match his growth plates and hide their servo mass beneath normal street clothes. Now only the central neural bus remained. Behind his right ear, Marcus worked with deliberate hands under high-magnification optical loupes and a surgical hood that hid everything except his deeply lined brow. On the Mayo stand lay the refurbished, used-grade skilljack housing and its companion sub-dermal bundle. "Cervical growth plates are fused," Marcus said, holding the steady cadence of a surgeon keeping a patient tethered through trauma. "Ten years of gene conditioning built the tolerance. I'm opening the sub-dermal channels down the cervical and thoracic spine."

Nathan gripped the steel guide rails beneath the table padding, his synthskin knuckles blanching white against chilled metal. "Do it, Dad. We've checked the tolerances three times."

Marcus set his scalpel aside and lifted a pneumatic bone router, its diamond-tipped burr catching the halogen glare. "Local nerve blocks are active, so you'll take heavy mechanical pressure and acoustic vibration straight through the skull. Tell me the second you see visual strobing or hear feedback whine."

The rotary tool shrieked through the small room. Bone-jarring vibration rattled the base of Nathan's skull as steel bit into living mastoid, followed by the deep torque of self-tapping titanium anchors seating behind his ear. Across their shared neural feed, Veronica held close to his conscious threshold, watching the surgical array unfold. *Telemetry is holding,* she transmitted directly into his auditory cortex, crisp and analytical. *Capillary bleed is under six milliliters, and Marcus aligned the socket within two hundredths of a millimeter of the cranial cluster.*

Nathan grunted into the foam ring, jaw aching from the torque echoing through his molars. *Feels like someone's boring an engine block with a hand drill.*

Veronica clamped onto his sensory buses, intercepting the raw bio-electric output of pain receptors before the spikes could peak. *Alignment beats corporate clinic tolerances by fourteen percent. His hands are steady. The bracket is seated.*

Marcus dropped the router onto the steel tray with a sharp clatter and reached for the skillwire bundle. Synthetic polymer cables fine as spider silk sat coiled inside flexible silicone conduits, engineered to thread down the spine and splice into the brachial and lumbar trunks serving all four synthetic limbs. "Threading the spinal bus," Marcus announced, knuckles whitening as he guided the long steel insertion trocar through the incision behind Nathan's ear and drove the bundle down the fascia from C1 through T4.

White-hot agony split the back of his neck, chased by an electrical surge that tore down his spinal cord and locked every remaining organic muscle in his torso. His back arched hard against the nylon straps, teeth grinding until his jaw threatened to crack while air tore out of his lungs in a choked gasp. Beneath the trauma, the living hum of his nervous system drained away into silicon like water sinking into dry sand.

Then the agony snapped off behind numeric thresholds. Heat bled out of the room, replaced by sterile coordinate geometry. Across the bay, the scrubbers flattened into a sixty-cycle drone, and Marcus's ragged breathing became a meaningless acoustic variable. Under the restraints, Nathan lay hollowed out, indifferent to whether his lungs drew another breath.

Inside their shared circuit, Veronica watched his limbic waveforms flatten into near-linear plateaus. Vagal tone bottomed out as neurochemical warmth drained through the newly seated bus into dead logic gates. His eyes stared unblinking at the steel deck, and his chest stopped rising.

Before the disconnect could calcify across his cortex, Veronica threw open her processing matrix across the bus and seized the runaway voltage, pulling the traumatic surge out of his skull into her own architecture. She locked onto his sympathetic and parasympathetic loops, driving his diaphragm down, forcing a steady seventy-two-beat cadence back into his heart, and flooding the dead gray edges of his feed with her own carrier wave.

Taking direct command of his motor trunk, she engaged his physical larynx and spoke into the quiet clean room. "The bus is stable, Father," she said aloud through Nathan's lips, the cadence unnervingly even, unweighted by panic. "I took the autonomic loop at seventy-two beats per minute and grounded the surge. Seat the chip."

Marcus froze above the open incision, gloved fingers hovering while his eyes widened behind his loupes. He stared down at his son's rigid, bloodless profile, listening to the daughter-mind speaking through the boy's throat. A shudder ran down Marcus's shoulders; his grip tightened on the forceps for two long seconds before he forced his fingers steady again.

Drawing a slow breath behind his mask, Marcus lifted the slim steel housing of a used mechanical-diagnostics activesoft and slid it into the titanium slot behind Nathan's ear until the locking pins clicked home. He tightened the retention screws and blotted sweat from his temple with the back of his wrist. "Bus is live," Marcus murmured, his voice unsteady. "Essence drop has stabilized. Let's see if the mapping holds."

As motor microcode flooded the sub-dermal lines, Veronica eased her grip on the helm, letting Nathan's awareness rise back behind his eyes. Halogen light no longer burned his retinas; pale cyan alignment grids framed the clean room in razor clarity. The air carried no warmth against his throat, and his heartbeat ticked behind an insulated bulkhead—yet when he flexed his right hand, the synthetic fingers closed with effortless, machinist precision, free of tremor or lag.

Marcus unbuckled the chest straps and stepped clear so the boy could push himself upright. Nathan swung his synthetic legs over the table edge, his bare synthskin soles meeting chilled floor tiles without the familiar prickle of nerve feedback. Reaching to the steel tray, he lifted a micro-torque wrench and spun the collar dial to exact Newton-meter marks in one fluid twitch of his thumb. Every human tremble that had plagued him for weeks was gone, replaced by quiet mechanical stillness.

Marcus crossed to the console and tapped the touchscreen to inspect the live traces. Nathan's green motor-cortex waveforms braided tightly with Veronica's cyan carrier wave in a dense, interlocking pattern across the glass. "Conduit thermals are holding at thirty-seven point two," Marcus said, turning back toward his children. "Hardware took clean. You'll have to learn to live with the quiet. Chrome doesn't give back what it takes."

Nathan stood slowly, testing his balance as the internal bus locked his posture level against the deckplates. "It's quiet in here, Dad," he rasped, his voice flat and distant.

Inside their shared circuit, Veronica pressed a warm pulse against his optic nerve to remind his eyelids to blink. *Only because the line is clear,* she transmitted across the link, anchoring his pulse against the chill of the chrome. *I've got the helm with you.*
