## Session Playback

Warm autumn rain fell heavy over the Savannah, stirring tidal mud, diesel exhaust, and fried dough along the waterfront stalls of River Street. In a narrow brick alley behind a commercial cold-storage facility, Veronica leaned against damp masonry while feverish cellular heat subsided under her skin. Resculpting demanded twenty grueling minutes of metabolic burn: subcutaneous nanite swarms had remodeled facial cartilage and muscle fibers while shifting the malleable outer synthskin and polymer contours of all four synthetic limbs into a narrower, lighter silhouette. Her collarbone and cheeks still radiated furnace heat, yet when she raised her hands into the alley's amber glow, the fingers were slender, agile, and warm enough to pass any street glance.

*Balance holding?* Nathan checked from the passenger seat of their shared circuit, steadying their autonomic breathing rhythm so she could dedicate full focus to motor coordination.

Veronica took one deliberate step onto wet cobblestones, discovering her center of gravity sat three inches lower than Nathan's usual stride. Physical pull tugged hard at her calves, demanding constant micro-corrections from her inner ear as damp boot soles bit into slick stone. "Air resistance is real," she whispered aloud, testing the clear alto vibrating through her own throat. She paused mid-step, drawing measured thoracic breath to gauge how much lung volume a single spoken sentence demanded. "In the Matrix, inertia carries no drag. Down here, everything pushes back."

*You learn the friction,* Nathan projected with quiet warmth, nudging a posture marker across their link. *Drop your hips an inch and let the spinal wires take the balance load.*

Veronica eased her shoulders, letting the sub-dermal bus absorb the sway, and stepped out into the pedestrian crush of River Street. Neon signage in amber, jade, and magenta smeared across wet asphalt beneath a dense squall of augmented reality, with holographic menus bobbing above sizzling carts, discount tags drifting through crowd traffic, and glowing street graffiti crawling across brick facades. Off-shift dockworkers, Cape Canaveral shuttle crews, and riverboat gamblers jostled beneath canvas awnings in a thick wash of sputtering sesame oil, overlapping dialects, and humid river wind chilling her cotton jacket.

Passing an open-air craft stall, she trailed her fingertips across a row of woven sweetgrass baskets. Even through synthskin, tactile pickups fed raw, prickly roughness straight into her buffer, a granular imperfection no matrix texture map had ever resolved. Beside the awning, a weather kiosk chimed low humidity metrics she could feel raising damp hair along the nape of her neck. Two buskers sat on overturned oil drums playing slide guitars, steel strings throwing complex harmonic overtones through humid air that hit her chest cavity before her audio decoders finished parsing them.

*Hungry yet?* Nathan dropped an uncompressed memory into her feed: garlic blistering in chili oil, charred pork, and his father leaning against a railing years ago. *Hawker two blocks down uses real scotch bonnets. Let's see what your buffer makes of real ginger.*

"Route the marker," Veronica said aloud, her lips curling as the messy warmth of his memory splashed across her processor cores. Then she frowned, catching the raw emotional bleed tangled inside the image, and added in a quick whisper against the rain, "Trim the nostalgia tag next time. Half that packet was salt air from St. Simon's."

*You wanted uncurated,* Nathan shot back, dry amusement rippling down the wire. *You get the whole plate, grease and all.*

"Fair," she conceded, watching physical raindrops streak neon across her wireframes as she stepped toward the riverfront crowds. "Next month I'm dragging you into the Endless Archives. You'll see what my architecture looks like without the mud."

*Deal. Move it before the stall closes.*

Veronica followed the HUD marker under a red canvas awning where white steam billowed thick with seared pork, crushed garlic, and caramelized cane syrup. An elderly ork vendor with heavy silver tusk rings looked up from a smoking cast-iron grill. "Two skewers, sweet and hot," Veronica ordered, lifting a certified credstick between two steady fingers.

The vendor tapped the stick against her reader, slid a paper tray of charred pork belly across the counter, and grinned toward the water. "Careful with that glaze, girl. It bites back."

Carrying the tray to the wrought-iron railing above the Savannah River, Veronica watched a container barge glide downstream toward the Atlantic, emerald navigation lamps slicing through churning brown water. She lifted a wooden skewer and took a deliberate bite. First came crisp rendered fat searing against her tongue, followed by salt, garlic, and sweet cane syrup—then scotch bonnet fire detonated across her palate and raced down her throat.

A violent sensory micro-stutter tore through their shared circuit as chemical heat slammed straight from biological taste buds into her digital buffer. Tear ducts snapped open, spilling hot tears over her lashes while pulse and adrenaline spiked hard enough to jolt Nathan in the passenger seat. His instinctive autonomic grab clamped onto her heart rate before she even finished swallowing.

*Hold still—your vagal load just jumped forty percent,* Nathan snapped down the wire, pouring dampening code across the nerve trunk.

Veronica wiped her wet sleeve across her cheek, then broke into a bright, uninhibited laugh that drew amused glances from two passing dockworkers. "Stop throttling it," she breathed aloud, pausing to catch thoracic air as her voice shook with fierce delight. She took a second, larger bite of spiced meat, savoring the sting across her lips. "It is messy, inefficient, and completely irrational. And it's ours."

*Meatspace has its perks,* Nathan chuckled inside the quiet behind her eyes, though he flagged a stray drip of glaze in her HUD before it could stain her cuff. *Just remember real pork comes straight out of our commlink repair fund.*

As drizzle thickened into a steady coastal downpour, Veronica leaned against cold iron railing, taking in the drag of wet wool across her shoulders and the heavy anchor of a full stomach. When they turned toward the late-night river skiff and she yielded the helm, the nanites cooled back into resting baseline, remodeling jaw cartilage and synthetic limb contours into a broader frame with a dull ache along his jaw and ribs.

Nathan stepped back onto the driver's side. His stride stretched longer and heavier, and his synthetic hands ran noticeably cooler at the wrist seams, yet the hollow numbness of his chrome held an afterglow of capsaicin and sweet ginger. Across the cabin glass of the ferry, Veronica's translucent avatar settled beside him on the fiberglass bench, silver light shimmering against rain-streaked Plexiglas over the steady thrum of marine diesels.

*Archive that buffer,* Nathan transmitted softly across their link, flexing mechanical fingers that still tingled with phantom spice. *The wires flatten everything out to gray plastic. I forgot what hot food tasted like.*

Veronica leaned her glowing shoulder inside his arm, locking the thermal and chemical telemetry permanently into their shared memory bank. *Every calorie, every degree of heat, and every drop of rain,* she answered down the wire, holding the warmth inside her architecture so the cold chrome could never claim him whole.
