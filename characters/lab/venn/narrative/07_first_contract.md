## Session Playback

A cold winter drizzle fell across the rusted rail spurs of the Fulton industrial yards, pooling between the blackened ballast gravel to reflect the distant sodium floodlights in dull amber streaks. The damp night air carried the sharp chemical bite of creosote-soaked ties, wet coal dust, and diesel exhaust drifting from the Atlanta freight bypass. In the shadowed alcove between two stacked shipping containers, Nathan leaned against the corrugated steel, his dark oilskin duster buttoned tight against the Georgia chill while his skilljack hummed with a faint sub-dermal warmth behind his right ear. The *Activesoft: Firearms (Rating 6)* streamed high-cadence motor microcode directly into his shoulder and forearm muscles, turning his grip on the composite pistol into cold, automated muscle memory wired straight into his tendons.

Through their shared neural link, Veronica projected a real-time tactical matrix overlay directly onto his optic nerves, highlighting three private security guards inside the staging warehouse and two automated surveillance turrets mounted high on the ceiling trusses. Without exchanging a word, she parsed the unshielded wireless diagnostic tap on the warehouse climate regulator, identifying the maglock on the personnel hatch and compiling an intrusion vector in less than four hundred milliseconds. Nathan pushed off the steel container, crossing the wet gravel in a low, economical sprint as the skillwires dynamically adjusted his center of gravity, his saltwater boots making no sound against the ballast as Veronica struck through the Matrix.

Her digital presence hit the local security network like a surgical blade, bypassing the host firewall with an exploit that looped the optical feeds of the overhead cameras and dropped the electronic deadbolts with a muffled mechanical click. Nathan breached the threshold the instant the latch cleared, stepping into the concrete loading bay as the three guards scrambled from their metal desks to reach for holstered sidearms. Before their weapons cleared leather, Nathan's skillwires fired with flawless mechanical precision: his right arm snapped through three firing arcs in rapid succession, the heavy capsule rounds rupturing on impact to release pressurized anesthetic gel that dropped all three guards to the floor in deep, harmless sedation.

"Guards down," Nathan murmured aloud, stepping over the unconscious men as he moved into the open warehouse.

"Surveillance loop holding for three minutes and twenty seconds," Veronica confirmed in crisp auditory telemetry, highlighting container Alpha-Seven forty meters ahead under the flickering fluorescents. Nathan jogged to the designated cargo crate, wedging a portable pneumatic pry-bar under the reinforced shipping latches until the thermal seal cracked open with a sharp hiss, exposing neat, refrigerated rows of military-grade neuro-immunosuppressants and gene-stabilization packs stamped with pristine Evo Pharmaceutical serials.

*We take what we need for our activesoft subscriptions and nanite maintenance,* Nathan said softly across their internal link, hoisting two heavy cases into his tactical duffel bag. *That covers our living expenses and diagnostic cycles through the spring.*

*And three cases for the Fulton squatter perimeter,* Veronica added firmly, projecting a highlighted delivery route across his vision. *Dr. Ortiz has been treating cyber-rejection cases with expired saline and black-market anti-inflammatories since November. The physical extraction risk of the extra weight is offset by community stability.*

*Parnell is going to complain about the reduced haul,* Nathan noted with a faint, wry grin, grabbing a heavy hand trolley from the aisle and stacking the additional crates onto the metal bed. *He's not in the charity business.*

*Parnell operates on short-term margin,* Veronica transmitted with crisp conviction. *We operate on long-term survival. Securing a reliable, beholden street medic ensures our own surgical anonymity when a run goes sideways.*

*Fair enough,* Nathan agreed, gripping the trolley handle as the skillwires dynamically adjusted his center of gravity. He rolled the heavy cart out through the secondary loading bay into the rain, loading the cases into the back of their beat-up civilian delivery van before the warehouse alarm could cycle back online.

Forty minutes later, the van pulled into the dark alley behind a shuttered storefront in the Fulton sprawl, where steam hissed from broken storm drains into the freezing drizzle. Dr. Caleb Ortiz emerged onto the rear loading ramp in a blood-stained surgical apron, his tired eyes widening in sheer terror and disbelief as he inspected the pristine Evo stamps on the insulated containers. "Are you out of your minds?" Ortiz whispered, his hands trembling as he touched the cold seals, glancing frantically toward the alley mouth for corporate retrieval teams. "If Evo's regional asset protection traces these lot numbers to my clinic, they will burn this entire block to the pavement with white phosphorus."

Nathan stepped back into the shadows of the alley, his face concealed beneath the collar of his heavy duster while Veronica projected her silver avatar beside him, her cyan light illuminating the doctor's steaming breath in the cold air. "The transponders are scrubbed and the manifest was wiped at the rail switch," Nathan replied calmly, his voice steady with unyielding certainty. "No one is tracing these to your door, Doctor. Use them for the street kids and keep your clinic open."

At midnight, they met Parnell in a secluded corner booth of a subterranean dive bar beneath the roaring concrete arches of the Atlanta transit interchange. The stout dwarf fixer adjusted his gold-rimmed data glasses, inspecting the remaining pharmaceutical crates beneath the table with calculating scrutiny while running an escrow handshake on his commlink. "Evo military stock is hot enough to blister paint, kid," Parnell grunted, leaning back to bite the tip off a cheap synthetic cigar while docking his thirty-percent laundering fee. "And your delivery is three cases light from the manifest. You're cutting your own margins to play street savior in Fulton."

As the data synced, Parnell squinted through his biometric lenses, tapping his display as he analyzed the live PAN telemetry streaming from their link. On the monitor, two distinct neural wave signatures overlapped in the center of the graph, pairing Nathan's biological spikes with Veronica's Resonant digital carrier wave in an interlocking pattern. "Look at this scrambled telemetry," Parnell scoffed with a rough chuckle, turning the screen around. "Two separate minds jammed into one skull like a glitchy Venn diagram. Around here, running half-wired usually gets you buried in the scrap yards."

Nathan looked at the glowing graphic on the screen, seeing the exact mathematical intersection that Lyra had proven twenty years earlier in the Renraku sublevels. In the back of their mind, Veronica's presence flared with quiet, fierce pride, her digital resonance aligning seamlessly with his own heartbeat as their voices merged into a single, unified tone through their physical lips. "Venn works," they answered together, taking the certified credstick from the table and sliding it into the duster's inner pocket.

They climbed the concrete stairwell out of the dive bar into the cold Atlanta night, stepping onto the rain-slicked pavement beneath the towering, overhead suspension tracks. High above the street, automated freight mag-rails roared through the freezing drizzle, sending showers of pale amber sparks cascading across the dark asphalt while commercial holo-billboards flickered against the low storm clouds. Veronica projected their updated financial ledger and local Matrix nodes directly into their visual field, logging forty-two hundred clean Nuyen in escrow and establishing a secure shadow repository under their new handle. Pulling the heavy duster tight against the wind, two minds moving in seamless biological and digital synchronization, Venn stepped out from under the transit arches and vanished into the grit.
