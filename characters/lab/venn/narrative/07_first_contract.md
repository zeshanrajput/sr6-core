## Session Playback

Winter drizzle fell across rusted rail spurs in the Fulton yards, pooling between blackened ballast gravel to catch sodium floodlights in dull amber streaks. Night air carried creosote from soaked ties, coal dust, and diesel exhaust drifting off the freight bypass. Nathan waited in the narrow shadows between two stacked shipping containers. His oilskin duster was buttoned tight against the Georgia chill while the skilljack hummed warm behind his right ear. Beneath synthetic skin, four limbs ran cool along their vascular seams, servos primed and silent. A streamed close-quarters activesoft settled down his spinal wires, turning his grip on the suppressed pistol into borrowed reflex.

Across their shared optic feed, Veronica painted a live tactical overlay. She tapped an unshielded wireless diagnostic port on the exterior climate regulator, mapping the personnel hatch maglock in crisp cyan geometry. Her Living Persona pressed against the warehouse host's perimeter, blind to isolated sub-grids humming behind insulated metal siding. In three Resonant strokes, she peeled back the exterior firewall and looped overhead camera feeds. The deadbolt primed. He shifted his weight across wet gravel, synthetic knees absorbing the crouch as Veronica dropped the latch with one muffled click.

Nathan cleared the concrete threshold the instant the lock released. Low and fast beneath flickering fluorescents, he drove forward into the bay. Two uniformed guards scrambled up from metal desks near the door, hands slapping toward holsters before the streamed software whipped his right arm through two clean arcs. Heavy gel rounds cracked into sternums and collarbones. Both men dropped backward across overturned chairs before clearing leather. At the far console, however, a gaunt night-shift attendant lunged sideways instead of reaching for a weapon.

The young worker slammed his palm against a recessed copper relay beneath the desk lip, bypassing the looped warehouse host on a hardwired analog line. Blast shutters shrieked down ceiling tracks toward the loading doors. An overhead sentry mount dropped from its housing with a whine of greased gears, twin barrels traversing straight onto his chest. Live slugs chewed concrete slabs at his boots as the kid scrambled backward over spilled manifests. Nathan dove hard behind a bolted pallet rack while shrapnel and pulverized stone sprayed the bay floor.

Automatic fire stitched industrial shelving above his head, tearing through his oilskin sleeve and gouging a hot furrow along his left forearm. Synthskin parted. Pale hydraulic fluid beaded over dark carbon-alloy, running cold where concrete dust caked the seam. Inside the Faraday-thick bay, the sentry mount's short-range diagnostic link finally bled into their shared sensorium, sharp and jagged against Veronica's Living Persona. She dove into that screaming local mesh while ricochets rattled their eardrums, taking raw signal static across her Resonant edge to pry open the weapon's optical bus.

Adrenaline surged through Nathan's starved organic heart and collided with the skillwires, snapping the warehouse flat and monochrome. Cordite and ozone vanished. In their place rose cold silver geometry: firing arcs, shutter descent clearance, and the trembling thermal bloom behind the overturned desk. The boy was no longer human. He was an uncontained variable with an exposed carotid pulse. He swung his right arm around the rack edge, finger tightening on the trigger to put a point-blank shot through the exposed throat.

*Hold the line! Do not fire!* Veronica's mindspeech cracked across the overlap, stripped of clinical calm as she split bandwidth between the ceiling bus and Nathan's runaway spine. She flooded his cervical loop with a heavy vagal brake to choke down the adrenaline spike, seizing the motor bridge to his right elbow servos half a beat before the trigger broke. Stolen torque wrenched his muzzle upward. The diverted gel round ruptured against a metal desk leg, splattering compressed anesthetic compound in a thick pressurized smear just above the boy's head. Searing feedback bit behind her eyes like sheared copper. She slammed a blind diagnostic loop into the optical tracker, freezing the descending blast shutters four feet off the concrete.

Tremors shook Nathan's ribs. Harsh fluorescent glare and deafening mechanical whine crashed back into his senses, metabolic heat rolling off the nape of his neck from the neural tug-of-war. Overhead, the stalled barrels stuttered uselessly in an aborted sweep. Vaulting the pallet rack, he closed the distance across shattered concrete before the worker could scramble free. One measured strike with a cushioned synthetic palm caught the boy's jaw, dropping him limp onto the linoleum without snapping bone. Chest heaving against lungs raw with dust, he wiped pale hydraulic smear from his forearm seam and eyed the jammed blast gate.

*Shutter interlocks are pinned, and the optical loop won't hold past a manual reset,* Veronica transmitted, carrier wave frayed thin as she locked the perimeter cameras. She tagged container Alpha-Seven forty meters ahead. A translucent cyan route vector painted the cracked floor, steady despite the electrical stutter pulsing through their shared nerves. *You have ninety seconds before dispatch notices the silent analog trip. Move.*

Nathan sprinted down the aisle. His damaged left arm throbbed where frayed synthskin snagged against wet fabric. At Alpha-Seven, he wedged a pry-bar beneath the thermal latches and heaved until the seal hissed open, spilling cold vapor over his boots. Rows of Evo Pharmaceutical neuro-immunosuppressants and gene-stabilization packs glinted inside, throwing condensation across the vascular seams of his wrists.

*Two crates cover our activesoft leases and nanite precursor through spring,* Nathan transmitted, hoisting two insulated containers into his tactical duffel while his heart still hammered against the vagal brake. He zipped the heavy canvas shut over the latches. *We lock those down first and clear the yard before patrol rolls up.*

*Three more go to Fulton,* Veronica countered, highlighting an alternate drop corridor straight across his HUD while static prickled along the Matrix barrier she was still holding shut. *The free clinic has been patching cyber-rejection with expired saline since November.* Her carrier pulse tightened across their shared ribs as she pressed the point home. *Carrying the extra bulk buys us an off-grid surgeon who will never run our biometrics. Right now, our left forearm needs a seam weld we cannot buy uptown.*

*Parnell is going to howl over the short manifest,* Nathan pushed back, his jaw tight as a distant siren wailed thin through the Georgia rain. He dragged a rusted hand trolley out from the racking, eyeing the four-foot clearance beneath the stalled shutter. *He does not run a charity. Neither of us is built to sprint with five boxes.*

*The fixer trades on short margin, and we trade on surviving the year,* Veronica shot back crisp and sharp, shifting spare focus to keep the shutter motor stalled against its thermal breaker. Her cyan trace pulsed once over the torn synthskin on his wrist, grounding the dull throb into manageable telemetry. *Having a beholden clinic beats saving three grand on a fence cut. Stack the cart.*

*Fair,* Nathan agreed. He gripped the trolley handle, skillwires humming tight as the center of gravity shifted. Three extra crates went onto the bed. He muscled the loaded cart under the jammed shutter with two fingers of clearance, hauling all five containers across wet gravel into their battered delivery van. Tail-lights dark, Venn pulled clear of the Fulton spurs seconds before the loop collapsed and yard sirens woke the block.

Forty minutes later, the van backed down a steam-choked alley in the Fulton sprawl. Dr. Caleb Ortiz stepped onto the ramp in a blood-stained apron, tired eyes darting from the pristine Evo seals on the insulated crates to the charred tear across Nathan's sleeve. "Are you out of your minds?" the doctor whispered, shaking fingers hovering above the cold latches while glancing toward the alley mouth. "If Evo asset protection traces these lot numbers to my clinic, they'll burn the whole block to ash."

Nathan stepped back under the alley awning, collar raised against rain. Veronica projected beside him, cyan photonic bleed catching the doctor's steaming breath and the pale trace drying along his torn cuff. "Transponders are wiped, and the rail manifest shows zero loss," he told him, keeping his voice flat and level despite the fatigue weighing down his shoulders. "Nobody is tracing these lots here, Doctor. Use them for the street kids and keep your doors open."

The surgeon gripped the trolley handle. His gaze lingered once more on the split synthskin under the torn cuff before he exhaled a slow plume of condensation. He hauled the cold crates across the threshold into the clinic's dim rear corridor, shielding the corporate stamps with his apron. "Back door is unlatched after two if that hydraulic line starts weeping," he muttered over his shoulder before easing the steel door shut.

At midnight, they met Parnell inside a corner booth of a subterranean dive bar under the roaring concrete arches of the Atlanta interchange. The stout dwarf fixer adjusted gold-rimmed data glasses, squinting down at the two remaining pharmaceutical crates under the table while running an escrow handshake across his commlink. "Evo military stock runs hot enough to blister paint, kid, and you look like you walked through a chop-shop," he grunted, biting the end off a cheap synthetic cigar and docking his thirty-percent cut. "Worse, your haul is three crates shy of the bill. You cutting your own margin to play saint in Fulton?"

As the escrow synced, the fixer tapped his display and frowned at the live PAN telemetry bleeding off their link through the damp cellar air. Across his monitor, two distinct neural signatures overlapped dead center on the graph, weaving Nathan's bruised biological spikes directly through Veronica's Resonant carrier wave. "Look at this scrambled mess," Parnell scoffed, spinning the screen around so the cyan and amber curves pulsed across Nathan's face. "Two whole minds jammed into one skull like a glitchy Venn diagram. Running half-wired down here usually buys you a ditch in the scrap yards."

He studied the glowing overlap on the glass, seeing the exact set intersection Lyra had proven in the dark decades earlier. Inside their circuit, Veronica's carrier wave hummed warm and steady against his pulse, carrying only the faintest metallic echo of the warehouse lock. "Venn works," Nathan said. He slid the certified credstick off the table into his duster pocket.

They climbed the concrete stairwell into cold Atlanta rain under towering suspension tracks. Overhead, freight mag-rails roared through freezing drizzle, throwing amber sparks down onto wet asphalt while corporate holo-billboards flickered against low cloud. Veronica dropped their updated ledger onto his HUD. Forty-two hundred clean nuyen had cleared in escrow, covering activesoft leases, nanite precursor, and a bench flush under their new handle. Pulling the torn collar tight against the wind, Venn stepped out from under the concrete arches and vanished into the city.
