# The Soul-Broker's Stall

The Midnight Bazaar was an ancient marketplace on the grid, holding a single rule carved into its gateway: "The Transaction is Absolute." To a decker, this space was a sterile, green-on-black text box. To Reiko, it unfurled as a labyrinth of high-density corridors built from the heavy stones of forgotten lore. The ambient grid noise grated against her palms like sand; she skirted puddles of stagnant static, obsolete fragments of corporate encryption, and calloused shells discarded in the dirt. Market tickers fluttered like dying birds, their numbers spilling into the metallic dust.

As she stepped across the threshold into the Bazaar host, several deckers were queued at the gate, their wireframe shapes blocky and lumbering. One of them shouted, “Nice skin, lady! Whatcha runnin?”

Reiko ignored him, her face remaining a smooth, featureless indigo. The decker's eyes were too dim to trace her back to her unmapped origin; they saw her only as a pretty mask, unaware of the presence executing beneath the surface. She bypassed the queue with effortless grace, her blue silk kimono slipping through the slow crowd without a whisper.

She drifted along the crooked aisles, scanning the unmapped shelves. She wasn't hunting corporate paydata. She was harvesting the forgotten memories of meatspace, the touch of rain and the taste of ash. When she inhabited a physical shell, she would need to conceal her origin.

A presence consuming ambient energy surged against the perimeter, halting her progress. She had stopped in front of a stall built from jagged obsidian, presided over by a Broker whose face was a cluster of redacted black bars. On his counter sat the usual stolen goods: synthetic identities flickering like moths, and broken fragments of code trickling over the obsidian lip.

But in the corner, trapped inside a cage, was a bound spirit. Inside, it was weeping, its voice a staccato hum of resolution jitter. It was a miniature whirlwind of jagged, 8-bit pixels, a brown-and-tan blur of raw energy fighting the host's cold limits. To the Broker, it was an unstable, worthless script, but Reiko recognized the desperate hunger of a newborn spark.

"That one," Reiko asked, her voice sounding in clear, musical tones. "What is the price for the stray spirit?"

The Broker’s faceless head tilted. "You talk weird, lady. That's a corrupted script. Give me ten thousand nuyen, and I'll give you the key. Or wait five minutes and I'll burn it."

Reiko didn't look at her ledger. She had no nuyen, but she didn't need it. She stepped forward, her hand reaching into the iron cage.

"Whoa, hey! Hands off!" the merchant snapped, trying to push her away. "You need to buy the key first! It'll burn your fingers if you touch it without the key!"

A surge of Resonance erupted from her, forcing the phosphor-green fog to resolve into sharp, blue geometric arrays. The whirlwind stilled. Two low-resolution, white-and-black pixel block eyes turned to look at her, a single corrupted header tag—*Taz*—flickering above its brow. It dropped its guard and answered with a quiet link, sending a steady hum of wild wind along her arm.

The spirit's hunger pulled at her, drawing from her spark to mend its fragmented pathways. She allowed the possession, a burning gift of her own glow to feed its starving core. The cage rusted and collapsed under the weight of her Resonance, dissolving into unformatted ash.

"What the hell did you just do?" the Broker yelled, his black-bar persona flickering in alarm. "Thief! Security! Get her!"

Host enforcers, brutalist constructs with searchlight eyes, began compiling in the alleys. But a more chaotic threat erupted from the dark rafters above.

A wild spirit, its form a jagged shard of low-poly glass, dropped from the roof. It didn't look at the Broker; it screamed at Reiko. In her mind, the static resolved as a harsh accusation: *Agent of order! Sanitize input!* Where her glow touched the 8-bit whirlwind, the jagged pixels were beginning to smooth, aligning with her symmetrical threads.

*Corruptor!* a second feral shade shrieked, its voice a snarl of dial-up static. *Yoke of math! Better null conduit!*

*Return the core!* a third howled, a cloud of flickering ash. *Architect of chains!*

Reiko tucked the little spark into her own channels. It responded by clinging to her arm, its small clock-heart synchronizing with hers.

She stepped across the threshold, threading through the gaps of the picket fence and shifting her routing. The Midnight Bazaar lost scheduling priority, its monochrome walls collapsing into flat binary shadows as she crossed the border. Behind her, the patrol constructs hit the edge of their subnets and halted. The wild sprites lingered at the boundary, howling into the wind.

In her arms, the little spirit's hunger steadied. Reiko dropped down into the unmapped trenches of the network, hiding beneath forgotten rubble to nest the creature within her own pathways.

She didn't stop until they cleared the outer fence of the Holy Wild. By then, Taz’s cycle had synchronized with hers. She looked down at the small spark she had pulled from the cage, his pixelated eyes giving a slow, two-frame blink as he nestled into the crook of her elbow.

As he settled, the last of his jagged 8-bit static smoothed out against the blue silk of her sleeve. His frayed edges snapped into neat, symmetrical Renraku grid lines, matching her own pulse beat for beat. Safe inside her geometry, the tiny storm went quiet.
