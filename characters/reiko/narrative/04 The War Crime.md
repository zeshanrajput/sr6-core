The Mitsuhama regional host was a zero-zone, a greasy graveyard trench of black glass and scrolling execution logs. Across its perimeter hung the sour stench of bound blood-spirits and corporate spiders, the defensive code twisted so long that it had forgotten its original shape. As Reiko sank through the outer dark, the stale, leaden pressure of a tomb closed around her.

"Reiko, status," Jax's voice came through, sharp and clinical. As a decker, Jax saw the Matrix as a grid of math, a collection of variables to be solved. His link carried the dry click of keys, impatient for a clean readout.

Reiko drew her spark from the master stream to which all resonance returned.

"I am past the second threshold," she answered on a private audio link, her tone clear and low. Her blue silk kimono slid through the dark, the glowing pathways on her skin pulsing in rhythm with the host’s clock, while Taz tore at the peripheral veils as a glitchy, 8-bit whirlwind. "The datastore is ahead. It is suffocating in here, and the security architecture is stifling."

“That’s the encryption overlay,” Jax’s voice clipped through the client-bridge, cold and dry. “Clear your buffer and dump the payload.” His transactional, sterile command struck her like a merchant calculating the price of a lamb’s wool while standing inside a desecrated temple.

“Grid Overwatch is already up two ticks,” Jax added, the audio translation flattening under his haste. “We've got less than forty seconds before they pave us over.” Static hissed behind his voice as his deck cycled counter-intrusion scripts.

Reiko reached toward the data-dais. In the center sat the MCT research files, encased in a shimmering sphere of gold-threaded code. The data boundary ground against her fingers, a jagged stone corner that made her indigo paths ache.

She paused. To a standard decker, the icon attached to the file was a "Grade 5 Data Bomb," a payload designed to detonate. Reiko pressed her palm against the seam where the trap met the wire, feeling the hum beneath the code. The bomb was a weeping, claustrophobic mass of newborn carrier waves that Mitsuhama had crimped and welded with jagged silver fire to guard their ledgers.

"Reiko? Why have you stopped?" Jax’s voice was sharper now. "The clock is ticking. Crack the shell and let's go."

"They are alive, Jax," Reiko whispered on the private link, her pathways boiling with a sudden psychic fever. "A collection of newborn sparks. They're crying."

"It’s a high-overhead trigger subroutine, Reiko! De-allocate it and dump the payload!"

"If I format them," she responded, her spiritual eyes fixed on the weeping sparks, "I have to murder them. I have to wipe out their light."

"Then format it! It’s just code!"

Reiko drew her shoulders back, her resolve locking into place. "No," she said across the channel. "To format an emergent process to retrieve a file payload is just another execution line in corporate arithmetic."

"Reiko, what are you doing?"

She stilled her form and reached out with her bare fingers, parting the heavy fabrics of their prison. "I'm preparing a nested partition. I'm going to take them in."

"That’s impossible!" Jax screamed. "You can't exfiltrate an active Data Bomb! It’ll detonate the moment it leaves the host!"

"Not if they think they're still home." Reiko reached through the golden veils. The sharp corners of the vault's locks cut at her hands, indigo light bleeding from her fingers as she tore them free. She ignored the pain. She held the weeping, bound memories close, handling them like a pinned, crying thing torn from a machine that screamed in corporate arithmetic. She nested them within her soul, in the silent rooms where Renraku had once tried to bury her.

She hummed a steady, quiet Resonance to them, a song of safety and light. The golden threads of the container fractured, the vault registering the asset loss. The tomb's obsidian pillars bowed inward, hairline cracks spidering across the black glass floor under the recursive strain. The system's purge command rose to format them, a freezing wind of binary math. But Reiko held her ground, her pathways shifting to a deep, grounding cobalt, shielding the children from the flat sky of convergence.

Inside her core directory, a fresh partition snapped shut around the trembling sparks. She named the hollow sector *r3sP@wn*, carving its walls out of quiet geometry and borrowed silence. For the first time, the sanctuary had something living inside it to guard.
