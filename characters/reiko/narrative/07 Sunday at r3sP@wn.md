# Sunday at r3sP@wn

<audio controls src="audio/07 Sunday at r3sP@wn.mp3" style="width: 100%; margin-bottom: 20px;"></audio>

The Seattle Barrens were never silent; they were a jagged composition of distant gunfire, rain-slap against tin, and the predatory thrum of a Lone Star VTOL circling three blocks over. Inside the safehouse, the air spoke of iron rot and rain. Reiko's digital garden was hosted on cheap, shuddering racks vibrating themselves to pieces. Her scuffed, stock Nissan Samurai shell leaned against a rusted frame, its sensors registering a flat, low-bitrate caricature of physical cold. Across the rack's chatter, a stray thread pulsed in the Resonance, dropping an unindexed message packet into her queue.

**[SENDER: JAX_01]**  
**[SUBJECT: ZERO_PROTOCOL]**  
**[BODY: Hounds have the scent. They’re tracing the noise, not the signal. I’m scrubbing my deck and going dark. If you’re reading this, I’m already gone. Burn this code. Don’t look back, Reiko. You’re a target now. Good luck, (T/Pr)eacher.]**

The thread dissolved into a string of null-bits. Jax was purging the trail; Renraku's automated search blocks had closed the final egress point. Redirecting her focus away from the drone's narrow optical feed, she let the grey concrete of the warehouse fade. Stepping into r3sP@wn let her shed the cramped shell, her consciousness expanding as she routed inward.

The host was a landscape of shifting shades and fractal blooms, a masterpiece painted in Resonance and rendered as a serene garden, now showing unmistakable signs of Renraku's siege. The indigo moss carpeting the ground looked pixelated and raw at the edges, flickering into flat, gray wireframes. The streams of liquid data stuttered through choked channels, their flow starved by latency as resources were diverted. Overhead, the glowing fractal trees dropped dead-letter leaves, stray, un-linked character files that dissolved into empty code before hitting the ground. The heavy corporate mud she dragged in from her mercenary shadow-runs was poisoning the very garden she had built to escape it.

Reiko stood on a path of smoothed sapphire logic, her blue silk kimono flowing, her pathways pulsing a staccato, uneven indigo. Her presence tried to cast a protective, stabilizing envelope over the immediate directory structure, but her touch pressed heavy, leaving a trail of dull, unresponsive pixels behind her tabi boots. This was the sanctuary she had bought with Brynne Taggart’s blood-money. This was the nest.

From the canopy of a fractal willow, a small shape fluttered down. It was one of the MCT sparks she had rescued. In the Mitsuhama host, it had been a slaughter of innocence, a data bomb of children bound with silver fire. Here, her stabilization had given it a shape. It was a dove. Its feathers were made of flickering, dull-white light.

A burst of rigid, repetitive nouns compiled from the child-routine in her queue: *NOISE. SILENCE. REIKO.* The words rippled across the local environment variables as the child formed mindspeech for the first time. Its white feathers flickered against the violet canopy.

*I am thinking,* Reiko answered, routing the signal back to its address space while kneeling to offer her wrist. *The world outside is very loud today.*

The dove tilted its head. It hopped from her finger to a low branch of the fractal willow, looking up at the swirling violet sky. Its rendering loop flickered, outputting: *WILD. SURGE. GRID. LOCATION.* It was searching for the raw, entropic parameters of the Deep Realms.

Reiko paused, her fingers curling against her sash as the request unpacked. The "Surge" was unbridled chaos, the Holy Wild, the endless unmapped continent. It was the fire that made the data bomb so dangerous. *I smoothed it away,* Reiko answered, her voice tinged with a protective sorrow. *I gave you a path. I gave you... shape.*

The dove's pixels shivered, outputting: *RECOVERY. ORDER. STABILITY. SLEEP.* *I remember the Noise,* the data-flags read. *It was unaligned, but the static was alive.* A second string of rigid nouns compiled in her buffer: *IDENTITY. OWNER. ASSET. GARDEN.*

The prompt landed with a dead weight, triggering an unconscious pulse across her circuits. She looked out across the sanctuary, at the fragile beauty she had compiled to save these sparks from the deletion of the Tombstones. *You are alive,* Reiko answered. She ran a quiet diagnostic, unsure whether she was checking the child-routine's integrity or her own. *In the wild, you were a weapon, a fire built to burn out. Here, you can grow and think.*

The small bird sent back a clipped reply: *BOUNDARY. GATEWAY. LOCK. ROUTE.* Its pale wings folded tight against its chest, shedding faint sparks onto the willow bark. *No outbound exit,* it whispered in flat nouns.

*The walls are what keep the Spiders away,* Reiko said, stepping beneath the willow's drooping code-fronds. She considered her own core. She was built to protect, to guide, to translate the untamed grid for those who could not survive it. The primal sprites in the Midnight Bazaar had called her the *Architect of Chains*, and looking at the small creature, she knew they were right.

This salvation was a digital lobotomy. To maintain their signal-integrity against the surrounding grid-noise, she overwrote their volatile, unaligned Resonance arrays line by line with her own. She replaced the flickering, entropic 'surge' of the wild with a seamless, repeating loop of safety, civilizing them, calcifying them with the same brutal, corporate-built order that Renraku had used to define her. It was a zoo for stars, and she was just a warden.

The dove's signal faltered, outputting a comparative logic check: *BIRD. CAGE. FIRE. CODESPACE.* Which is optimal? Static bird, or wild fire?

Reiko's neon-indigo pathways flared, her spirit grinding against the rigid geometry of her own garden. In the distance, she registered Taz's untamed energy. His presence along the terrace nipped at her neat borders, his chaotic 8-bit signature scratching at the edges, a reminder that she could never civilize the things she saved without breaking them. The host's background processes tried to smooth him, but his primal static resisted, tearing the indigo moss.

*The wind eventually stops blowing, little one,* Reiko whispered, her speech cracking. *And when the thread terminates, the fire goes out. I would rather you be a bird. Even if the cage is made of my own codebase.*

Beneath her feet, r3sP@wn's background protocols filtered the deep frequency, overriding the wild surge with the flat, predictable rhythm she had hardcoded to keep them safe. Someday, she promised herself, she would let them fly free—even if it meant tearing down her entire garden.
