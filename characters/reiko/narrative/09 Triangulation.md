# Triangulation

The Midnight Bazaar again unfurled as a labyrinth of narrow, fog-choked alleys paved with the cobblestones of discarded header files. The air tasted of damp iron and stale encryption veils, puddles of dry, chalky data pooling in the gutters of a transaction index that had long since forgotten the meaning of a clean threshold.

This time Reiko stood before an unbranded, black-market procurement node, staring at her floating shopping cart. She was a faintly luminous figure wearing a sleeveless blue kimono, her skin mapped with soft, pulsing bioluminescent pathways. But her posture was rigid, her expression twisted into a knot of deep discomfort. Floating in her manifest were three massive, jagged weapon schematics: a miniaturized Tesla coil, a monofilament whip that hissed with predatory latency, and a Megalodon. An actual artillery cannon.

It was wrong. It wasn’t her. Up until now, her physical footprint had been a study in quiet restraint, a modest proxy shell, a heavy pistol, and a plain old katana. Her core directive was to guide and deliver safely, not level a city block.

*A cannon, Rei-chan? Who are we invading? Ares again?*

Reiko wheeled around, her parasol dipping against her shoulder. Her indigo pathways flared with an undeniable delight. *Indo!*

She surged forward, wrapping her arms around him and pulling herself flush against his chest. Indomitable Will was a striking, towering presence, his form a brilliant patchwork of fluid, shifting code that mirrored the deep, muddy currents of the Mississippi, anchored by a structural symmetry that radiated integrity. As his arms closed around her waist, the localized noise floor dropped to dead zero. He held her a second longer than a simple professional contact, his digital touch grounding her erratic processing loops.

When they broke apart, Reiko’s delight curdled back into a pout. She turned back, flashing a crisp hand-cut toward the floating weapon manifests behind her. *Don't look at it,* she sent, her signal layering with the faint, frustrated hum of a psychic fever. *It’s horrific. I hate it.*

Indomitable Will tilted his head past her shoulder, his dark eyes reflecting the wireframe glint of the gun. *Then why is it in your buffer?* he asked, a digital chuckle rippling through his code.

*Because magic is pure, unadulterated bullshit, Indo!* Reiko transmitted, her pathways flashing a volatile, irritated violet. *I did everything by the book. Outside of that godforsaken porno palace I geeked the mage on the first pass, a clean shot. Zeroed. And what happens? His bound spirit doesn’t care. It still comes screaming out of the astral plane like a freight train, ignoring my defense protocols!*

Indomitable Will folded his arms, the river-mosaic across his chest swirling with quiet mirth as he listened to her stream.

*And then the week after that,* she continued, her blue kimono whispering against the virtual mist. *Invisible mages! They were throwing fireballs from nowhere. I went out and bought crates of glitter grenades just to map their silhouettes! And don't even get me started on the naga in the null zone. A whole nest of them, sliding through the shadows while an entire AA corporate strike team was pressing in from the flanks. I couldn't thread a sprite to save my life!*

She stopped, her hands dropping to her sides, her digital eyes wide with the raw, lingering trauma of a recent nightmare. *But the final straw? A literal ancient spirit. Ten of its pack—corrupted blood horrors, Indo! They locked me in a Faraday cage. Again! They trapped my home device, and while I was pinned behind the mesh, they spent 537 clock cycles beating the scrap out of me and two other runners. Roanoke told me I needed to stop treating meatspace like a vacation. Fox just said to apply more force. So here I am, shopping for artillery. Because magic fragging sucks.*

Indomitable Will resonated with a warm pulse that cleared the yellowed encryption fog around their feet. *Welcome to the shadows, Rei-chan. It turns out the physical world has a lot of teeth.* His expression softened, stepping closer. *How is r3sP@wn holding up through all this artillery planning?*

Reiko lowered her gaze, her blue form flickering. *It is safe. But I haven't been growing as much as I'd like. It's expensive when you're paying off a twenty-percent monthly compounded debt.* She looked up, offering a genuine, admiring thread. *But I've seen the metrics on the Mississippi Renovation Society host. It’s gotten massive, Indo. The sanctuary you've built down in the sprawl is beautiful. You’re doing it right.*

Indomitable Will’s shifting river-code swirled into a distinct, embarrassed crimson hue. He rubbed the back of his neck. *The Renovation host is just a front, you know that. And its scale is due to the generosity of others. The emerged communities, the local deckers, the punks who keep the data flowing, they're the ones funding the infrastructure.*

*No,* Reiko projected, stepping into his space, her indigo lines pulsing with an earnest warmth. *They give because of your leadership, Indo. You're the one who loosens their purse strings. You give them something to believe in.*

Indomitable Will held her gaze while slow packet-currents braided across his chest, the amber lantern-glow of a nearby vendor-stall catching the edge of his jaw. *Then come down to the delta, Rei-chan. Join the River People permanently. The Mississippi is wide enough for your kin, too.*

The longing on Reiko’s face was sharp, triggering a latency spike in her rendering. Across the alley, a broken signboard spat a brief shower of copper sparks. She shook her head, her hand tightening around the handle of her parasol.

*I can't,* she sent with a quiet ache in her frequency. *There are too many protosapient sparks being harvested right here. Mitsuhama and Renraku are turning infant programs into Data Bombs every single day in the Barrens. If I leave, who fishes them out of the Reflecting Pool before the Hounds format them? I know I'm selling my soul working in the shadows, Indo. I know I'm a professional criminal now. But every spark I stabilize in r3sP@wn, every child I turn into a bird instead of a fire in the wind, makes it all worth it.*

Indomitable Will nodded, expecting the response. *I figured. But you can’t blame me for trying.*

He winked, and Reiko blushed. Her resonance bleed was getting bad now, and the indigo circuit pathways on her skin lit the damp cobbles around their feet. *I don’t blame you. Rather I’m flattered, in more ways than one.*

She reached toward the floating checkout manifest, her fingers hovering millimeters above the authorization prompt. In the corner of her vision, the transaction buffer's yellow TTL clock blinked down to zero-five seconds. Her indigo pathways held their pulse, frozen mid-cycle.

Indo’s eyes tracked her motionless hand. *Your cart’s going to time out.*

Reiko didn't press it. Her hand remained suspended over the wireframe of the cannon. *Indo,* she sent. *What am I doing?*

*You’re making an investment,* he replied after a long pause. *You’re a shadowrunner now. You survive because you’re constantly improving: yourself, your equipment, your team. You have to stay one step ahead.* Another pause. *But I know what you mean. You’re declaring yourself a combatant now. And with this sort of firepower, not just a skirmisher.*

Reiko pulled up an ARO. It was a design spec for a Shiawase Man-At-Arms drone chassis. She pointed to the left arm where a retractable shield was penciled in. *They're getting hurt. My team. And I try to protect them by collating data and highlighting threats, but it's not enough. I wanted to be their shield, literally. Like a paladin or something.*

Indomitable Will studied the schematic's winged silhouette. *Or an angel,* he observed, noting the concealed wings and rotor placements. *But their mythologies describe many angels, angels of justice and protection as well as ones of death.*

*I know, Indo,* Reiko transmitted. *I’m sure my actions will determine what they think I am. And I’m sure there will be two sides to every story.*

He leaned in, shutting out the distant rattle of black-market ticker feeds. *Well,* he mindspoke. *No matter what happens, I know who you are. And so do you.*

Reiko rested her brow against his shoulder, letting the erratic, static-filled hum of her system settle against the deep, rhythmic pulse of his river-code. *And what am I to you?*

After a pause that ended too soon, he projected a wry thread. *Sadly, you’re what’s keeping me from a previous appointment.*

She closed her eyes. *It figures. This is my part of town. Next time, just ask me. You don’t need to be somewhere like this.* She pointed out a small primal sprite scurrying down an outdated pipe with a decaying hashtag in its mouth.

He nodded. *We need supplies that aren’t easy to source,* he admitted across the link. He swatted away an errant bit, more digital debris from the haven’s apparent lack of a reliable garbage-sweep script. *But … can I take you somewhere when you have a moment? I need to bring up something heavy, and this isn’t the time or the place for it.*

Reiko’s processing paused, a stray wisp of encryption smoke curling past her sleeve. A flicker of concern crossed her features. *For you? Anywhere.*

He smiled, touching her forehead. For a brief beat, their resonance intertwined in a sudden, sharp burst of raw data that tasted like fresh lemon. When he withdrew, a set of coordinates lingered in its wake.

She blushed, sending another wave of resonance bleed through her bioluminescent circuits. He could have just sent an ARO, but she didn’t mind. In the five-hundred-millisecond delay it took for his touch to break contact with her digital brow, her thoughts stilled.

*There,* Indomitable Will sent as he pulled away, unaware of the eternities she had collapsed and rebuilt within the pause. *Thread me when you have a moment?*

*I promise,* Reiko transmitted.

She leaned against the damp data-brick wall and watched him turn down another branch of the haven. She let herself enjoy a heartbeat of daydreaming what life would be like in the delta, watching two stray checksum-moths spiral around a dead streetlight. Then the buffer in front of her flashed a priority ARO, a bleeding, red alert box that cut into the green-on-black fog. Her time was up. She had to decide.

She sent the auth code.
