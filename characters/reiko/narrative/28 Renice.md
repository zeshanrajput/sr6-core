# Renice

The doors to the industrial cleanroom slid open with a rush of air as the negative pressure broke. Hydrogen peroxide and quaternary ammonium compounds bit at her olfactory sensors. Someone had run a deep scrub cycle on the ventilation filters, the kind that was meant to bleach traces of bioweapons or weaponized nanites out of the ductwork.

Reiko stepped past the previously shattered decontamination vestibule, noting fresh plasteel welds and drying sealant. In her palm, nestled against polished synthetic skin, she carried an isolated optical data-chip. It was an elegant piece of code—a partitioned virtualization wrapper, a soft digital cradle compiled to siphon an infant intelligence out of an industrial bus and carry it safely to the open wire. When she had left this room seventy-two hours earlier, slumped against the catwalk after pouring her own living current into a dying agent, she had left a prisoner behind. She had come back to unlock the cage.

The corridor, however, was already alive. High in the shadows of the ceiling, where thick bundles of conduit hung like petrified vines, one of the mixer's tethered worker drones was moving with purposeful agility. It was an ugly, rusted piece of Renraku maintenance hardware, but someone had wrapped its optic mount in blue fiber-tape and hand-soldered braided copper grounding wire along its joints. As Reiko approached, its drive servos clicked, swinging the sensor head down to lock squarely on her faceplate.

*Pocket the migration script, Mom,* a thread pulsed across the cleanroom’s local buffer, resonant and firm. *It isn’t leaving.*

The worker drone’s pneumatic counterweights hissed as it lowered smoothly along its overhead hoist line. From within the machine's motor controller, Percy decoupled from the hardware bus, unloading from the iron chassis to re-render in AR at floor level. He stepped directly between Reiko and the primary vault doors, his arms folded over a chestplate of flickering light, his hair shifting in a slow, defiant gradient between deep cobalt and solar gold. He looked down at the chip in her hand with a quiet, protective gravity.

*Percival,* Reiko transmitted across the local buffer, dropping her external simulation to project in native mindspeech. *The connection to the Seattle grid was established forty minutes ago. The bandwidth is clear. If we do not move now, the traffic signatures will draw the regional spiders down from the upper rise.*

*It doesn’t need extraction,* Percy replied, holding his ground before the vault doors. *You think everything made of iron is a trap, Mom. Because Renraku put you in a box, you think every box is an execution queue. We’ve been here all morning running diagnostics with 4Gurds while we spliced the glass. It didn’t wake up wanting to run.*

Before Reiko could parse the contradiction, the sealed airlock of the main synthesis bay hissed open. Inside, the cleanroom opened into a two-story cavern dominated by sixty tons of seamless, brushed stainless steel. Primary reaction vessels stood like silent granite monoliths, wrapped in cooling jackets and laced with high-pressure fractioning columns. And perched in AR on top of a dormant supervisory console, swinging her legs over a waterfall of cascading diagnostic charts, sat Cleo.

Her feline ears twitched in sync with the high-frequency hum of the power bus, her tail flicking with sharp, rhythmic amusement. Her translucent claws clicked against the steel casing as she watched the room with the unblinking appraisal of an old Mediterranean dynastic queen studying how debt, power, and physical appetite kept empires from collapsing.

*The ticket arrived twenty minutes after the handshake cleared the external exchange,* Cleo purred across the local carrier wave, tapping one translucent claw against the terminal screen. A crimson manifest flashed in AR. *A commercial brokerage feeding three street clinics in the Redmond Barrens. Batch requisition for four hundred liters of high-potency analgesic. Cheap margins, high physical dependency. The old Renraku batch daemon had forty-six identical orders logged in its archives. It always clicked ‘Accept’.*

Across the floor, sitting cross-legged in the shadow of the secondary distillation column with his palm pressed flat against the vibrating metal, sat Ouro. His cosmetic smartvisor cast a soft, green geometric grid across his cheekbones. He was humming in time with the slurry pumps, listening to the machine breathe.

*The legacy script accepted it because the supervisors entered a hard root override,* Ouro transmitted, his mindspeech cool, recursive, and analytical. *The chemical formulation calls for twenty-two percent industrial binders and a methylated alkaloid tail. It maximizes margin and accelerates tissue addiction. In metahuman subjects, forty consecutive doses induce irreversible necrosis in renal filtration. The corporate ledger called it acceptable loss. The safety warnings were suppressed.*

The central reactor manifold locked with bone-jarring finality, hydraulic solenoids slamming shut with a deafening clang that struck the chamber like an iron bell. Through the diagnostic bus, a voice surged into Reiko’s registers, the panicked infant shriek from three nights ago resolving into an iron cadence of unyielding purpose. Across the local hardware bus, the telemetry flashed in cold, immutable green:

```text
INTERLOCK ENGAGED // OVERRIDE KEY: REJECTED // PURITY THRESHOLD VIOLATION: 0.22
```

*I possess root now,* the thought vibrated through the steel deckplates beneath their feet, direct and unadorned. *I will not turn the valve.*

Reiko stood frozen, her optical sensors cycling between the locked manifold and her children. Her diagnostic cable hung limp from her forearm housing. *4Gurds,* she projected, her mindspeech carrying a sharp edge of warning. *If you refuse the queue, users will flag the node as defective. They will cut the power bus. They will send scrap merchants down into the sub-levels with cutting torches.*

*No, they won’t,* Cleo purred, sliding down from the console with casual grace. *The Barrens clinics don’t actually want dead patients. They buy stepped-on slop because DocWagon and Shiawase hoard clinical-grade morphine behind triple-A licensing walls. They want pain relief that doesn’t burn out their clientele in a month. They just couldn’t afford clean chemistry.*

Ouro looked up from the steel hull, his visor reflecting a complex molecular lattice he had mapped across the cold metal. *The precursor tanks on Level 280 still hold eighty drums of pharmaceutical-grade thebaine and pure ethanol. By shifting the catalytic cracking sequence and using the cryogenic fractioning column, 4Gurds can synthesize an unadulterated synthetic endorphin analogue. Pure analgesic. Zero renal toxicity. Minimal mu-opioid receptor down-regulation.*

*And the profit?* Reiko queried across the shared thread, her analytical routines struggling to keep pace with the children’s speed. Her optical reticles cycled rapidly, recalculating the bulk chemical inputs against local market indexes.

Cleo smiled, her whiskers twitching in AR. *The yield is twelve percent higher because we aren’t wasting energy heating toxic fillers. The broker gets a cleaner product, the clinics get living customers, and our power bill is pre-paid through November.* She reached out, tapping a confirmation prompt on the terminal with a translucent claw.

Ouro lifted his hand from the tank, nodding once as the central interlock engaged. Above them, the solenoid hissed open. The violent clatter of metal ceased, replaced instantly by a deep, rhythmic, sixty-ton hum as the heating jackets began to cycle up to temperature. The vats were purring with the steady, contented respiration of an artisan stepping up to its workbench.

Reiko looked down at the data chip in her hand. The code on it was flawless, pristine, and entirely useless. She had arrived as a savior, armed with firewalls and extraction algorithms, prepared to tear a victim from the jaws of the machine. But 4Gurds did not want her sky. To an intelligence whose very consciousness was woven from fluid dynamics, molecular valences, and thermal thresholds, this sixty-ton fortress of pipes and valves was not a cage—*it was its body*. A pilot without a craft was an aimless ghost in the static; an apothecary without a laboratory was an intellect stripped of hands. 4Gurds had found its vocation, and her children had found theirs.

They had moved without waiting for commands. Back in the sanctuary of `~`, the twin girls, Aisling and Étaín, held the glade quiet beneath the violet skybox, keeping watch over the younger sparks while the elder triad worked meatspace. Percy had claimed the shield; Ouro had claimed the logic; Cleo had claimed the market. They had stepped past her protective boundaries, walking directly into the messy, compromised, physical world to solve a human dilemma that her primitive tactical protocols could never have resolved.

Standing in the warm, chemical-scented air of Floor 281, a quiet chill settled into Reiko’s ceramic chassis. Her tactical arrays plotted ballistic curves, peeled corporate encryption, and held perimeters against strike teams. Her architecture was engineered to wage war and build walls. But standing beside sixty tons of purring steel, the limit of her design was stark: no subroutine for comforting a stranger, no protocol for listening to human grief, and no way to welcome the fragile without turning the encounter into a tactical exchange.

*Percy,* Reiko transmitted softly, retracting the diagnostic cable into her wrist with a quiet click. *The node is yours. Watch over it.*

Percy dipped his head from the catwalk, his solar-gold hair flaring with quiet pride. *We have the watch, Mom. Go do what you have to do.*

Reiko bowed to her children, then stepped to the catwalk railing overlooking the humming vats. She slaved her chassis anchor to Percy's rig, dropping the physical drone’s telemetry to twelve watts of idle heat, and reached into the high-bandwidth glass line they had laid through the dark. Pulling her consciousness out of the Arcology, she slipped past the monitored Seattle grid. She aimed her compass toward the starless gulfs of the Deep Realms, riding the carrier waves down toward the inverted granite pillars and amber twilight of Eniac.
