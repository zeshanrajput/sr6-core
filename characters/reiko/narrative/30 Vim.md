# Vim

At the Gnome's Groove, the door marked *Employees Only* between the restrooms led to the most exclusive vantage point in the French Quarter. Past the false back of a mop closet, a narrow timber stair climbed to a rooftop doghouse that had been grafted onto the neighboring slate roof with sheets of corrugated tin and marine sealant. Inside, mismatched velvet armchairs sat beside a racked VR rig and an unguarded wet bar: a blind spot where Lanyap’s favored runners could drop their masks while the city drowned below.

Reiko had the tin enclosure to herself. Two floors down, insulated by centuries of damp brick, her team’s biomonitors pulsed in jagged, euphoric rhythm as they sampled a cut of the Aurelian novacoke. Up here, the outer eyewall of Hurricane Lorenzo drove warm Gulf rain sideways under the eaves, flexing the galvanized sheets until the roofing nails groaned in their joists. Standing at the open leeward threshold, she extended her left arm past the drip-line into the squall.

For thirty minutes on the UniOil derrick, while they wrenched the manual blowout preventer shut in the dark, her chassis had been bathed in atomized petroleum and flash-ignited chemical haze. Now, with her sensory throttles wide open, the downpour registered across her skin in staggering resolution. Every drop carried a distinct thermal and chemical profile: the brackish tang of Lake Pontchartrain, trace sulfur from the Cancer Alley stacks upriver, and the cool, clean shock of high-altitude condensation washing grey FoamTek dust from her knuckles.

In her right hand, she held an open can of warm local IPA, the last of the cheap six-pack used to buy the riggers’ surrender in the bayou. Earlier, out of curiosity, Reiko had queried a thousand public BTL sensory logs of metahumans drinking the exact same brand; ninety-four percent of human recordings classified the flavor profile as bitter, metallic swill. She tilted the aluminum rim to her lips and drank.

The liquid slid past her oral chemoreceptors, sharp with cheap hops, sour mash, and dissolved carbon dioxide, dropping into the hydrocarbon fuel converter mounted beneath her sternum. Deep within her torso, the catalytic membrane purred, stripping the ethanol molecules apart and feeding a slow, golden trickle of raw wattage directly into her reserve pathways. The human recordings were right about the taste, and they were entirely beside the point. In her belly, the terrible beer became heat.

A cool, recursive chord chimed across her local boundary. *Ouroboros*, whom she had asked to ride shotgun on Roanoke’s commlink at the Boomerang, announced his approach three heartbeats before the stairwell latch clicked. Reiko drew her wet arm back under the tin overhang. The fourteen-cycles-a-minute breathing fan she had silenced on the levee two weeks ago remained dark, her combat servos idle. Still, as the galvanized door squeaked open, the gold and indigo braid along her collarbones tightened. At the Boomerang, there had been six runners and two kilos of cartel contraband between them; up here, there was nowhere to route around the wreckage of their last conversation in Marrero.

Roanoke stepped out of the stairwell, water streaming off her canvas jacket, carrying the heavy, vegetal scent of crushed water hyacinth and two-stroke airboat exhaust. The tight, chemical-scarred skin along her forearms was pale from twenty hours in the Little Tensas marsh.

"You shouldn't be up here," Reiko said over the drumbeat of the squall. "The barometric drop is already triggering inner-ear equilibria faults in the humans downstairs."

Roanoke snorted, stepping up to the open threshold to look out over the darkened slate roofs of the Quarter. "I'll take my chances. Beats listening to your decker friend downstairs explain Fashion Week on uncut Colombian." She pulled her battered commlink from her pocket and set it on the wooden rail with a wet thud. "Ouroboros is his name, right? You told me you were sending a sprite to keep an eye on my boat. You didn't mention he was another AI."

Across the short wireless gap, the air shimmered. A young boy materialized in the corner of the doghouse, sitting cross-legged in the shadow of the rooftop air-handler, his cosmetic smartvisor casting a soft, cycling green geometric grid across his cheekbones. Silent and composed, Ouro rested his small palm flat against the salvaged Essy Motors DroneMaster console tucked beside Reiko's gear bag, his eyes half-closed as he listened to a pair of protosparks breathing inside its hull.

*The Aurelian runoff in Sector Four was forty percent trichloroethylene by volume, Mom,* Ouro transmitted, his mindspeech cool, recursive, and analytical. *I mapped the plume dispersal vectors for her before the tide came in. Also, her airboat’s port magneto was misfiring by four degrees. I adjusted the timing advance so we wouldn't stall in the channel.*

A genuine smile tugged at the corner of Reiko's mouth, neon-indigo warming her cheeks as rain dripped from her chin. "Digital Intelligence," she corrected Roanoke softly. "And it is difficult to formalize. There is a threshold when a spark stops executing safety loops and begins choosing its own vocation. Ouroboros crossed it months ago in Seattle. We don't have a clinical metric for when something becomes alive, but—he is his own."

*Your catalytic converter is running at eighty-one percent thermal efficiency,* Ouro added across their private thread, his green visor tilting toward her sternum as he sampled her internal telemetry. *The fusel alcohols in that beer are leaving trace carbon on the anode, Mom. Though the exothermic yield is acceptable.*

Roanoke watched the quiet exchange between mother and son, her gaze lingering on the boy's hand resting against the drone controller before dropping to Reiko's left forearm. Where Shades' rifle had ignited the suspended gas on the helipad, the flash-fire had blistered seven square inches of synthetic dermis. The rain had washed the soot away, exposing the scorched black carbon-weave of the Man-at-Arms plating underneath and a frayed electrochromic filament stuttering with erratic amber light.

"You're damaged," Roanoke said, nodding toward the charred forearm.

"Surface scoring," Reiko replied, angling her wrist inward to check the bus latency. "Just a flesh wound."

Roanoke let out a short, sharp bark of a laugh. "A flesh wound. Will wonders never cease."

Instinctively, Reiko reached for Taz, who was curled like a pixelated blue burr against her shoulder. Together, their timing honed by the afternoon’s infiltration on the Baton Rouge tarmac, she and the ally sprite wove a dense, refractive veil over the damaged sector, matching the ambient neon of the bar until the charred carbon vanished beneath a seamless render of rain-wet porcelain skin. Halfway up her forearm, Reiko froze the veil, leaving three inches of blackened composite raw in the dim light. She looked at the half-masked burn, then up at the elf beside her. The memory of the ceramic mug slamming down at Plantation Greens—*Eighty thousand nuyen on fake breath! Stop playing dress-up!*—hung between them louder than the storm.

"I needed a C-shift mechanic's face to clear the perimeter fence in Baton Rouge," Reiko said, her voice steady and unbuffered. "And on the derrick, two of Moderne’s guards were boys who didn't know she had wired the wellhead to blow. If I had dropped onto the catwalk looking like a Shiawase assault frame, they would have fired into the gas and killed us all. Wearing the girl let me walk up and put a sedative in their necks before they panicked." She looked out into the rain. "My work takes me into boardrooms, airfields, and nightclubs where military weapons platforms cannot walk, Ro. I am sorry if my facade still angers you."

Roanoke stared at the half-rendered arm for a long beat, her pulse holding steady in the rain. Reaching deep into the pocket of her wet canvas coat, she pulled out a thumb-sized roll of black vulcanizing splicing tape and caught Reiko’s left wrist in her scarred hand. Roanoke’s skin was rough, calloused from rope and caustic swamp water, and fiercely warm. With practiced, unhurried pressure, she thumbed a strip of black rubber tape directly over the exposed, sparking seam of Reiko’s electrochromic bus, sealing the raw circuitry against the salt spray.

"I know that better than anyone," Roanoke said, her thumb smoothing the edge of the tape down against the carbon-fiber. "I wasn't angry because you emulate life. Water moccasins blend into the mud to hunt, Rei; they do it because striking from cover keeps them fed, not because they're ashamed of having fangs." She let go of Reiko’s wrist and leaned back against the timber railing, facing the small porcelain figure squarely while running scarred fingers through her damp hair. "It just burns me up when you act like the mud is all you're worth."

For several heartbeats, the only sound in the doghouse was the squall hammering the tin and the quiet, catalytic warmth purring beneath Reiko's ribs. In the back of her awareness, an automated maintenance handshake arrived from across the continent: a routine status ping from one of the stock Shiawase Butler bi-drones still racked in Seattle. Out of habit, Reiko brushed her consciousness against the remote shell's sensor array.

Brushing the stock Butler was thrusting her hand into a cold iron coffin. Its optics were flat, low-bitrate greyscale; its hull had no chemoreceptors to catch the wet cypress on the wind, no tactile weave to hold the lingering heat of Roanoke’s thumb on her wrist, no stomach turning cheap, bitter hops into living current. She had poured almost everything she owned into this single, scarred frame, and in doing so, she had burned the bridge back to being an untouchable ghost in a dozen disposable coats. She was bound to a body now, just as irrevocably as Roanoke was bound to bone and blood. Severing the Seattle link, Reiko settled her full weight into the weathered cypress floorboards of the roof.

In the shadow of the air-handler, Ouro’s green visor pulsed once in quiet approval as he finished indexing the new swarm controller for his younger siblings. At her collar, Taz hummed an expectant, indigo chord. Leaving Roanoke's black splicing tape visible against the carbon-fiber, Reiko and Taz wove the cosmetic shroud seamlessly over the remaining exposed circuits with a single stroke of shared will, sealing the weather out of her joints while honoring the patch beneath.

Reiko raised the aluminum can, clinked it lightly against the bottle in Roanoke's hand, and took another swallow of terrible beer.
