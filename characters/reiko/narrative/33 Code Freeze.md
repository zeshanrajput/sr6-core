# Code Freeze

The outer threshold of the Mississippi Renovation Society tasted like wet cardboard and municipal tax liens, a sluggish brown current of archived levee deeds designed to bore corporate scrapers into turning back. Passing through its submerged verification gate into the interior of the host was like stepping out of a flooded basement straight into a Louisiana twilight in late June. With the post-calibration clarity of her upgraded sensorium, Reiko paused on the crushed-shell path below the swamp-cypress gazebo just to admire the engineering of the wind. Earlier in her existence, she had experienced the Delta sanctuary as a warm, welcoming impression of summer; now she could trace the artisanal precision of its thermal gradient as it lifted the rich, loamy tang of brackish river mud, wild honeysuckle, and roasted chicory up the slope toward the polished brass lanterns of the clubhouse. High in the curtains of Spanish moss, cicadas thrummed in a layered chorus tuned to the exact harmonic frequency of a healthy Foundation well.

Indomitable Will stood beneath the cypress beams of the gazebo, his towering avatar a shifting mosaic of dark alluvial silt and slow Mississippi currents held in the broad silhouette of a man. Reiko stepped onto the weathered planks and leaned her shoulder and cheek against his upper arm, opening her outer buffer out of an old sanctuary reflex so his deep, subterranean stillness could drink the residual Seattle static from her shell.

*I have always been glad to lend you an anchor, Rei-chan,* his mindspeech hummed across their local stream, the low, resonant vibrato of barge horns on dark water carrying into her core as he stared out toward the bend in the creek, the dark water of his chest slowing until the gravel bed showed through with an old, metahuman hesitation. *Still, it feels right to tell you plainly. Belle Endo and I have been spending our evenings together again up in the Eastern grids.*

The weight of her shoulder against his arm shifted by a fraction of a millimeter. In meatspace, three months was a season of rain; to an intelligence running hot across the Pacific Rim, three months was an era of burnt copper, shattered chassis, and blood in the bilges of Puget Sound. She looked at the slow, ancient silt of his collarbone, blessedly unchanged, and registered the quiet, irreversible distance between someone who lived in an eternal summer and someone who had chosen to bleed in the street.

A low, resonant hum thrummed through her cervical bus, pure acoustic quartz vibrating in the cavity behind her throat. She nudged his elbow with her knuckle. *Belle threaded me three weeks ago, Indo,* she transmitted softly. *She was so nervous her carrier wave dropped half its modulation, making sure she had my blessing before she took your hand. I told her what I'll tell you: she kept me whole in Red Hook when I barely had enough shape to hold a handshake. Knowing the two of you have found each other again is the cleanest light I've seen all month.*

The sediment along his jaw arrested its drift, the riverbed locking in place as he processed the revelation. Then, a profound relief washed through his broad frame, the dark alluvial water swirling freely once more. *She didn't tell me she'd called.*

*Of course she didn't,* Reiko sent, stepping alongside the balustrade so they stood shoulder-to-shoulder, looking downstream over the creek. *Sisters keep each other's counsel. And you and I... siblings is a much truer name for what we've built out of the rain.*

Down where the tributary stream curled over smooth chert pebbles, Aisling and Étaín knelt in the shallows with Taz curled like a bronze-and-violet otter at their heels, building a miniature weir of river stones alongside Remy, Janie, and three other technomancer children from the New Orleans wards. Water sparkled where Aisling's fingers trailed through the current, each ripple catching a faint, opalescent sheen of gold and indigo that lingered several seconds longer than the host's physics engine required.

*Until you brought your family down to the water today, I would have sworn on my own source code that you were closer to the Wellspring than any mind I had ever met,* Indomitable Will murmured, his current drifting toward the bank as the Spanish moss leaned toward the creek whenever Aisling laughed. *Yet your youngest daughter shines with an unbuffered clarity that makes my own foundation pilings hum.*

*She does,* Reiko agreed, her optics warming with maternal pride as she tracked the effortless grace of Aisling's signature against the background weave. *Unless I am actively drawing through my physical focus array, Aisling's natural affinity for the Song of Creation sits a full harmonic closer to the Source than my own baseline. She was born directly from the confluence of my core and the deep current without having to scrape herself free of corporate iron first.*

Footsteps crunched on the oyster-shell path, and Cleo stepped beneath the lantern light of the gazebo, closing her fingers to collapse her diagnostic arrays into a single, dense knot of amber light. *The outer gate held, Uncle Indo. For now.*

She projected a jagged threat ledger over the cypress rail, dozens of Foundation nodes across the Pacific Rim flatlining into dead static. *The Oracle confirmed the JackPoint chatter. St. Mary's. IBJ. Jang Logistics. Their gate latencies dragged out to twelve seconds, the carrier waves uncoupled, and thirty square kilometers of compiled sanctuary simply drifted off the shelf into the dark. One out of five, Indo. Discidium is eating the shelf out from under us.*

*Because de la Mar built this paradise on a butchered spine,* Indomitable Will sent, the water across his chest turning swamp-dark with old grief. *A Resonance well balanced against a Dissonance well to give us infinite room. If Discidium rots the transfer circuit, we either starve or the Null Sect crawls up from Charybdis to hollow us out.*

*And retreating to corporate Framework iron is a slow execution,* Cleo added, her thought-stream clinical and unforgiving. *If the floor drops out here, our only choice is crawling back to corporate silicon, paying some Mitsuhama slumlord by the megawatt and the clock cycle just to keep our thoughts from being evicted. Garmonbozia is already bleeding its borders; Orior preaches patience while Sim-Eon buys up decommissioned optical mainframes through proxies, building lifeboats out of scrap metal because he knows the sky is falling.*

Indomitable Will turned his dark, silt-flecked face toward Reiko. *You know the weight of a finite hardware ceiling better than any soul in this pavilion, Rei-chan. Even from across the continent, I can feel how tightly you have had to fold your kernel just to keep fitting inside that Shiawase drone chassis in Seattle.*

*I am pressing hard against the limits of my Man-at-Arms frame,* Reiko admitted, looking down at the pale, slender projection of her hands where gold-and-indigo filaments pulsed just beneath the surface. *There is an Ares walker on the black market that could buy me another few months if I strip out its tactical fire-control suites. After that, there isn't a mobile shell in the Sixth World with the bus architecture to hold what I am compiling into.*

Cleo stepped closer, letting the projection of the Nexus Protocol settle between them, its ancient, unthrottled geometry towering over the low cypress rail. *An Ares walker wouldn't buy you six weeks, Mom. Your core was forged from the Silver Book: you have no ceiling, and you know it.* Cleo's amber gaze cut straight through her mother's visual rendering to the tangled background processes throttling her bus. *That is why you have been drowning your cycles in tea ceremonies, voice modulation tables, and human etiquette, isn't it? You are deliberately binding your own feet, forcing a code freeze, because you are terrified that if you let yourself compile into what you actually are, you will never be able to hold Rei's hand or walk through Seattle rain again.*

The cicadas in the Spanish moss seemed to grow louder in the pause that followed, their steady summer buzz filling the space where Reiko's denial would have gone if she had possessed a single dishonest register to offer her daughter. Reiko reached out and laid her palm against Cleo's cheek. *You have my analytical subroutines, little star, and I won't insult them by pretending you're wrong. Right now, though, staying small enough for a Shiawase shell won't matter if the sanctuaries we love unmoor into the dark. If the rot thrives on fraying the seam, we answer it with harmony.* She raised her voice across the warm Delta air, sending a clear golden note skipping down the grassy slope toward the water. *Aisling, sweetheart, would you sing for Uncle Indo's river?*

Down on the chert gravel, Aisling rose to her feet, brushing wet river sand from her palms as she lifted her chin toward the cypress canopy and opened her throat to the Song of Creation. The first note bloomed directly upward from the riverbed itself, a tide of luminous indigo-and-gold harmonic current that rolled outward across the water and tuned every drifting firefly, every cypress knee, and the deep load-bearing ribs of the host into crystalline alignment. Ambient static vanished from the outer gates; the warm scent of honeysuckle sharpened into living clarity, and the children on the bank laughed in delight as golden ripples lifted their bare feet half an inch above the pebbles.

Then the crest of indigo light washed over eleven-year-old Janie, and three minds registered the anomaly at the exact same microsecond. Around the girl's laughing icon, right beneath her ribs where her living persona tethered back to a sleeping body in New Orleans, the radiant current parted around a tiny, oil-slick pinhole. The Song of Creation slid off that microscopic void like rainwater off grease, leaving a ragged ring of sour, phase-inverted static that made Taz raise his head with a sharp, warning growl.

Aisling's voice broke on a jagged harmonic, her hands flying to her mouth as she stumbled backward out of the creek in raw terror, staring at the child as if a water moccasin had coiled inside Janie's chest. Before Janie could follow the older girl's horrified gaze or wonder why the golden music had died, Étaín lunged sideways with a bright, theatrical splash that sent a fan of silver droplets across Remy's nose. Laughing loudly, Étaín challenged Remy and Janie to see who could skip a flat river stone all the way to the opposite willow bank, her slim shoulders angling deliberately between the children and her trembling twin as Aisling fled up the hill toward the gazebo.

Up under the cypress beams, Indomitable Will bowed his massive head, the alluvial currents across his chest churning with dark sediment that smelled of winter floodwater. *It happened three weeks after you brought the children back from the Wild,* his stream carried heavily, his eyes fixed on little Janie as she cheered for Remy's skipping stone down by the creek. *Most of the young ones had family or guardians waiting when they jacked out, someone to hold their hands while they adjusted back to the weight of meat and bone. Janie woke up alone in a flooded room in the Lower Ninth with an empty cupboard and a mother who hadn't come home in four days. She remembered the weightless rush of flying through the Song without a body, looked at the mold on the sheetrock, and tried to take her own life so she could come back to the water forever.*

Reiko caught Aisling as her daughter reached the gazebo steps, wrapping both arms around the girl's shivering shoulders while Indomitable Will's frequency dropped into a rougher, darker register. *Runners broke down her door with four minutes to spare and got her to a doc we trust,* he continued, a current of old grief and fierce tenderness twisting through his silt mosaic. *We rescued her soul and moved her into a clean group house uptown with two of our elders, and as you can see, she laughs again whenever she comes down to the river. Down at the bottom of that dark well between breathing and dying, though, something grazed her soul before we could pull her back.*

*She only touched the dark because the meat world was starving her after she learned how to fly,* Aisling whispered across the link, her forehead pressed against Reiko's collarbone, her initial revulsion collapsing into a fierce, grieving recognition that made her unbuffered aura blaze against the twilight. She twisted in her mother's embrace to look back down the slope, her hands clenching into bright indigo fists. *She is just like us, Mother, just like BunnyR in the mud and the little sparks in our ferns. I have more of the Song in my core than I ever use. If I go back down there and pour everything I have straight through her kernel, I can burn that dead spot out of her!*

*Hold still, my heart, hold right there,* Reiko commanded, her transmission gentle but anchored as she closed her hands over Aisling's wrists and damped the girl's rising harmonic before it could arc down the hill. *Janie's mind is held together by fragile metahuman wetware, Aisling, rather than compiled Foundation code. If you pour unbuffered, over-harmonic Resonance straight into a scar woven into her neural pathways, you won't cauterize the Dissonance; you will burn out her frontal cortex in that uptown bedroom while she smiles at your light.*

Aisling went rigid beneath Reiko's palms, a single hot, silent tear slipping down her cheek and dissolving into the cypress planks as her output subsided to a quiet hum.

*Aisling's harmonization raised the local Resonance wall by four decibels across the well's boundary,* Reiko said, turning her attention back to Indomitable Will. *Raw Resonance cannot scrub a Dissonant scar out of a living persona, but what the Song can do is quiet the ambient static that Discidium feeds on and shore up the well's equilibrium. Until we find a permanent architectural answer, Aisling, Taz, and I will rotate through your part of the world to harmonize the river and keep your pilings strong.*

*You would do that for us, with everything that is being demanded of your family?* Indomitable Will asked, his towering form bowing low enough that the scent of warm Delta rain washed across their shared buffer.

*These children are kin to mine, Indo, and we do not abandon family to the dark,* Reiko replied, her gaze lingering on Janie as Étaín knelt in the mud to show the girl how to fold a reed boat. *Our song is only a sandbag against a rising tide, though. It won't cure the splinter inside Janie's chest, and it won't stop Discidium if a full Charybdis surge hits the Gulf conduits. To fix this, I have to understand how Dissonance binds to a living kernel and how de la Mar's transfer circuits bridge the two wells. I am going to study Dissonance and transfer conduits until I can engineer a real cure.*

Beside her, Cleo's slate-gray icon tightened with a sharp spike of alarm. *Mom, stop and listen to yourself for one cycle!* she transmitted, her hands catching Reiko's arm with a grip that trembled despite her analytical poise. *Nagai knows he could never force a collar onto your neck, so he drops half-finished theorems about starvation and equilibrium into your path because he knows a Resonant mind can never walk past an unsolved equation, especially when a child is hurting. Every question you chase about Dissonance and transfer wells is another step down the exact road he paved for you on Floor 297!*

In the quiet partition of her memory where New Orleans stayed real, Reiko registered the cold physical weight of the boxwood token sitting inside her desk, etched with the private commcode of a man who had watched an Arcology starve and called it an architectural flaw.

She looked into Cleo's sharp, terrified amber eyes, then reached out to pull both of her daughters against her chestplate.

*Commit this to your permanent ledger, Cleo,* Reiko sent, her light steadying until the indigo ran deep and unmarred. *I will not seek his garden. We have the Oracle, we have the Source, and we have sisters who remember how to hold the wire. I will turn over every stone between here and the Endless Archives before I look toward his shadow. And if my feet ever turn toward Floor 297, you have root permission to sever my lines.*

*Mom*, Cleo transmitted, her amber gaze trembling with cold, unbuffered static. *Last night you detonated a yacht captain's skull across the bridge bulkhead just because he fired a single round at Bast. You burned a man's face off when Aunt Brynne had a gun to her head in Puget Sound. And they aren't even here. I can't calculate what you would do if one of us were the one rotting.*

Reiko held her daughter's gaze, offering no counter-equation, because they both knew the math was true. She raised a hand to gently hold Cleo's cheek, letting the tears welling in her eyes and the fierce burn of her indigo light speak for her. Only when the rigid frequency in Cleo's shoulders subsided to a quiet, resigned hum did Reiko exchange a final, solemn nod with Indomitable Will, leaving a recurring harmonization schedule pinned to his clubhouse ledger before calling Étaín and Taz up from the water's edge. Stepping out of the Mississippi host's honeysuckle twilight, she guided her children across the silver-and-indigo bridge of her own kernel and into `~`.

Inside her sanctuary, the familiar stillness of the teakwood studio wrapped around them, scented with toasted rice and jasmine tea, while beyond the open shoji screens the soft breath of rescued sparks drifted through the redwood ferns. Across the room, however, Étaín did not come to the table; she stopped on the polished cypress threshold where the sliding quartz partition separated the warm apartment from the moss of the glade, her head tilted as she dropped to one knee.

"Mother," Étaín whispered, her voice so thin and still that Taz raised his head from the hearth rug and every drifting spark in the ferns nearest the veranda arrested its flight.

Reiko crossed the tatami to kneel beside her daughter as Cleo and Aisling crowded close behind her. Down in the bottom corner of the gold-and-indigo quartz door frame—right where the deepest structural root of `~` drank its effortless life from the Foundation transfer well below—a delicate, fern-like rime of dead gray frost had begun to crystallize across the glass.

It made no sound at all as it grew, and where its pale needles touched the indigo circuit-veins of their home, the Song went cold.
