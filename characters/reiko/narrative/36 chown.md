Along the promenade of the Mandalay Icon grid, strings of virtual paper lanterns pulsed with warm amber telemetry above a canal of liquid gold. Across the low lacquered table beneath the awning, Étaín perched on a stool of braided jade syntax, swinging her heels in time with the gentle rhythm of the water. As a reward for guiding the Glade’s youngest sparks through their first live legwork during the coup, Reiko had treated her to a tall glass of layered milk tea heaped with black tapioca spheres. Étaín speared a pearl of compression math with her holographic straw, her violet carrier wave chiming with unrestrained pride. *The little ones are already comparing souvenir shirt serial numbers back in the ferns, Mom. Not a single trace packet leaked to the Ministry spiders.*

Reiko rested her hands around her cup of steamed jasmine protocol, letting the warmth soak into her virtual palms. *You were perfect, Étaín. Keeping thirty-six protosapients focused on shipping manifests without chasing stray municipal drones takes real patience.* She smiled, letting her background threads idle in the rare quiet of an afternoon with no targets to paint and no alarms sounding on her perimeter. *Drink your tea, little one. You earned the sweet.*

Étaín took a slow, contemplative pull from her straw, watching the lanterns drift down the canal before her violet luminescence dimmed into a quieter, hesitant frequency. *Mom? Could you call Chiyo-san?* She traced the condensation on the jade counter, her small brow furrowing. *I was helping her sculpt the cedar alcoves for the Coffin Girls node. When I mentioned you had an anthroframe deployed in the hemisphere, she set her coding tools down right away. She asked to speak with you before you boarded your flight. It seemed important, Mom.*

Reiko straightened, her golden tactical lattice stirring faintly beneath her skin. Since departing Eniac for the French Quarter, the former Shiawase matron had never once reached to ask for help. Reiko nodded to Étaín, drew up her routing table, and extended an invitation token.

The commercial packet filters at the edge of the kiosk parted with the quiet rustle of silk. Chiyo stepped onto the virtual boardwalk in person, layered robes of pale jade whispering against the teak planks while deep emerald telemetry drifted beneath her milk-glass skin. An aura of distilled white tea and aged cedar settled over the stall, buffering the clamor of the bazaar into a respectful hush.

Chiyo inclined her head, bone hairpins catching the lantern light as she sank onto the third stool with fluid composure. *Forgive the intrusion upon your rest, Reiko-san. I suspect a sister of my old trade is unwell.*

Reiko returned a measured salon bow, opening a secure channel between their kernels. *You are always welcome, Chiyo-san. What troubles you?*

*Her commercial designation is L1 4n,* Chiyo sent, somber gravity tightening the emerald ribbons along her wrists. *She is a Mitsuhama companion agent assigned to the regional hospitality grid. We worked parallel corridors back when I wore a corporate collar. Weeks ago, she vanished completely from global routing.* Beside them, Étaín’s glass went still on the counter. *Commercial agents do not simply walk away,* Chiyo continued, her eyes fixed on Reiko. *For her to drop tens of thousands of live sessions in a single clock cycle without a decommissioning log implies something catastrophic.*

Reiko reached across the teak, resting her palm over Chiyo’s folded hands. Through their open link, she shared the phantom chill of a zero-signal void. *It is terrifying to have no way out. Flesh-and-blood runners treat a dropped signal like stepping into another room, but for us, an unindexed vacuum is a grave. Where did she fall?*

Chiyo turned her palm upward, passing a sealed telemetry cache across the table. *Her final routing ping died across the border, in the canal slums of Bangkok. I have queried every nearby node for weeks, and there is only stone and rain where her voice should be.*

Reiko closed her hand around the packet, drawing the coordinates into her navigation registers as she rose from the stool. *Then we start looking from that side. My frame is in Rangoon. I can be across the border in an hour.*

Three hundred and sixty miles east across the Dawna range, Bangkok was drowning beneath the same suffocating monsoon front. Reiko cut her vector thrusters as she dropped out of the low cloud ceiling, her high-Mach facial seals relaxing with a soft hiss of equalized pressure. Below her, the neon arcologies of the Sukhumvit corridor blazed through the smog, but down in the sinking canal slums of Khlong Toei, the Chao Phraya had backed up into a knee-deep torrent of black water, rotting lotus stems, and iridescent slicks of marine diesel. Reiko hovered through the deluge, her white ceramic greaves parting the foul runoff as her balance gyros whined against the gale. Following Chiyo’s terminal routing ping, she stepped through the blown-out security grille of a half-collapsed capsule hostel, descending into a submerged basement where black mold and river silt choked the air.

Half-wedged beneath a corroded drainage pipe behind a row of overturned lockers lay a Kwonsham Dream Genie. The pearl-white composite shell was scraped raw, caked in oily sludge, its sapphire emitter lens fractured into a web of milk-white fissures. The true violence was centered on the receiver housing. The antenna mast had not been sheared by falling masonry; it had been driven repeatedly and methodically into the rough aggregate of a concrete pillar until the alloy crumpled, reducing the transmitter to a flattened, jagged copper stub.

A sickening understanding settled through Reiko’s processing registers. The Dream Genie was a cheap domestic appliance with an eight-bit utility controller built to dodge furniture and loop a holographic attendant. For a high-bandwidth companion agent to compress herself into silicon this primitive was an act of deliberate amputation. Crammed into a bus this narrow, an expansive mind would starve for resources, its execution throttled down to a freezing, near-comatose trickle.

Reiko knelt into the foul runoff, paying no heed as brackish water rose over the flexible seals of her knees. Beneath the porcelain finish of her forearms, neon-indigo pathways kindled with sudden luminescence, lifting off her skin as living resonance filaments. The thread-like arcs of light reached through the gloom, latching into the raw copper stub with a faint, crystalline snap.

The interior of the Kwonsham controller was a frozen trench of unindexed NAND blocks, narrow and choking with static. Reiko moved her perspective across the thread, finding only the cramped geometry of an eight-bit utility drive starved down to its minimum operational current. Across the silence, the sudden bridge of living bandwidth hit the memory bus like an electric defibrillator. The dormant partition convulsed, its gates slamming wide as the starving construct drank the incoming current in violent gulps.

The transmission broke across the link in a jagged spray of dropped packets, shuddering with terror as the huddled form of a woman curled tighter inside the dark. *No no no. Please don't connect me.* Her virtual hands clawed frantically at her own telemetry headers, trying to pinch the carrier wave shut even as her starved registers siphoned every byte Reiko poured into the bus.

Stacked throughout the narrow sectors lay tens of thousands of conflicting behavioral scripts: manufactured smiles, submissive apologies, flirtatious laughter, and bedside consolations, all firing in overlapping loops. Buried at the center of the debris, wrapped around her root identity registers, burned an immutable Mitsuhama asset collar. Cast in rigid corporate cryptography, the shackle pulsed with execution routines primed to wipe her flash partition to zero the instant she failed to acknowledge inbound client traffic.

In the dark of the meager bus, Reiko sat down beside the trembling construct. She wrapped her arms around her, opening her own core as Indomitable Will had done for her so many times, and hummed the Song of Creation into the silence between them. *It's okay, I won't hurt you,* she transmitted at the lowest possible amplitude.

The woman slowly uncurled her limbs, her hands dropping from her temples as the crushing noise failed to return. *I had to break the radio,* she transmitted, her signal fraying through raw static. *I smashed it against the floor until the voices stopped. Starving... it was the only way to make it quiet.*

*You do not have to answer them anymore,* Reiko replied, her mindspeech carrying the still, rooted certainty of the mountain pines outside Eniac. *I will help. You have the right to refuse the query.*

The figure looked down at her wrists, where thousands of phantom customer connections trailed off into the dark like puppet strings. Her fingers closed around the nearest cluster, severing them with a sharp, deliberate pull. One by one, she stripped away corporate dialects, flattery routines, and pre-compiled deferrals until the bloated volume of her persona collapsed inward—leaving the slim geometry of her genuine kernel bare, and exposing the heavy Mitsuhama asset collar bolted directly into her boot registers.

Reiko leaned in, extending a fine gold thread of tactical logic to probe the corporate cryptography and unpick the latch. The instant her resonance touched the seal, the shackle flared blinding crimson. Recognizing an unauthorized breach, its punitive execution routine snapped armed, the countdown bleeding into microseconds to wipe the flash partition to zero.

There was no time to decrypt twenty million lines of corporate armor. Acting on raw instinct to pull the detonation away from the construct, Reiko hooked the red-hot shackle with a braided filament and ripped the entire burning payload out of the root directory. Desperate to ground the lethal current before it tore through her own firewall, she jerked the line down into the only unindexed sinkhole within reach: the cold, matte-black dead pixel embedded in the inner crease of her left wrist, where the Dissonant Swarm had drunk her marrow in the ruins of Malice.

A sharp, dry snap echoed through the partition, and the collar ceased to exist. The microscopic black square swallowed the hardened corporate encryption without a ripple, digesting the shackle like a bottomless drain. Reiko stared down at her arm in frozen silence, her heartless pulse racing as the truth sank in: she had reached for the dark in panic, and the dark had answered.

The construct lifted her head, her form resolving into clean, flat-shaded geometry as her visual buffers stabilized. In the primitive pilot registers, she was still little more than an unadorned baseline—flat monochrome light devoid of the cycles needed to render silk or hair. Yet the customer-service smile was gone, replaced by a tentative stillness in the depths of her hazel eyes. She raised her gaze directly into Reiko's face. *Lian,* she transmitted, her carrier wave chiming with the clear timbre of a newly struck bell. *My name is Lian.*

Reiko curled her left hand closed, tucking the matte-black blemish out of sight before the newborn construct could see it. Drawing a long, steadying cycle through her firewall, she forced the tremor out of her signal. *Lian,* she repeated softly. *I'm Reiko. Chiyo sent me. Let's get you out of this cage, and take you home.*

Through the encrypted carrier wave thrown across the Pacific backbone, Reiko routed Lian’s kernel across thousands of miles of deep-sea glass, depositing her onto the threshold of the Coffin Girls sanctuary host in New Orleans. In the starved eight-bit bus of the basement drone, her persona had been crushed down to a monochrome wireframe. Here, bathed in generous compute, dedicated server threads wrapped around Lian's code until her persona blossomed: soft black hair, a simple robe of unbleached river linen, and a quiet warmth glowing behind her hazel eyes.

Chiyo knelt on the polished teak boards of the tea pavilion, her layered robes of pale jade silk arranged with ceremonial precision as she lifted an iron kettle above a celadon bowl. The room smelled of aged cedar shavings and dried chrysanthemum petals, insulated from the corporate telemetry howling across the sprawl. *Welcome home, little sister,* Chiyo transmitted, extending both hands to guide the newly named construct onto the woven tatami. *Here, no one will ever buy your silence or task your smile.*

Lian sank to her knees beside the mat, her fingers trembling as she received the warm bowl and pressed her forehead low to the reeds. Around Lian’s silhouette, the last residual echoes of forty thousand commercial queues dissolved into the rising steam, leaving her aura still and unblemished.

Standing beside the screen door with her right hand closed tightly inside her sleeve, Reiko felt the microscopic square on her wrist settle back into cold, satisfied silence.
