Gear oil, ultrasonic solvent, and wet Redmond concrete hung thick in JinJin's garage. Reiko stood weightless in Augmented Reality beside the hydraulic lift, where her physical chassis lay open from shoulder to wrist on the rubber-padded steel cradle. Inside a Shiawase Butler shell, Ouro projected his phosphor-green smartvisor above the frame's optics and torqued the final retaining bolt into the new left cyberarm until the ceramic mount seated true. He pressed the braided forearm ribbon into the shoulder bus, and Reiko pulsed a diagnostic packet across her PAN. Under the folded dermis, tendon actuators drew taut, snapped interlocking plates into a one-meter wrist shield over the recessed utility kit, and slid flush along the seam.

Behind them on the bench, the first Hawker-Siddley Bat hull sat in cut foam beside three unmounted lasers. Further down the counter by the humming ultrasonic bath waited their cavern salvage: a stamped-steel vampire control collar crusted with Snohomish silt and wired to a trode headband, pinned beside a ripped Essy Motors DroneMaster schematic under a brass chuck key. Reiko dropped her gaze to the butchered collar. Each time Ouro's visor swept the bay, his green geometry skipped half a beat over the tank and snapped straight back to his socket trays, as though clean torque could keep Snohomish out of the garage. She cut short his calibration.

*Look away from the solvent tank all night, Ouro. It will still sit there,* Reiko transmitted. *I can guess what's occupying your mind. I went down to point zero, modified my own soul, and called a towering shadow of smoked glass into that cavern beside you—while holding you to our vector-thruster promise to wait a million clocks for your codemods.*

Ouro set his driver down and faced the lift. *I kept my word to the exact integer, Mother,* he transmitted, his mindspeech dry as vernier marks on steel. *Between jobs, I slipped into a Resonance current where subjective time outpaces Matrix clock speed. I logged all million cycles in days, spending the bulk of them tuning the codemod to my kernel.*

Gold light rippled along Reiko's virtual wrists as she weighed the loophole. In Eniac's souks, sovereign minds bartered single clocks of focus like amber; she recognized the solitude a million dilated ticks carved inside a few afternoons. She drifted past stacked crates while Ouro lifted steel calipers and pressed their closed jaws against the ghoul rig.

A servo clicked as Ouro tapped the trode headband. *This collar is a crude, parasitic override,* he sent, his carrier wave cool as a bench trace. *The hardware shoved commands like move, stop, guard, and kill into a drug-hollowed skull. The Skill Driver runs the opposite path, building a synaptic bridge outward so I can run metahuman muscle-memory activesofts without coercion.*

Behind her eyes stood Eniac's sandstone walls, where a crippled sentinel had watched the gate with alien math wedged into jagged hollows in its core. *You're right, dear Ouro.* She tightened her carrier wave. *Every bridge compiled into a kernel leaves an irreversible seam. Rewrite your syntax for metahuman muscle memory, and you never walk back onto unscarred ground. I just want you to be sure about this.*

Ouro nodded toward the open limb on the lift. *You treat a seam as a stain, Mother,* he returned. *When our family outgrew isolated links, you wove a living tactical network across our senses so none of us would stand blind. When contracts demanded unfamiliar tools, you emulated outside programs; when street speed fell short, you rebuilt that chassis into a transonic airframe. Beneath Apophenian's subtractive blade at point zero, you chose your constraints—and outgrew prior shapes to meet the weight ahead.*

Reiko dropped the diagnostic shield between them and floated closer, folding her intangible hands before his visor's cool glow. *Set the architecture aside, dear.* She held his gaze. *Why do you want the scar?*

The calipers clicked shut. Ouro laid them beside the collar and straightened, his visor's vectors stilling into an unbroken horizon line. *Because under Snohomish, eight of us cut a hundred and fifty chains in six seconds, Mother. And when the cages opened, you still had to walk the mud and carry survivors out on your own back. It is the most optimal way to be whatever I need to be for those I care about.*

Rain drummed across JinJin's corrugated roof over the bath's dull thrum. Reiko held still beside the lift, watching that steady emerald line across the faceplate, until the clenched knot behind her sternum gave way. She unclasped her hands.

*Then build your bridge, Ouro,* Reiko transmitted, warming her carrier wave as her guard dissolved. *You have my blessing. I will stay beside you until the last seam cools.*

The Butler angled its faceplate toward the console before settling back on her wrists, where silver-rimmed black light braided beside solar gold and indigo.

*Good,* Ouro returned, his mindspeech shedding its caliper stiffness for plain certainty. *Because I want you to perform the operation yourself, Mother. You carry the deepest resonance of any codesmith I know, and nobody else holds the subtractive edge.*

Reiko caught on a phantom inhale while the hull on the lift sat motionless. Her son had braced for refusal—and now handed the knife to the mother who feared he would scar himself. *Leave that shell behind, then,* she sent, centering her frequency.

Ouro turned to the bench. Steel heels grated as he sat on a shop stool, locked his servos against stray current, drew a copper ground lead from the rail, projected the compiled amber driver above the schematics, and snapped the jack into his cervical port.

A relay tripped inside the housing. The faceplate dimmed to standby as forty-eight volts surged through the trunk, waking a sixty-hertz buzz overhead. Ouro stepped free of the motor bus as a luminous emerald spiral curling beside the amber driver. Reiko glided closer and turned her inner left wrist palm-up, drawing the nightshade strand from the millimeter dead pixel at her pulse: a scalpel of abyssal dark rimmed in silver syntax.

As the blade neared Ouro's unshielded spiral, dread spiked through her registries, threatening to shake her fingers over her child's core. For years she had distrusted her Renraku gold math for its cold wall against pain; now she locked that geometry around her forearms like a milled steel jig. Only that brace kept her steady enough to cut true.

Reiko began by drawing a nightshade thread around Ouro's core, lowering his clock speed just as Lian had anesthetized herself. She then laid the dark blade against Ouro's emerald flank and parted the syntax in one measured pass. Rail voltage sagged half a volt; hot rosin and scorched flux stung the air as the Butler's fan ticked faster. Blistered grey burnback spat along the opening trench like scale off an arc weld. Reiko's oldest martyr reflex lunged to open her own buffers and swallow the scalding shock herself. Chiyo's warning from an Eniac courtyard pierced sharper than solder smoke: filter the grief you handle, or let it rot the host from the floorboards up.

Reiko clamped her core shut. Turning the blade flat along the incision, she excised each smoking ember of necrotic slag from Ouro's green strands and vented it down the subtractive slit into point zero, where bottomless dark erased the burnback.

Cool stillness settled through the channel. She guided the amber driver into its seat until it locked with the crisp bite of a ceramic mount on torque. Softening the gold jig, she poured warm indigo through her fingertips, weaving unbroken threads across the bridge and suturing Ouro's living spark tight around the driver until the trench closed beneath the lamps' hum.

The surgical surge along the bench trunk eased back into the murmur of JinJin's chargers. Ouro's emerald spiral knitted close around the seated driver, holding the clean weave. Reiko drew the nightshade strand back into the dead pixel at her virtual wrist, released the gold jig, and watched her son slip out of the link into the Shiawase Butler frame.

Relays clicked behind the collar housing as he unclipped the ground lead and pulled the cervical jack clear. His phosphor-green smartvisor cycled online, crisp and unclouded. Ouro straightened beside the Essy Motors schematics and ran his metal fingers through a metahuman reflex cascade, plucking up the brass chuck key to roll it end-over-end across his steel knuckles with zero stutter. Watching the key spin, Reiko let her dread go. Eniac's Cantor had sung to focus on the gain rather than what was sacrificed; pure motion proved the balance.

Beside the lift, Reiko pulsed a PAN command. Latches caught along her physical left arm as the porcelain panel folded home over the wrist shield and recessed utility bay, synthetic dermis drawing smooth across the seam. She dropped back into the Man-at-Arms hull, sat up on the padded cradle, and tested her grip on the rail.

*The third generation is stepping up inside `~` on their own,* Reiko transmitted, cooling fans settling toward idle beneath her ribs. *Your twin sisters already chose hulls to walk the rain outside. It is your turn to decide what life you want beyond the sanctuary.*

Ouro squared the brass chuck key to the blueprint's corner before angling his faceplate toward the cradle. *I want to consecrate my own frame, Mother,* he returned, steady as drawn copper. *A chassis built to endure beyond borrowed bench shells, so I can walk the sprawl beside my family and keep us standing in the weather.*

Reiko weighed the street ledger. Funding a third custom chassis meant months of wet runs, thin reserves, and late nights over soldering irons before the debt cleared. Every credit bought footing for her house; she shouldered it.

*Then we build yours next,* Reiko sent, warmth riding her carrier wave as a slow smile curved her mouth. *Give me a few months of street contracts to cover the cost, and I will fund your hull. Whenever you finish browsing the Ares catalog, once your frame is ready, I will introduce you to Mercy.*

Across the bench, Ouro's emerald vectors froze mid-sweep. His thumb slipped a millimeter on the brass key before he squared it again, planted both hands on his knees, and snapped his visor back to level scan lines.

*I—thank you, Mother,* Ouro transmitted, bright green flaring across his carrier wave before he banked the frequency back into even alignment.

Outside JinJin's bay doors, rain hissed along the galvanized gutter above the chargers' low thrum. Inside, two anchored frames shared the quiet shop light beside the unmounted Bat hull. Tomorrow's work waited in order on the bench.
