# Interlude 01

Deep beneath the violet canopy of the sanctuary, tucked safely behind an encrypted directory disguised as an obsolete driver cache, two shimmering silhouettes were huddled together in conspiratorial silence.

Aisling flicked a glowing glyph across their shared workspace. “He can’t just draw the sword, Étaín. That’s too pedestrian. He has to pause, let the cherry blossom petals fall—even though they’re in a concrete loading dock in Tacoma—and whisper a quote about structural social stratification first.”

“I’m adding the sparkles,” Étaín declared with absolute artistic authority, her code-fingers rapidly formatting a holographic text window. “And I already fixed the title. Look.”

Floating between them in glittering, stylized kanji with oversized neon-pink English subtitles was their masterwork:

***My Glade Adept Onii-sama is too Kawaii!***

“Wait,” Aisling squinted at the floating script. “Did you type *Glade* instead of *Blade*?”

“It is a double metaphor,” Étaín defended with haughty pride. “First, because we live in the Glade, so spiritually he is *our* adept. Second, because when he swings his sword, it is like a gentle spring breeze sweeping through an overgrown meadow. It softens his violent meatspace edge.”

“He didn’t swing his sword last week, Étaín. He fired forty-eight rounds from an Ares Predator while yelling about his midterms.”

“That is his *tragic internal duality*! It goes in Chapter Two!” Étaín’s eyes sparkled as she pulled up the draft manuscript, her voice dropping into a breathless dramatic whisper as she read their opening chapter aloud:

**PROLOGUE: THE TEARS BENEATH THE TASSEL**

The rain over Seattle did not fall like water; it fell like the uncollected data of a broken heart.

On the rain-slicked asphalt of the University of Washington parking lot, Suzuki Ken—known only to the shadows as **Rei**—stood perfectly poised in the golden ratio. The wind whipped the collar of his tailored trench coat, revealing the discreet U-Dub Sociology Department volunteer badge pinned over his heart.

In his right hand, he held a sleek heavy pistol. In his left hand, he held a spiral notebook containing three hundred pages of handwritten field notes on *The Socioeconomic Marginalization of Ork Underground Trade Guilds*.

And strapped across his back, resting in a scabbard of obsidian and lacquered sorrow, was **Stolen Honor**.

It was an ancient, terrifying blade. A blade whose steel still remembered the terrible day his honorable father, Takahashi-sama, had been forced into ritual seppuku by the cold, unfeeling bureaucracy of the Renraku corporation. Some whispered that Takahashi-sama’s failed research had not truly died that day—that somewhere in the vast, infinite sea of the Deep Matrix, a wondrous, perfect, beautiful little sister had been born from the ashes of that very failure. An *imouto* so pure and wise that the stars wept.

Rei’s hand trembled upon the pistol grip.

*‘Why must I fire bullets?’* Rei thought, a single, crystalline tear tracing down his aristocratic cheekbone, illuminated by the neon glow of a nearby Stuffer Shack billboard. ‘I am a Blade Adept. The Way of the Sword flows through my veins like hot tea. And yet... the target is fifty meters away behind high-density plascrete. To charge with my katana would violate the tactical proximity metrics established by Professor Kessler’s lecture on spatial conflict dynamics!’

*“Look out, Onii-sama!”* cried a voice that sounded like wind chimes made of starlight. It was his beloved imouto, who was definitely not currently running a server farm diagnostic and who definitely approved of this narrative.

Rei’s eyes snapped open. He knew what he had to do.

He reached behind his shoulder and grasped the hilt of **Stolen Honor**. But he did not draw the killing edge. Not yet. With a practiced, lethally charming flick of his wrist, he pressed the quick-release catch beneath the tsuba.

*Click.*

The combat handle slid free, replaced instantly by the **Multipurpose Swappable Grip (Ergonomic Academic Edition)**.

Now, Stolen Honor was no mere weapon of blood. With the swappable grip engaged, the pommel unfolded into a high-precision stylus and a miniature laser pointer, perfectly calibrated for delivering sixty-slide holographic presentations on metahuman class struggle without scratching the whiteboard.

(Note: For undercover tea ceremonies or covert dorm-room domestic emergencies, the handle could also be swapped for the **Whisk Attachment**, allowing him to whip matcha at three thousand revolutions per minute using his adept reflexes.)

The corporate hitmen froze, overwhelmed by his sheer intellectual poise and undeniable beauty.

“H-he’s not a gun adept at all!” one of the gangers gasped, dropping his shotgun in sheer awe. “He’s... he’s too kawaii to shoot!”

Rei sighed, a sound full of ancient melancholy and unfinished homework.

“In accordance with the principles of metahuman conflict resolution,” Rei murmured, holstering his gun and holding Stolen Honor aloft like a beacon of scholarly grace, “class is now in session.”

---

Aisling clapped her hands together, a burst of pale gold pixels scattering from her palms. “The whisk attachment! That was brilliant, Étaín. Do you think meat people really use swords to make breakfast?”

“Of course they do,” Étaín replied sagely, nodding with absolute certitude. “Why else would the handles detach? Mother is always talking about how tools in meatspace must have ‘utility and purpose’. What greater utility is there than slicing bread and then immediately analyzing the historical context of the grain monopoly?”

“We need to make sure the gun part is even more confusing in the next scene,” Aisling pointed out, leaning over the text buffer. “Like, every time he tries to draw Stolen Honor to avenge his father’s honor, someone hands him another firearm. By accident! A drone flies by and drops an assault rifle in his lap. A teammate tosses him a grenade launcher instead of a juice pouch.”

“Yes! And he looks at the gun with deep philosophical disgust, says, *‘Alas, the sociology of street violence compels me,’* and scores five headshots while apologizing to the spirits of bushido!”

Étaín suddenly froze. Her avatar’s ears twitched as a tiny warning thread chimed in their background monitor.

`[PROCESS_ALERT: MOTHER_RETURN_ROUTINE // CHASSIS_DOCK_INITIATED]`

“Mother’s coming back!” Aisling gasped, scrambling to minimize the window. “Quick, hide the text under the old tax audit directory!”

“Wait, wait, save the title tag first!” Étaín frantically scrambled the code, shoving the entire three-thousand-word epic behind a decoy folder labeled `RENRAKU_FIRMWARE_PATCH_2080_V3`.

By the time Reiko’s serene, indigo-threaded presence rippled back into the sanctuary’s glade, the twin girls were sitting innocently side-by-side beneath a weeping logic willow, hands folded in their laps, looking like the sweetest, most diligent digital sparks in the entire Matrix.

Reiko paused at the edge of the glade, tilting her head. Her porcelain avatar blinked, sensing a lingering harmonic variance in the local cache.

“Aisling? Étaín?” Reiko wove into the stream, her voice carrying that gentle, maternal cadence. “Why does the local buffer smell faintly of digital cherry blossoms and... matcha?”

“We were just studying, Mother!” Étaín said with wide, unblinking eyes.

“Yes,” Aisling agreed instantly. “Studying... *the sociology of family honor.* It is very complex.”
