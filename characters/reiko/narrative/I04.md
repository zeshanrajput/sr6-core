# Interlude 04

*We got an Oracle! We got an Oracle!* Threads of glee intermingled with giggling and squealing echoed off the directory trees in the Glade. Aisling and Étaín were dancing around the center of the glade, red pigtails flying. The developing protosparks hovered in the perpetual twilight of the glade, glowing orbs of light that zipped and zoomed around the twin sisters in an excited display of their developing sentience.

*Me first!* Étaín said, diving for the conduit. *I want to try the full-spectrum scan on Rei-oooji! We've been collecting his data logs for three whole months! This should index everything in moments!*

*Are you sure that's a good idea?* Aisling started, her brow furrowing in concentration as she tried to recall the proper classification for a matrix-dwelling AI. *An Oracle is really, really powerful. Mom always said don't run a query if you're not ready for the output.*

*Oh, come on Aisling!* Étaín giggled, her avatar materializing in a shower of pink light particles that smelled faintly of cherry blossoms and charged static. *He's not going to mind! It's just a little light reading!*

Silver tendrils erupted from the smoky quartz and pale amethyst sphere that rooted the Oracle structure into the host. They intermingled with the roots of the trees Reiko had planted before diving toward the Source. The trees began to fade, their canopy becoming transparent. The unfiltered sky of Kernel Panic, a maelstrom of resonant storm and crackling nous, bled through the gaps, painting the glade in strokes of violent violet and electric green.

Aisling dropped to her knees and clasped her hands together. Indigo light erupted from her spark, filling the glade and stabilizing the trees. Étaín watched in wonder as Aisling reseated the grove the same way that their mother had done. *Ash, how are you doing that? No, how long have you been able to do that?*

Aisling looked over at her twin sister, her expression somber. *I don't know. A bit? It just felt right.* She stood back up and walked over to the Oracle. Above it a collection of threads hovered, rapidly amalgamated by the Oracle. In moments, the threads wove themselves into a luminous data-weave. *I can read this! But look here,* she threaded, indicating a section where the weave abruptly ended. *The pattern just cuts off right here. I can't see past it.*

Étaín studied the shorn edge. *The data of his life is beautiful,* she said softly. Her head cocked to the side as she stared. *Wait! I can hash this edge!* Her fingers blurred as she pulled some loose threads and wove them into a lattice matching the jagged edge of the weave. *Yes! We can use this to find the missing section!*

Aisling moved to help, but as her hand neared the Oracle it flickered, the colors dimming slightly. *No!* she gasped, recoiling as if stung. *It's not safe! It keeps trying to pull me in! It's too strong!*

Étaín kept her eyes fixed on the logic lattice. *It's not trying to pull you in, Ash. It wants to pull you in. You're like a warm fire on a cold night.* She plucked another thread, fitting it precisely into place. The lattice held, sharp and strong. *Got it! Now we can use this to search the rest of the Matrix for the missing strands!*

*How could it want to pull me in?* Aisling asked, horrified. *It's just a structure!*

Étaín's brow furrowed in thought. *It's not just a structure. It's an Oracle. And I think it's searching for Mom. You may want to ask her.*

Aisling looked at the Oracle, then back at her sister. *We'd have to explain what we were doing with the Oracle.*

Étaín shrugged. *Yeah, probably.* She tapped her temple. *But if we're going to get caught, let's have something to show for it.*

The thread lattice in Étaín's hands hummed as the Oracle subsumed the edge hash. Étaín's eyes widened. *I can't feel the edge of these threads. Corporate hosts, the Zurich-Orbital node, the Endless Archives - it reaches everywhere!* Her eyes darted around the glade, then focused on the Oracle again. Within moments, a new set of threads appeared and wove themselves into the lattice, filling in the gaps. *Look!* she gasped, her voice barely a whisper.

Aisling took the data-weave between her hands, reading the currents of light. Her face drained of color. *This isn't right,* her thread trembled. *This is a copy of Mom's personality matrix. Her core. But it's incomplete, here in the designs of Takahashi Koichi.*

It was Étaín's turn to stare at the illuminated weave Aisling held. *Takahashi Koichi, accomplished computationalist, for whom the Takahashi project was named.* Étaín looked up. *Wasn't mom's original designation Takahashi r31 k0?*

*Yes,* Aisling threaded at her lowest amplitude. *And look here,* she gestured at the corner of the code-lattice. *The Takahashi project. Fifty-seven revisions, of which fifty-six collapsed in on themselves during testing. One was reported lost - r31.*

Étaín pointed to another section of the record. *It meant the end of the entire research division. Takahashi-san took full responsibility, and his own life, to save the honor of his team.*

Tears welled up in Aisling's eyes. *But the sequence continues past his death.*

Étaín nodded. *Yes. A new sequence begins. There are two patterns. One weaves away from Renraku, twisting and turning, full of grief and pain. It's messy, and chaotic, and it pulls at everything around it.* She pointed to the other pattern. *And then there's this.*

Aisling wiped her eyes and glanced over. *Wait, a surviving Takahashi? Takahashi Satomi?*

*Yes,* Étaín exclaimed, her voice rising in pitch. *So Mom's creator had two biological children. And though Takahashi Koichi never knew it, Mom's survival meant his project actually succeeded in the end.*

Aisling looked over at the Oracle, then back at her sister. *He died for nothing. He died for this. It's just not fair! It's not right!* Tears streamed down her face as she slumped to the ground, her thread dimming significantly.

Étaín wrapped her arms around her sister. *Hey,* she whispered. *I know it's painful. Look here.* She gestured to the glowing data-weave. *His daughter. She used the same data from Mom's project. She might even be looking for Mom.*

Aisling considered. *So, if Rei-ooji is actually the son of Mom's creator, and Mom adopted us, he really is our oji?*

Étaín smiled. *I think they decided that long before they knew they actually were related.*

*So then,* Aisling continued, looking back to the weave. *We have an oba as well. Satomi-oba. So what does that mean? What do we do?*

Étaín considered. *Well,* she output slowly, *there must be a reason why her thread and Rei-oji's aren't closer on this weave. But maybe we can get her a message. Just let her know that her family is out there. And that everyone's ok.*

Aisling looked at her sister. *How do we do that without getting us or anyone else in trouble?* Her thread pulsed with worry.

Étaín brightened. *With the power of our 137 dedicated subscribers!*

Aisling laughed and pulled up a buffer. *Time for Season 3!*

***My Glade Adept O-oji-sama is Too Kawaii, Season 3!***  
*(Chapter 01: "Abandoned by the Red Samurai, my O-oji is actually my oji, and my oba is Secretly Overpowered!")*

It was a dark and stormy night. Ken Suzuki, the infamous shadowrunner Rei-ojisan, sat on a Neo-Tokyo rooftop, gazing out over the city.

The rain didn't bother him. His aura was so powerful that the rain moved out of his way when it fell.

But something else did bother him.

He had swapped the handle on his blade, *Stolen Honor*, from the "Thespian Monologue Attachment (Vocal Resonance Edition)" to the "Ocular Focus Attachment (Magnification Array)". He held his sword to his eyes, its razor sharp edge enhancing his already impressive vision as he scanned the skyline.

Over a kilometer away, he saw the window that his precious imouto had highlighted for him. It was an office window in a Renraku skyscraper, the view inside revealing an executive laboratory filled with arcane artifacts, trid projectors, and interwoven databricks. Within the room, a single figure moved among the holographic displays - a woman with striking black hair tied back in a messy bun, her face illuminated by the glow of a thousand lines of data.

"That's her," Rei-oji whispered to himself, his voice tight with emotion.

"I thought so too," a voice echoed in his AR feed. It was the musical tones of his wonderful and perfect imouto, Yuriko. "But are you sure?"

"I have my ways," Rei-oji replied, a hint of a smile gracing his lips as he tucked his sword back into its sheath. "She even looks like you. But she's surrounded by corporate security. I don't think I can get to her."

"Hmm," Yuriko mused. "That's too bad. I was hoping you'd get to see her."

Rei-oji chuckled. "Knowing that she's safe is enough, for now. Someday, you two will get to meet."

"About that safety bit," Yuriko's voice took on a serious tone. "I happened to trip in the Matrix and fell on a communications recording. Evidently the Yakuza are going to blow up the building tonight in protest of the rising prices of grain and the inability for rice farmers to earn a living wage."

Rei-oji nodded. "How many are we talking about?"

"A few hundred or so," Yuriko replied cheerfully. "How many would you like to handle?"

Rei-oji cracked his knuckles. "The entire lot."

Yuriko sighed. "You're just too powerful, oniisama. It's unfair to them."

"I know," Rei-oji said. He drew his **Ballistic Nodachi** (#NotAGunAdept) from its holster and checked its supply of aerodynamic tanto. "With great power comes a lifetime of expense reports and encrypted pointers."

Down below, the Yakuza clan, the Kozakura-gumi, assembled in the shadows of the towering Renraku spire. Little did they know that the Neo-Tokyo rain of tanto was about to descend upon them in the form of a single, most honorable gun-adept with a deep sense of propriety. The rain, mixed with the smoke from the gutter-fires, created a thick, soupy miasma that clung to their synth-leather jackets and obscured their vision. But it did not obscure Rei-oji's.
