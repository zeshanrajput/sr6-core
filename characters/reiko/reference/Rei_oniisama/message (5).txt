The night after the run at the Eagle’s Nest, Rei and Dolly got to talking about Combat Cycling.  That turned into talking about driving.  Which turned into a drunken pact, sealed with a shot of cheap synth-bourbon.  Dolly would teach Rei how to drive like he was “being chased by revenuers.”

Dolly pinged him the next morning, bright and chipper.  Class was in session.

She ran Rei through the backroads of Kentucky.  Hilly terrain, roads ranging from wide and paved to barely there, and bored local KE-contract deputies with nothing better to do than chase suspicious out of towners.  It was, as Dolly put it, “the perfect fraggin’ classroom.”  Sensei approved when he heard about the training and insisted Rei pull up Smokey and the Bandit on the trid to watch with her.
Most nights they ended up back at the Eagle’s Nest, Dolly lecturing between wings and long pulls of beer.

“Driving’s all about friction management,” she said, tapping the table for emphasis. “Exceed your friction limit and you’re skidding out of control on molten rubber until you get back under it.  Accelerating, braking, turning, all of it costs friction.  Spend too much in one place, you don’t have enough left for the others.  Go too fast into a turn, you’re over the limit.  Brake while turning, you’re splitting the bill.  Brake too hard while turning too sharp, you’re exceeding the limit.  Brake, then turn.  Friction for one, then the other.”

Some nights Granny joined them.  Granny had very clear expectations about “how a gentleman should treat a lady,” and radically different expectations about how a family matriarch could treat gentlemen callers who didn’t meet those expectations.  Violence was tolerated in one and not in the other.

Still, Granny was a gracious host when her rules were followed.  She insisted on showing off her beloved Kentucky to Rei, which also meant showing it to Dolly, to her general distress.  They toured the Bourbon Trail, watched races from Granny’s private box at Churchill Downs, and visited historically significant sites like Abraham Lincoln’s birthplace, Fort Boonesborough, the Perryville Battlefield, and, for reasons Granny refused to explain, the Kentucky Fried Chicken museum.  She insisted they experience all Kentucky had to offer.

After about a week, the next job came in.  Europe this time.  Rei made his goodbyes.  Dolly waved him off with a grin and a drawl.

“Y’all come back now, ya hear?”
