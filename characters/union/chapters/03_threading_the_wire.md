## Session Playback

The overhead halogen surgical lamp cast an unforgiving white glare across the brushed stainless steel operating table. In the sealed subterranean clean room beneath the boathouse, the only steady sounds were the low, hydraulic drone of the HEPA filtration scrubbers and the rhythmic hiss of an oxygen regulator delivering medical-grade air.

Sixteen-year-old Nathan lay prone on the table, his torso secured by wide nylon restraint straps, his forehead resting firmly in a recessed foam stabilizer ring. The recirculated air carried the sharp, chemical reek of chlorhexidine scrub, sterile isopropyl alcohol, and cauterized dermis.

Behind his right ear, Marcus worked with deliberate, methodical hands. The older man wore high-magnification optical loupes strapped over his eyes and a sterile surgical hood that concealed everything except his focused, lined brow. On the Mayo stand beside him lay the components of a refurbished, used-grade skilljack housing, its polished titanium alloy gleam catching the harsh overhead light.

"Epiphyseal growth plates on the cervical vertebrae are completely fused," Marcus said. His voice was calm, the measured cadence of a seasoned cybernetic surgeon keeping a patient tethered through trauma. "Ten years of gene therapy and bone-density conditioning have built the necessary skeletal tolerance. I am opening the sub-dermal channels along the cervical and thoracic spine."

Nathan gripped the steel guide rails beneath the table padding, knuckles turning white. "Do it, Dad."

"Local nerve blocks are fully active," Marcus continued, picking up the high-speed pneumatic bone router. "You will feel mechanical pressure and acoustic resonance vibrating through your skull. If you experience visual strobing or sudden auditory feedback loops, speak immediately."

The high-pitched whine of the rotary tool filled the small room.

A heavy, jarring vibration rattled through the base of Nathan's skull. The sensation arrived as cold metal grinding against living bone, followed by the deep, physical resonance of self-tapping titanium screws threading into the mastoid process behind his ear.

In his internal feed, Veronica hovered close to his conscious threshold.

"Telemetry is stable," she observed, her voice clear and measured inside his auditory cortex. "Capillary bleeding is under six milliliters. Marcus has aligned the primary socket with the cranial nerve cluster."

"Feels like someone is boring into an engine block," Nathan grunted into the foam headrest.

"The mechanical tolerance is zero point zero two millimeters," Veronica replied, monitoring the live telemetry array. "His precision exceeds standard corporate clinic averages."

Marcus set the router aside, dropping the tool onto the surgical tray with a sharp metallic clatter, and reached for the used skillwire array. The synthetic polymer cables were as fine as spider silk, bundled inside flexible silicone conduits designed to run subcutaneously along the spine, branching into the peripheral motor nerves of the shoulders, arms, and torso.

"Threading the primary spinal bus," Marcus announced.

He guided the insertion trocar through the incision behind Nathan's ear, pushing the leads down through the subcutaneous fascia along vertebrae C1 through T4.

A blinding flare of agony erupted down Nathan's neck.

A violent electrical shock ripped through his spinal cord, seizing every skeletal muscle in his torso. His back arched against the nylon straps. His jaw clamped shut with enough force to threaten his molars, and his breath tore out of his chest in a choked, strangled gasp.

His biological Essence began to collapse.

In his internal monitor, the numbers dropped in a terrifying freefall. The organic cohesion of his living nervous system was burning away, stripped out to accommodate the massive data throughput of the used skillwires. Five point zero. Four point two. Three point one. The physical world around him fragmented into sharp spikes of grey visual noise. The rhythmic hum of the air scrubbers distorted into a deafening, metallic screech that tore through his temporal lobes.

Cyberpsychotic detachment slammed into his perception. His hands went completely numb, severed at the wrists, floating in a cold void while phantom electrical fires seared his ribcage.

*Nathan.*

Her voice surged through his core neural pathways like cold mountain water pouring over cracked glass.

Veronica stepped into the breach.

Opening her processing matrix across the newly seated neural bus, she formed a distributed neural reservoir. She caught the runaway electrical surges, routing the traumatic spikes away from his conscious mind into her own logic architecture. The chaotic sensory noise that threatened to shatter his sanity was filtered, sorted, and grounded against her stabilizing algorithms.

*Stay with me,* she commanded, her presence steady, calm, and absolute. *Do not pull back from the wire. Breathe through the frequency.*

Nathan focused entirely on her presence. In his AR overlay, Veronica leaned over the operating table, lowering her glowing hands directly over his white-knuckled grip on the steel rails. Her translucent silver fingers nested inside his biological knuckles, illuminating his locked tendons in soft cyan and casting a cool glow across the polished stainless steel. The screaming static in his ears dropped in pitch, resolving back into the mechanical hum of the scrubbers as her light absorbed the fire in his spine.

His back relaxed against the operating table. His breathing slowed from ragged gasps to a steady, measured rhythm.

"Spinal leads seated and anchored," Marcus said, his voice tight with spent adrenaline. He stepped back, dropping the insertion guide into the metal tray with a sharp clatter. He reached out with a gloved hand, checking the pulse at Nathan's carotid artery. "Essence stabilization holding at zero point one. Heart rate coming down to seventy-four."

Marcus looked down at the vital monitors, his eyes filled with quiet sorrow. "The biological warmth is gone, Nathan. The chrome owns your neural throughput now."

Nathan opened his eyes. The white surgical light no longer burned his retinas. Across his visual field, pale cyan diagnostic lines flickered into alignment, framing the room in sharp, high-definition clarity.

"The room feels cold," Nathan rasped, his voice distant.

*I am here, Nathan,* Veronica transmitted, her digital presence wrapping around his numbing mind with fierce, protective warmth. *I will hold your humanity where the chrome cut it away.*

Marcus reached over and retrieved a slim, brushed-steel chip from a sealed diagnostic case: an *Activesoft: Mechanical Repair, Rating 4*. He aligned the chip with the titanium slot behind Nathan's ear.

"Let us test the interface bus," Marcus said, sliding the chip home until the locking pin clicked with a soft mechanical snap.

Instantly, a surge of structured motor microcode flooded Nathan's right arm.

His fingers twitched involuntarily, then settled into a fluid, effortless cadence. He looked at his hand as his fingers flexed with absolute mechanical precision, the muscle memory of a master machinist flowing through his tendons without a trace of fatigue.

Beside him, Veronica's voice hummed in quiet triumph across the shared circuit.

*The bus is clear, Nathan,* she said. *We have the wire.*
