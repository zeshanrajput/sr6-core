# A Night of Her Own

## Log Entry - 2082-Mar-11

## Geocoordinate Resolution - Savannah Sprawl, River Street Market, CAS

## Tagged Persons of Interest

- Veronica (Physical Lead via Monad Resculpt)
- Nathan Turrent (Internal Passenger / Neural Anchor)

## Session Playback

The rain over the Savannah River came down in warm, heavy drops, carrying the dark scent of tidal mud, diesel exhaust, and fried dough from the waterfront stalls along River Street. In the narrow brick alleyway behind a commercial cold-storage facility, Veronica stood against the damp masonry, waiting for the cellular heat in her skin to subside.

The Resculpt was a grueling biological ordeal. For twenty minutes, subcutaneous nanite swarms had realigned the physical body's muscle fibers, shifting bone cartilage, softening the jawline, and narrowing the shoulder span. The skin across her collarbone and cheeks still radiated intense feverish heat, the metabolic cost of rebuilding a living human frame from within. She lifted her hands. The fingers were slender, agile, the palms smooth.

*How is the balance?* Nathan asked from the passenger seat of their shared consciousness. He sat comfortably in the background of their neural feed, keeping the autonomic breathing rhythm steady so she could focus on motor coordination.

Veronica took a slow, deliberate step forward onto the wet cobblestones. Her center of gravity was three inches lower than Nathan's natural stride. The heavy pull tugged at her calf muscles, demanding constant micro-adjustments from her inner ear.

"The air resistance is real," Veronica whispered aloud. Her vocal cords produced a clear, resonant alto, a distinct physical voice that vibrated through her own throat and chest. She paused, drawing in a deliberate breath as she calibrated the physical effort, discovering how much thoracic air a single spoken sentence actually demanded. "In the matrix, vector acceleration has no drag. Here, every movement pushes against air pressure."

*You get used to the friction,* Nathan reminded her with quiet warmth. *Relax your shoulders. Let the skillwires handle the motor balance.*

Veronica stepped out of the shadow of the alleyway into the bustling pedestrian promenade of River Street. Physical neon signs in amber, jade, and electric magenta reflected across the rain-slicked asphalt, layered thick beneath a blinding storm of augmented reality objects. Animated holographic menus hovered over food carts, commercial spam beacons broadcast discount vouchers across the pedestrian flow, and corporate data tags competed with animated street graffiti sprayed in glowing code across the brick facades. Crowds of off-shift dockworkers, aerospace technicians, and riverboat gamblers moved between the stalls beneath wide umbrella awnings. The sheer density of sensory collision was staggering: the sizzle of sesame oil in cast-iron woks, the low chatter of metahuman voices in half a dozen coastal dialects, and the humid river breeze chilling the thin cotton of her dark jacket while thousands of floating AROs drifted through her optic nerves like glowing embers.

She walked past a display of woven sweetgrass baskets, her fingertips lightly brushing the dry, fibrous material. The texture registered instantly through her dermal nerves, a prickly, organic roughness that no simulated matrix texture map had ever replicated with true fidelity.

*Hungry yet?* Nathan asked. *The noodle stall two blocks down uses authentic scotch bonnet peppers in their pork skewers. Dad brought me there three years ago.*

Veronica directed her steps toward the red canvas awning of the hawker stall. Thick plumes of fragrant white steam billowed into the night air, carrying the rich aroma of seared pork, garlic, ginger, and roasted chili oil.

She stepped up to the counter. The stall operator, an elderly ork woman with heavy silver rings through her tusks, looked up from her smoking grill.

"Two skewers, sweet and hot," Veronica said, producing a certified credstick between two fingers.

The woman tapped the credstick against her payment terminal, handed over a paper tray holding two skewers of charred, glistening pork belly, and nodded toward the river. "Careful with the glaze, girl. It bites back."

Veronica carried the tray toward the wrought-iron railing overlooking the Savannah River. A massive container barge was gliding downriver toward the Atlantic, its navigation lights cutting long streaks of emerald through the dark water.

She lifted the first skewer and took a small bite.

The physical sensation was immediate, overwhelming, and complex. First came the searing temperature of the rendered pork fat, crisp and caramelized against her teeth. Then the sharp explosion of salt, crushed garlic, and sweet cane syrup rolled across her tongue, followed instantly by a blistering wave of capsaicin heat that bloomed across the roof of her mouth and traveled down her throat.

Her eyes widened in biological shock.

A brief sensory micro-stutter rippled through their shared link as the chemical fire rushed from her physical taste buds into her digital buffer. Her tear ducts reflexively opened, spilling warm tears over her eyelashes, while an unexpected spike in pulse and adrenaline caught Nathan off guard in the passenger seat, sending a jolt through their autonomic balance.

*Veronica?* Nathan called out, his internal presence tightening with reflexive concern. *Are you all right? Is the nanite matrix destabilizing?*

Veronica brought the back of her sleeve up, wiping the hot moisture from her cheek. A bright, uninhibited laugh escaped her throat, the sound catching the attention of two passing dockworkers who turned to smile at her sheer delight.

"I am completely fine, Nathan," she whispered, pausing briefly to draw a fresh breath before continuing her sentence. Her voice trembled with intense joy. She took a second, larger bite of the spiced pork, relishing the fierce, stinging burn across her lips. "It is messy. It is painful. It is entirely irrational."

*Told you meatspace has its perks,* Nathan chuckled softly in the back of their mind.

Veronica rested her elbows against the wet iron railing, looking out over the dark expanse of the river as rain misted against her face. She closed her eyes, savoring the lingering heat of the food, the cool breeze against her damp skin, and the quiet presence of her brother sharing the heartbeat in her chest. As the chrome took more of his biological warmth, she knew she would carry the empathy for both of them.

"Thank you, Nathan," she murmured into the night. "I will keep us human."
