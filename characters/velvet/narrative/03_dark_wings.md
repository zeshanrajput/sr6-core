# Dark Wings

Under the hundred-thousand-watt daylight arrays of the Wuxing soundstage in Neo-Tokyo, the arena floor of *Astral Athletics: Idol Showdown* stretched across three acres of polished chrome obstacles, magnetized climbing lattices, and synchronized broadcast cameras. High in the acoustic rafters, fifty automated camera drones hovered like oversized beetles, tracking every metahuman leap and spell-assisted pivot for millions of viewers across the Pan-Asian Pacific feed. Tanaka Ryo stood at the western staging gantry in his unit's charcoal and silver racing silks, his pulse held at a steady sixty beats per minute while the sub-floor transducers pumped synthetic adrenaline anthems through the reinforced decking. Without warning, the secondary transformer bank beneath the gantry let out a high-pitched acoustic scream, its cooling lines rupturing under the load of the lighting grid. The main breaker tripped with an explosive crack, killing the daylight arrays in an instant and plunging the three-acre soundstage into sudden, suffocating darkness.

In the confusion of shouting floor directors, blaring static alarms, and rebooting security feeds, Ryo stepped off the staging gantry. Low-light filters in his retinas painted the dark corridors in sharp monochromatic grey. He moved through the backstage maze with unhurried, measured strides, his boots making no sound on the vibration-damped rubber flooring. Above the staging bays, the scent of vaporized transformer coolant and scorched fluoropolymer insulation drifted through the air vents, thick enough to mask his movement from the perimeter security dogs. He navigated two soundproofed access tunnels, reached the private VIP green room at the far end of the talent corridor, turned the brass handle, and pulled the heavy acoustic door shut behind him. The latch caught with a soft click, sealing the room against the external roar of the arena.

Luna sat in a low armchair in the far corner of the unlit room, her white stage silks catching the faint amber glow of an emergency exit sign above the private washroom. In the astral space near the acoustic ceiling panels, the bound watcher spirit tethered to Ryo's aura drifted in a slow, circular patrol. The entity was an icy draft of corporate firmware, tracking his core temperature, his blood pressure, and the cadence of his respiration, programmed to ping Mitsuhama security in Kyoto at the first physiological deviation from his talent profile.

"You're late," Luna said, her voice stripped of the airy, manufactured brightness broadcast across the weekly entertainment grids. A faint mechanical tick came from the cervical actuators in her neck as she turned her head toward him, optical shutters clicking twice to adjust to the dim gold light. She sat perfectly upright in the armchair, ankles crossed in the resting posture drilled into every contract trainee.

"The secondary corridor had security patrols scanning talent passes," Ryo said. The syllables slipped out in the soft, breathy cadence of Lee Ji-yoo before he caught himself, forcing his larynx down into Tanaka Ryo's rigid baritone. He crossed the carpet in three silent steps, keeping his shoulders angled away from the overhead watcher's patrol cone.

He sat on the arm of the leather sofa opposite her, resting his hands flat across his knees. In the absolute gloom, away from the staging lights and corporate lenses, the tension in his throat held between his assigned profiles. He deliberately released the cosmetic spell's hold on his jawline, letting the compressed cartilage settle into an aching, neutral middle. The bone-latch pressure behind his ears subsided into a dull, thrumming heat as the artificial symmetry relaxed into the raw baseline of his breath.

Luna raised a slender hand, her fingertips tracing the edge of her collarbone where Wuxing dermal plating met living flesh. Thin chrome seams split the porcelain surface in the faint emergency glow, inlaid with microscopic silver serial numbers stamped directly into the alloy: WUX-EXP-884-LUNA. "They adjusted the biometrics this morning," she said, her voice flat against the hum of the wall scrubbers. "Lung capacity up twelve percent for the aerial choreography. My chest cavity feels hollow, like an empty tin can."

"They plane the cartilage first," he said, the softer elegance of Ji-yoo's phrasing still bleeding into the lower register as his vocal cords rested. "Then the binding wash sets the tissue. It stays dry behind the ribs for the first year until the nerve endings stop firing." He kept his hands motionless on his knees, refusing to reach across the dark before she invited it.

"Does it ever stop?" she asked, her eyes catching the faint red strobe from the ceiling smoke detector. Her fingertips stayed resting against the seam at her clavicle, tracing the inlaid silver letters as if testing whether they were still flush.

"No," he said, the voice settling entirely into the un-sculpted baseline. "You learn to stop checking the mirror." He drew a quiet breath into his lower ribs, letting the manufactured composure drain out of his shoulders until nothing remained in the room but two bodies waiting out a power failure.

Luna leaned forward, the white silk of her racing tunic rustling against the leather upholstery. Her shuttering optics fixed on his throat in the gloom, tracking the un-augmented pulse beating beneath smooth flesh. She reached across the narrow space between their seats, her fingertips touching the back of his hand. Her palm was warm from the staging lights, yet the fingers were rigid with micro-hydraulic joints that lacked the irregular, organic tremors of birth tissue. Through the physical contact, the hollow in her aura pressed against his astral senses. Most of her native essence had been scooped out to seat reflex boosters, internal telemetry lines, and sensory processing chips, leaving behind an expanse of quiet emptiness framed by corporate chrome.

"You're both," she whispered, her voice barely carrying above the low hum of the corridor ventilation. "Aren't you?" Her thumb brushed once across his knuckles, as if testing whether the pulse beneath belonged to a person or a script.

The watcher spirit drifted closer in the astral plane, its icy aura brushing the hairs on the back of his neck as it sampled his galvanic response for signs of panic. Ryo kept his respiration perfectly level, timing his breaths to the sixty-beat pulse dictated by his biometric tracker while his thumb gently found the edge of her wrist. Beneath the floorboards, the heavy backup generator whined back to life, its low-frequency vibration rising through the decking as emergency beacons along the baseboards flickered from amber to green.

"Hana," she whispered into the dark between them. "My real name is Hana." The syllables settled into the quiet room, un-stamped by corporate patent offices or marketing telemetry, lingering for a single breath before the staging circuit caught.

"Jin-Young," he answered, his thumb pressing against the warm chrome seam where her hand met the wrist casing. He let the name stand between them unadorned, holding her gaze until the overhead relays clicked.

She pulled her hand back a fraction of a second before the main circuit caught. The soundstage flooded with blinding white light, washing the red shadows from the walls in a single pulse. When the Wuxing floor manager shoved the door open with a datapad in hand, Hana was already sitting upright with her posture straight and her chin lifted, eyes wide and smiling for the broadcast lens, while Tanaka Ryo stood by the door, his shoulders squared and his face locked back into immaculate corporate poise.
