The sub-floor dressing room beneath the Singapore mega-dome sat forty feet below the coastal water table, encased in six-foot slabs of vibration-dampened ferrocrete. Outside the climate envelope, the heavy equatorial heat of the Malacca Straits pressed against the reclaimed shoreline, but down in the prep trenches, the air was chilled to a dry, sterile eighteen degrees. Through the reinforced observation viewport overlooking the staging gantry, Tanaka Ryo watched two Mitsuhama corporate mages secure an inductive copper collar around Hana’s neck. The technicians worked in silence, their charcoal coats bearing the crimson triple-rhombus of Mitsuhama Media Logistics. Their sensor gloves glowed pale emerald as they calibrated the resonance couplings, tuning the frequencies until the secondary stage conduit hummed with a low, sub-audible vibration that rattled loose bolts in the deck plates.

Hana stood motionless on the prep riser under flickering mercury vapor tubes. Her porcelain dermal seams caught the harsh downlight, throwing sharp reflections across the bare concrete walls. Her chin stayed angled toward the overhead acoustic baffle, cervical actuators clicking at two-second intervals as internal micro-fans vented heat from her vocal processors. Across her collarbones, the corporate serial numbers were inlaid in microscopic silver foil: *MCI-EXP-884-LUNA*.

Beside Ryo's vanity mirror, the stage logistics feed scrolled in amber characters across the glass: *Unit 7 // Broadcast Apex // Full Resonance Extraction // Terminal Encore*.

"Sixty seconds to lift locks," the floor manager’s voice came through his earpiece, flat and clipped against the muffled seismic rumble overhead. "Ten-No-Koe choreography engages on the downbeat. Clear the gantry." Ryo stepped away from the glass, checking the immaculate reflection one last time: charcoal tunic tailored to the millimeter with silver silk piping, shoulders broadened by structural bio-sculpting, dark hair parted with razor-sharp corporate geometry. Beneath the polished exterior, his larynx carried the dull, grinding ache of the vocal shift, the cartilage held rigid against the instinct to soften into Lee Ji-yoo’s lighter cadence. He fastened the magnetic seals on his stage cuffs while high above his left shoulder in the astral space, the bound watcher spirit hovered—a cold draft that tracked his core temperature, his blood pressure, and the cadence of his pulse straight to the security node in Kyoto.

"Ten seconds," the floor manager said, his tone flat in Ryo's inner ear as the hydraulic lock pins retracted along the pit. Ryo walked out onto the central staging platform, boots planting on the copper conductor plate chilled by subterranean cooling coils and etched with concentric spirals into the arena's geomantic lines. Ji-Hoon stood at the northern riser, chrome forearm casings gleaming under the staging floods, while Tae-Hyun adjusted his lacquered wolf mask at the eastern mark and Min-Ki checked his footing on the western boundary plate. None of them spoke; speech outside the designated broadcast telemetry carried automatic behavioral penalty deductions against their quarterly talent trusts.

The lift engaged with a heavy hydraulic hiss, sub-floor acoustic drivers pumping seventy tons of sound pressure through the alloy rigging until it vibrated straight through his shins into his jaw. As the lift breached the stage deck into the blinding glare of the daylight arrays, eighty-five thousand voices hit him like a physical wall. The crowd packed the tiered terraces beneath the smart-glass dome, a vast sea of synchronized glow-sticks and flashing AR banners undulating under the humid equatorial haze. Fifty-foot corporate holograms rotated in the stadium airspace, projecting the idol unit's faces into the clouds above a sea of pulsing telemetry feeds.

The downbeat dropped. The choreography seized the unit with automated precision. Ji-Hoon hit the northern apex, limbs snapping into position as his internal cybernetics hummed in sync with the heavy synth-percussion. Tae-Hyun drove the eastern harmony, his wolf mask throwing sharp blue shadows across the first twenty rows, while Min-Ki anchored the western perimeter. Ryo crossed the center mark on count four, boots planting on the central conductor plate as the opening vocal line began.

The circuit closed, surging across the arena floor as the geomantic siphon opened wide to harvest the raw, uncritical adoration of the packed terraces. As the unit's lead channel, Ryo became the biological lightning rod. Manufactured euphoria tore through his meridians with scalding heat, expanding his aura until the spirit ribbons of his Musok training frayed under the sheer volume of collective desire. His voice rose into the arena's quad-directional acoustic array, pure and effortless, riding the crest of the chorus while the telemetry monitors in his peripheral vision spiked deep into the red.

Through the white glare of the pyro flares, the secondary lift crested the stage floor. Hana rose on the sacrificial riser, framed in shimmering white brocade. The instant the staging conduit touched her copper collar, her cervical actuators locked with a sharp metallic snap. Her jaw was pulled upward, throat exposed to the daylight arrays as the Mitsuhama extraction protocol began tearing into her spiritual baseline. The crowd roared in response, mistaking her rigid, trembling posture and the radiant aura flare around her shoulders for an advanced holographic effect. The collective adoration of eighty-five thousand fans, channeled through Ryo's voice, was being compressed and focused into her neck collar, stripping her essence to charge the corporate geomantic batteries buried in the arena foundation.

Ryo hit his turnaround at bar sixteen, spinning toward the center riser. Through the swirling stage smoke and strobes, he fixed on the porcelain seam along her cheek. Her breathing came in short, shallow gasps, optical shutters cycling erratically as the circuit burned through her neural firmware. Then her gaze shifted, cutting through the sweep of automated broadcast drones, and locked directly onto his face.

The rehearsed stage-smile dropped from her lips. In the two seconds before the chorus peaked, her mouth formed a quiet, crooked curve—an un-scripted expression of recognition that answered his silence and released him from his marks. Holding his position for eight more bars would allow the extraction to complete cleanly, leaving Hana an empty shell in the medical ward while Tanaka Ryo returned to his penthouse with his corporate contract intact. The logistics division would file the incident as an acute resonance burnout during a historic performance, paying out a modest survivor stipend to her registered relatives in Neo-Seoul.

At the crescendo, as the stadium roar crested in a single synchronized wave, Ryo broke alignment. He dropped his chin, clamped his teeth until his jaw joints ground together, and wrenched the flow inward. Planting his heels hard against the copper plate, he twisted his ankles to sever the grounding trace, pulling the un-buffered current straight into his own bone and adept channels rather than letting it feed the stage capacitors.

The backlash struck in a tight, silent flash under his soles. His spirit conduits pulled taut as the un-filtered mana tore through his ribs, unraveling the bound watcher tethered to his shoulder in a single cold breath. Under his heels, the inductive copper plate blistered and warped with a sharp hiss of melting resin as the synthetic leather of his soles fused against the metal. White arc-light snapped once under the stage deck, tripping the primary stadium breaker across the arena grid. The daylight arrays died. Seventy tons of sub-floor bass cut out mid-note, dropping the eighty-five-thousand-seat dome into sudden, ringing darkness before the panicked murmurs even rose from the dark tiers.

Ryo dropped silently onto one knee on the cooling deck, his lungs locked in a dry spasm while the acrid scent of scorched insulation drifted through the unlit pit. Red emergency beacons pulsed along the catwalks, painting the rising haze in narrow streaks while tactical mages in the wings shouted into dead headsets. Before the perimeter floods could cycle onto backup power, he rolled quietly off the shattered riser and vanished into the debris-filled service trench under the stage deck. Blistered fingers dragged across concrete until they hooked the iron ring of a drainage hatch, hauling the cover clear so he could slip down into the dark.

He dropped into waist-deep brackish seawater and industrial runoff in the storm culvert beneath the arena foundation. The cold hit his scorched skin like a sheet of needles. In the pitch blackness of the sewer, Ryo ripped the smoking Mitsuhama tunic from his shoulders, tearing the embroidered silver piping and letting the oil-slicked water wash away the stage foundation and grey streaks of sweat from his neck. Above him, the distant drone of harbor sirens faded behind thick ferrocrete.

The bound watcher spirit was gone, severed in the grounded surge, and his commlink lay dead in the sludge. Wading against the tide of seawater, palms stinging from fresh electrical burns, Ryo pushed through the subterranean channels toward the container docks at Jurong—nameless, stripped of his corporate skin, and stepping into the shadows on his own terms.
