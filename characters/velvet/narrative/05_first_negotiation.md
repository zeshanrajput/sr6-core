# First Negotiation

Tanaka Ryo kept the unbadged Suzuki idling between his knees in the Redmond alley, the engine's rhythmic vibration rattling loose fairing bolts against the damp steel frame. Across the polarized visor of his Shoei helmet, pirated AR spam flickered through the sulfur drizzle, with glitching advertisements for Stuffer Shack ramen and second-hand cyberware fighting for bandwidth in the local mesh. The exhaust pipe gave off a faint metallic ping as it cooled against the wet air. He cut the ignition with a turn of the key but left the helmet locked down tight over his chin; in the Barrens, bare skin was an invitation to street violence, while features sculpted with corporate symmetry were a magnetic disturbance that drew eyes from across a crowded room.

Inside the *Red Line*, sub-floor bass vibrated through the grease-stained floorboards from a jukebox looping thirty-year-old Neo-Tokyo synth-pop. Still wearing the dark, faceless dome of his riding helmet, he paused near a dead arcade terminal in the corner, shoulders slouched to mimic a delivery courier waiting on a delayed package. In the back booth beneath a flickering Shiawase promotional banner sat the Johnson, wearing a surplus wool overcoat with the frayed stitch scars of a NeoNet security patch cut from the lapel. The man nursed a glass of amber synthetic rye, his eyes darting between the front door and the bar taps with the ingrained nervousness of an ex-corporate middle manager stranded outside the grid.

Through the smoked shield of his visor, Ryo watched him in the scratched reflection of the arcade glass. When a broad-shouldered ork mercenary in armored ballistic plates pushed past the aisle, the fixer’s aura flared with jagged, defensive orange, his right hand twitching toward an illegal pocket holster beneath his coat. Yet when the bar's floor manager stepped over to clear an empty beer bottle, the man’s posture shifted instinctively—his spine straightened, chin dipping into the reflexive deference of a salaryman accustomed to taking directives from executive suites. A masculine runner would trigger his panic and draw a defensive standoff, whereas a corporate woman with clean, icy composure would slide beneath his guard and command the table.

Ryo slipped past the bar toward the narrow corridor leading to the rear storage rooms. The restroom was a damp concrete cell lit by a single buzzing fluorescent tube that threw grey shadows across stained tile, where chill water dripped steadily from an overhead copper pipe into a cracked ceramic urinal. He locked the iron latch, unclipped the Shoei helmet, and set it on the water tank before stripping off his riding leathers. Initiating the shift as his Transys Avalon commlink re-keyed the local PAN registers to broadcast the clean, forged SIN of Lee Ji-yoo, he braced for the biological tax: gripping the porcelain edges of the sink, knuckles whitening as structural magic compressed his ribcage with a grinding ache, vertebra settling against spinal bone while cartilage in his larynx aligned with a crisp click that thinned his breath to a lighter register.

She opened her eyes in the stained mirror, waiting three slow breaths for the momentary vertigo to clear while the bone-latch behind her ears cooled. She smoothed the dark silk shell over her narrowed shoulders, left the riding jacket folded behind the exposed water pipes, and stepped into flat leather shoes. Unlocking the iron door, she walked back into the smoky gloom of the bar with the quiet, acoustic poise of an executive assistant entering a boardroom, slipping into the booth opposite the Johnson with fluid grace. The man looked up, his hand freezing on the rim of his glass as the red neon from the bar sign caught her face—clean, balanced, without a single blemish, scar, or line of street fatigue. His throat moved as he swallowed, fingers slipping off the synthetic rye as his nervous system struggled to recalibrate under the sudden, focused gravity of her presence.

"You're late," he said, setting the glass down twice as carefully as he meant to. Even with his jaw squared, his gaze dropped to her open palms on the laminated booth surface before flicking back to her eyes.

"Knight Errant sweeps were active along the perimeter highway," Ji-yoo said, keeping her vocal pitch just low enough that he had to lean inward by half an inch over the jukebox. "The transit took longer to avoid automated checkpoints."

The ex-manager cleared his throat, wiped a sheen of damp sweat from his palm against his wool trousers, and slid a battered Sony datapad across the cedar laminate. "Renraku storage node in Bellevue," he said. "Ten thousand nuyen total—half up front for operational expenses, the rest on delivery."

Ji-yoo glanced at the encrypted node coordinates on the screen, then rested her hands flat and open on the table. "Eight," she said, letting the single figure sit between them without rushing to fill the air.

The man blinked, his brow furrowing as he leaned back against the cracked vinyl. "What?"

"Eight thousand," she repeated. "And no advance."

He pulled back into the corner of the booth, his eyes narrowing. Street runners fought over every scrip, demanding up-front deposits for ammo, forged credentials, and decker fees; an operative negotiating downward had an unstated agenda. "Runners don't negotiate down," he said, his grip tightening on the table edge. "Who are you working for?"

"Clean retrieval," Ji-yoo said, tilting her chin slightly without raising her voice. "Renraku will not log the intrusion as an external breach. When the datapad is delivered, you owe me a marker—one favor, on demand, within your departmental authority."

The broker sat motionless. His relief at saving corporate operating budget died, replaced by the sobering calculation of an open debt. Nuyen was spent and forgotten; a binding obligation lasted much longer. He studied the poise across the table, the absence of street desperation, and gave a single, tight nod. "One marker," he agreed, sliding the pad fully across the wood. "We have a deal."

Ji-yoo picked up the datapad, slipped it into her coat pocket, and stood. She walked out into the Redmond drizzle, leaving him alone with his synthetic rye beneath the glitching neon.
