# Tea in Tacoma

<audio controls src="audio/08_Tea_in_Tacoma.mp3" style="width: 100%; margin-bottom: 20px;"></audio>

Salt fog off Commencement Bay rolled up the shadowed slope of the Tacoma waterfront, wrapping around the low wooden eaves of *Xingfu Chaguan* and muffling the rhythmic clang of container cranes out in the deep-water harbor. Inside the private rear tea room, the mechanical roar of the industrial sprawl fell away, replaced by the gentle hiss of water reaching a simmer in a cast-iron kettle over a bed of glowing hardwood charcoal. Old-growth cedar panels lined the walls, insulated with acoustic damping and copper mesh that blocked external Matrix feeds, wireless transceivers, and optical surveillance drones. The air carried the dry, earthy fragrance of roasted oolong and warm cedar resin.

Tanaka Ryo sat on a low tatami cushion across from Ni Ni Xiaolu, his posture held in his masculine baseline profile, shoulders square under an unbadged charcoal silk overcoat. Across the cedar surface, Ni Ni Xiaolu sat with immaculate poise in a tailored midnight blazer over ivory silk, her black hair swept up and fastened with antique bone pins. She watched him over the rim of a small celadon cup, the stillness in her frame carrying the hard-won vigilance of an Octagon Triad Red Pole who had survived three separate syndicate purges along the Seattle waterfront.

Between them on the cedar wood, resting precisely six inches from her right hand, lay her customized Ares Predator heavy pistol, its mother-of-pearl grip catching the amber glow of the charcoal burner. Outside the rice-paper sliding door, the broad silhouette of Teddy Chin stood post in the corridor, his cybernetic arms crossed over his chest while the distant harbor foghorn sounded once across the water.

Ni Ni set the clay teapot down with a soft, deliberate click against the cedar tray. “The Cutters have pulled their enforcers back from the West Seattle bridge,” she said, her voice smooth, level, and quiet in the enclosed space. “Bookie’s Ancients agreed to keep their bike patrols east of the river. And Baxter’s bootleg Bliss refinery in Puyallup is cold slag.”

She paused, her gaze fixing on the calm symmetry of his face with sharp, analytical appraisal. “My courier brought back the credstick basket from the noodle cart,” she continued, her tone conversational yet probing. “You left the Bliss capsule on the street.”

“Counterfeits poison the market, Ms. Xiaolu,” Ryo said, his voice resonant and steady. He kept his elbows relaxed at his sides, letting his hands rest visibly beside his saucer. “Yours and mine. An asset who relies on synthetic numbness makes mistakes on the operational perimeter.”

Ni Ni studied him across the rising steam, tracking the steady line of his jaw, the complete absence of nervous micro-tremors, and the quiet focus in his obsidian eyes. Her right hand slid across the polished cedar table, manicured fingers coming to rest lightly over the pearl grip of the heavy Predator. “Who engineered you, Mr. Tanaka?” she asked softly. “Was it Mitsuhama? Or did Wuxing send a bio-sculpted asset to listen through my cedar walls?”

Ryo did not reach for an internal holster, nor did the muscles in his neck tighten into a defensive posture. He deliberately allowed his active presence to settle into neutral stillness, releasing the subtle spell-tension behind his jaw. Reaching out with bare, steady hands, he lifted his celadon cup and took a slow sip of the steaming oolong.

“No corporation sent me to you, Ms. Xiaolu,” Ryo said, setting the cup back onto the cedar tray without making a sound. He met her gaze across the wood, his hands resting open and flat beside his saucer. “I operate in the shadows to pay for my refusal to take directives from corporate boardrooms.”

Silence settled across the cedar-lined room, filled only by the gentle bubbling of the iron kettle and the distant groan of a foghorn out in the bay. Ni Ni’s fingers remained on the pearl weapon grip for three slow, measured breaths. Then her hand withdrew, settling flat in her lap beneath the edge of the lacquer while her shoulders sank slightly, easing the rigid tension along her collarbone. Lifting her teapot with both hands, she poured a fresh stream of amber liquid into his cup.

“The leaves are harvested from old bushes near Ningbo,” she murmured, watching the steam rise against the wood ceiling. Her thumb traced the warm clay handle once before she set the pot back onto its iron trivet. “Smuggled across the Pacific in dry cargo containers without corporate tariffs.”

“It tastes of clean iron and rain,” Ryo said, lifting the warm celadon slightly in unhurried acknowledgment before taking another slow sip. Between them, the heavy pistol remained untouched beside the tea tray, neither weapon nor ornament in the hushed room.

They sat together in the warm, cedar-lined room as the Puget Sound drizzle drummed softly against the roof tiles, drinking un-monetized tea in the quiet space away from the sprawl. When Ryo stood to depart, he left two certified credsticks on the edge of the table for the tea master, bowed with respectful corporate economy, and stepped out into the Tacoma salt fog.
