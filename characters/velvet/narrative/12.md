The exhaust fan in the high transom rattled against its rusted security mesh, drawing the damp, diesel-soaked chill of Touristville down across the linoleum. In the far corner of the clinic, an automated sanitizing unit hummed along its ceiling track, misting empty vinyl examination chairs with an acidic fog of synthetic peroxide. Fluorescent tubes buzzed overhead, casting a sickly green glare over surgical steel trays, sealed biohazard bins, and rows of unlabelled amber vials.

Velvet leaned hard against the stainless-steel counter, her weight braced on locked elbows to keep her trembling knees from buckling. Her body remained trapped inside the compressed, underfed chassis of Sun-hee Diaz: clavicles pulled tight into a human girl's narrow frame, spinal column throbbing where the lumbar vertebrae refused to settle, the salt-stained Shiawase utility jumpsuit stiff with the dried sweat of two hundred and eighty flights of stairs. Every breath dragged through her trachea like crushed glass, burning with the residual ache of an arcology's concrete dead-zone where ambient mana had thinned to an asphyxiating crawl.

She flicked a jagged crumb of conductive glass from her sleeve, watching it click into a stainless kidney dish. Her fingers trembled against the cold metal edge, the skin around her cuticles gray with soot and powdered drywall.

"You could have warned me," she said. The voice came out in Sunny's dry, raspy Cityspeak register, cracked around the edges from breathing recycled algae fumes and hydraulic mist in the intermediate maintenance sumps.

Behind the wash basin, Whiskey tossed a pair of blood-flecked latex gloves into the incinerator chute. She turned the heavy brass taps, letting icy municipal water roar over her raw, scarred knuckles before drying them on a coarse linen towel.

"I did," Whiskey shouted over the drone of the sanitizer track. "Who cuts pharmaceutical acid with Pacific herring oil? I practically tattooed *red herring* across your forehead before you left this clinic. Donovan told me he gave you an easy breach. I figured you'd crack his proving ground in an hour, collect your union scrip, and buy decent street noodles. When twenty-four hours went by and my feed stayed dark, I started clearing an operating table."

"An easy breach?" Velvet's jaw tightened, the bone-latches groaning behind her ears as cartilage strained against the mask. "He walled off the lifts. He turned twenty-five kilometers of vertical firewells into a pressure cooker. By the time we hit the Peachtrees project on floor two hundred, my street samurai was shaking so hard he couldn't steady his katana, my rigger's battery was stone dead, and I had to hand five hundred nuyen of crossroad silver to an Ork gang boss just so we wouldn't die of lactic acidosis on a concrete landing. That was his idea of a vetting?"

"Donovan doesn't build playgrounds for tourists," Whiskey said, leaning her hips against the rim of the porcelain sink. Her dark-ringed eyes swept over the frayed slate-gray canvas of the jumpsuit, cataloging the hollows under Velvet's cheekbones with the flat, clinical appraisal of a veteran street doc. "He was looking for someone who keeps their head when their legs give out. The League of Freelance Assets doesn't back chrome-heads who shoot their way through an apartment block the moment the pressure spikes. If you had dropped a single bystander or left a corpse in that stairwell, you wouldn't be standing in my clinic right now."

The realization landed with a dull, hollow sting in Velvet's chest. She had come to Touristville three days ago trusting Whiskey as an elder of the Conclave, seeking intelligence on a street drug. Whiskey had known about Pyke's trial the entire time, almost certainly bound by mutualist non-disclosure agreements, leaving Velvet with a cryptic riddle about tainted capsules that she had been too frantic to unravel. Even within the mutual aid networks of the shadow guild, every job was an audit.

Whiskey's gaze flickered upward, drifting past Velvet's left ear, and froze. The cynical smirk fell away from her scarred mouth; her fingers curled tight into the linen towel. Her pupils dilated behind her spectacles as she shifted her awareness into the astral plane.

"Anyway," Whiskey murmured, her voice dropping beneath the drone of the ceiling track. "Who's the spirit?"

Velvet looked down at the grease-stained serial numbers stenciled across her chest. "Sun-hee Diaz. Sunny. Eighteen-year-old sump courier on the intermediate floors. Died in a vat-fume blowout last Tuesday. I had to dig her work-chit out of a black-market discard bin to get past the seventh-floor firewall."

"I can see the soul you're wearing," Whiskey said, pointing a single wet finger past Velvet's shoulder. "I'm asking about the entity standing right behind you."

Velvet turned her awareness inward, opening her sight through the astral veil.

The air behind her shimmered with a dense, quiet radiance that cast an iridescent weight across the clinic's mundane linoleum. Standing at the threshold was a towering astral presence, silent and upright, veiled in interlocking ribbons of midnight indigo and burnished bronze. Her form anchored the room like an iron pillar driven through bedrock.

Velvet gripped the edge of the steel counter, her pulse hammering against her ribs. In hermetic and shamanic conjuring, the Rule of the Veil was clear: a summoned spirit departed the moment its service ended or when the sun slipped beneath the horizon. Velvet had invoked an ancestral guide before breaching the arcology, seeking a kindred presence to give somatic weight and emotional depth to Sunny's ghost so the disguise would withstand close inspection.

The sun had set four hours ago, while an unmarked Ares assault helicopter was carrying them down through the rain from the roof of Floor 282. A bound spirit answering a service should have dissolved into ambient mana the instant night fell over Puget Sound.

"It should have vanished at sundown," Velvet whispered, Sunny's cracked voice trembling.

"That thing isn't an errand boy answering a summons," Whiskey said quietly, stepping sideways to keep clear of the threshold. "Look at the posture. It's standing guard over you."

Whiskey reached beneath the hem of her scrub top and pulled a heavy brass key from a paracord loop around her neck. She walked toward the rear of the clinic, bypassing the glass medication cabinets, and stopped before a reinforced steel security door set into the concrete back wall.

"My back room is grounded," Whiskey said, unlocking the dual deadbolts with heavy, authoritative clicks. "Solid cedar beams, copper earthing wire woven through the sill, dried mugwort hanging from the ceiling. Zero electronic feeds, zero monitoring drones."

She pulled the heavy door open, revealing the dim, amber sanctuary within. The scent of dried herbs, cold beeswax candles, and unvarnished timber drifted into the clinical antiseptic air of the triage bay.

"Get inside," Whiskey told her, stepping clear of the opening. "Take off that girl's face before your ribcage splinters. And figure out what you dragged into my clinic before it draws an astral patrol down on both of us."

Velvet stepped over the brass threshold into the lodge, the heavy steel door swinging shut behind her with a dense hydraulic seal that cut off the hum of the sanitizing unit. The quiet inside the sanctum was heavy and tactile, smelling of earthy mugwort and aged cedar. Around the perimeter of the room, polished copper braid was stapled flush against the baseboards, grounding stray ambient mana into the damp Seattle soil beneath the foundation.

Her legs gave way. She caught herself against the edge of a low cedar chest, gasping as the agony in her thoracic cage peaked. With shaking hands, she reached behind her neck, pressing both thumbs into the cranial base to collapse the cosmetic lattice.

The bone-latches released with a series of muffled, wet clicks deep within the mandibular joints. Compressed facial bones expanded outward; her jaw widened into its natural elven contour, and the shortened spine lengthened with an audible shudder that sent waves of cold relief down her lumbar vertebrae. Her trachea broadened, releasing the tight soprano of Sun-hee Diaz as obsidian pigment flooded back across the outer rings of her irises.

He collapsed onto his knees on the polished cedar floorboards as Kim Jin-Young.

He stayed on all fours for a long minute, forehead resting against the cool wood, listening to the ragged rhythm of his own lungs while Jin-Young lifted his head toward the center of the lodge. The dead courier's lingering terror bled away into the floorboards, leaving only the raw, throbbing truth of his own exhausted frame. Beneath the salt-stiffened collar of the Shiawase jumpsuit, the cool nephrite jade and heavy copper of the *MCI-EXP-884-LUNA* collar rested against his collarbone—the weaponized focus Hana had reforged, the debt he had accepted in Amsterdam.

The presence stood before him in the center of the lodge, hovering inches above the cedar planks. Up close, her astral form resolved with crystalline clarity: a Sin-jang, the Divine General of the Musok pantheon, draped in lacquered lamellar plates and deep ceremonial indigo silks. Her face was that of an ancient martial matriarch, carved from seasoned oak and polished iron, unyielding as a fortress gate. Her gaze held the immovable gravity of an elder who had watched generations of hearths burn and rebuilt every single wall by hand.

Jin-Young looked into the spirit's eyes and understood why she had stayed. He had not summoned a servant; he had issued an involuntary calling. Throughout the nightmare ascent of the arcology—refusing to slaughter corporate guards, dragging his comrades through suffocating mana voids, bartering with sacred silver to buy shelter for his exhausted team, absorbing the crushing physical agony of the climb without retreating into cruelty—he had proven the nature of his vessel. The Wise Warrior had not answered a contractual demand; she had recognized an empty vessel (*Bin Geureut*) strong enough to house her sovereign will.

In the cold canals of Amsterdam, Min-seo's parting words had pierced through his ascetic delusions: *She needs teeth, Jin-Young. Stop playing the martyr behind a child's face.*

The Sin-jang extended a single hand, gauntleted in dark bronze lamellar. Her presence radiated the unshakeable discipline of the aegis—the martial backbone, tactical authority, and sovereign iron required to defend a sanctuary against corporate predators. She demanded the steadfast resolve to hold the gate.

"I accept," Jin-Young whispered, his true voice low and steady against the cedar.

He closed his eyes and drew a deep, measured breath down into his diaphragm, centering his awareness against the residual lactic burn in his muscles. The Divine General stepped forward, her astral form passing through his chest as a dense, tempering frost that settled directly into the marrow of his bones. The frantic flutter in his pulse slowed to a deliberate, rhythmic cadence; the throbbing ache behind his temples smoothed into cold, analytical stillness.

Jin-Young pushed himself up from the cedar floorboards and stood on his own feet. The Shiawase jumpsuit was still stained with sweat, and his knuckles were still scraped from the firewells, but his spine was locked with sovereign iron. He adjusted the reforged collar against his chest, turned toward the steel door, and stepped back toward the sprawl, carrying teeth at last.
