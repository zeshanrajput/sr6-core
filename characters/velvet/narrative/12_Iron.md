The exhaust fan in the high transom rattled against its rusted security mesh, dragging the damp, diesel-soaked chill of Touristville down across the linoleum. In the far corner of the clinic, an automated sanitizing unit hummed along its ceiling track, misting empty vinyl examination chairs with an acidic fog of synthetic peroxide. Leaded fluorescent tubes buzzed overhead, casting a jaundiced glare over stainless-steel instrument trays, sealed biohazard bins, and rows of unlabelled amber vials.

Velvet leaned hard against the examination counter, her weight braced on locked elbows to keep her trembling knees from giving out. Her body remained trapped inside the compressed, underfed frame of Sun-hee Diaz: clavicles forced tight into a human girl's narrow skeleton, spinal column throbbing where the lumbar vertebrae refused to settle, the salt-stained Shiawase utility jumpsuit stiff with dried sweat. Every inhalation dragged through her trachea like crushed glass, burning with the residual ache of the arcology's concrete dead-zone where ambient mana had thinned to an asphyxiating crawl.

She reached into the breast pocket of the canvas jumpsuit and pulled out a heavy, encrypted optical chit bearing the embossed seal of the Seattle Shadow Chapters. She slid the plastic across the stainless steel toward the wash basin.

"Donovan got his clean breach," she said. The voice came out in Sunny's dry, raspy Cityspeak register, scraped raw from thirty hours in the intermediate maintenance shafts. "Nobody died in his firewells. We kept our blades sheathed, our guns holstered, and the local gangs took silver instead of lead. But you could have saved me twenty miles of climbing."

Behind the wash basin, Whiskey turned the heavy brass taps, letting icy municipal water roar over her raw, scarred knuckles before drying them on a coarse linen towel. She looked down at the union chit, the corner of her mouth twitching with a faint, tired smile.

"And spoil Donovan's fun?" Whiskey asked over the drone of the sanitizer track. "If you knew the guns were loaded with gel, you would have walked through there like an actor on a soundstage. Pyke needed to see if you stayed human when your knees gave out. Besides, I gave you plenty. Pacific herring oil in pharmaceutical capsules? I practically branded *red herring* across your forehead."

"I was too busy counting stairs to appreciate the pun," Velvet said, a faint, weary chuckle rattling in her chest. She leaned her weight forward against the counter, meeting Whiskey's eyes through the sterile glare. "I need to borrow your back room, Whiskey. I need to ground."

Whiskey leaned against the porcelain basin, studying the salt stains across the jumpsuit and the deep hollows beneath Velvet's cheekbones. Her gaze drifted past Velvet's left ear, her pupils dilating behind her spectacles as she shifted her awareness into the astral plane.

"You brought company," Whiskey murmured, her voice softening into quiet reverence.

"I know," Velvet replied, keeping her palms flat against the steel. "Her weight settled onto my shoulders the moment the chopper lifted off the roof. Sun-hee Diaz was supposed to be a kindred soul to help me carry this disguise. But the sun crossed Puget Sound four hours ago, and she is still standing sentinel over my aura. She needs something still."

Whiskey looked at the dense, pressurized radiance shimmering in the doorway, nodding slowly. "That isn't a common shade, Velvet. That's an elder standing guard. Go into the back. The mugwort is fresh, and the earth wire will keep the sprawl's eyes off you."

Whiskey reached beneath the hem of her scrub top and pulled a heavy brass key from a paracord loop around her neck. She walked toward the rear of the clinic, bypassing the glass medication cabinets, and unlocked the heavy steel door set into the concrete back wall.

"Solid cedar beams, copper earthing wire woven through the sill, dried mugwort hanging from the ceiling," Whiskey said, pulling the reinforced door open. The scent of dried herbs, cold beeswax candles, and unvarnished timber drifted into the clinical antiseptic air of the triage bay.

Velvet stepped over the brass threshold into the lodge, pulled the reinforced door shut, and engaged the hydraulic seal. The sudden silence was deep and sheltering. She stood in the center of the cedar floorboards, bowed her head, and brought both hands to her chest over the stenciled numbers of the Shiawase jumpsuit.

"Thank you, Sun-hee," she whispered in Korean, the words gentle and clear in the quiet. "Thank you for your breath. Thank you for your bones. Rest now. We finished your last climb."

With steady, reverent fingers, she reached behind her neck and pressed both thumbs into the cranial base, releasing the cosmetic lattice. The bone-latches released with a series of soft, wet clicks deep within the mandibular joints. Compressed facial bones eased outward; her jaw widened into its natural elven contour, and the shortened spine lengthened with an audible shudder that sent waves of cool relief down his lumbar vertebrae. His trachea broadened, releasing the tight soprano of the courier as obsidian pigment flooded back across the outer rings of his irises. He knelt on the polished cedar floorboards as Kim Jin-Young.

He rested his palms flat against the cool wood, listening to the ragged rhythm of his own lungs as the courier's lingering terror dissolved into the cedar grain. Beneath the salt-stiffened lapel of the Shiawase jumpsuit, the cool nephrite jade and heavy copper of the *MCI-EXP-884-LUNA* shackle pressed against his collarbone—the weaponized focus Hana had reforged, the debt he had accepted in Amsterdam.

The entity stood in the center of the room, displacing the air until the tallow flames flickered blue and flattened against their wicks. She was draped in soot-blackened lamellar plates and coarse indigo hemp carrying the sharp reek of woodsmoke and old slaughter. Her face was that of an ancestral matriarch carved from weathered oak, her jawline severe as an executioner's block, her eyes dark, flat, and unblinking—the gaze of someone who had watched her settlement burn to ash, who had choked enemy soldiers in the mud with her bare hands to keep her children from starving.

Jin-Young lifted his chin, meeting her pitiless stare. In the vertical slums of the arcology, one hundred and fifty thousand souls lived stacked in concrete pens above the mass graves of the Shutdown. He had crawled through that charnel house for thirty hours, swallowing the agony of the climb and bartering with sacred silver rather than leaving corpses in the dark. He had believed his clean hands were proof of spiritual discipline; he had believed his mercy was an adequate shield.

The ancestral matriarch stepped forward, her movement carrying the crushing momentum of falling stone. She reached down, her callous, spectral hand gripping the front of his jumpsuit and the heavy jade collar beneath it.

The impact tore through Jin-Young's chest with blinding agony. Mitsuhama had spent fifteen years chemically and surgically conditioning his flesh to be soft, compliant, and seductive—an acoustic battery engineered to yield, to smile, to de-escalate, to invite the desires of strangers. Driving ancestral martial iron into that meat was a rusted rebar hammered into unbaked porcelain. His spine arched violently off the floorboards; his jaw clamped shut until his molars groaned, fighting the autonomic reflex to weep, to apologize, to charm his way out of the grip. The entity crushed that cowardice under her weight, burning through the polished veneer of his training until raw marrow met raw iron.

Through the suffocating pressure, Min-seo's parting indictment from the Amsterdam café rang in his ears with brutal clarity: *She needs teeth, Jin-Young. Stop playing the martyr behind a child's face.*

The ancestor drove her thumb deep into the hollow of his right shoulder. Jin-Young’s arm jerked forward in a violent spasm.

His nervous system seized with foreign meat: frozen mud, scorched barley, and children holding their breath behind a wicker screen as a rough-hewn timber door buckled under boots. An intruder’s hand thrust through the splintered latch, clawing for the bolt. Jin-Young’s fist locked around the balance-forward heft of a chipped iron cleaver. His forearm drove down in a practiced arc, burying the edge through the intruder's wrist against the wooden sill. Wet spray coated his knuckles; a dense shudder jarred the radius as iron notched into grain and bone, leaving severed fingers twitching in the dirt.

The vision collapsed, slamming Jin-Young back onto the cedar planks of the clinic lodge. His right hand remained locked in a white-knuckled claw, fingers cramping around empty air, his forearms burning with the phantom recoil of severed gristle. He looked down at his trembling, sculpted fingers. The ancestor survived by waiting behind the rattled latch with butcher's iron.

Jin-Young forced his breathing deep into his diaphragm, centering his focus against the pain. He reached up with his left hand, locking his fingers firmly around the ancestor's spectral wrist. With his right hand, he caught the heavy nephrite jade and copper band of Hana's collar, flooding the focus with his own Awakened mana until the stone flared with cold, resolute green light.

"I know what the threshold costs," Jin-Young whispered, his true voice ringing steady against the cedar beams. "Enter and be welcome." He pulled. He dragged the ancient, blood-stained weight of the matriarch straight into his sternum on his own authority.

The presence locked into his marrow with the permanent, structural force of cast iron. The frantic flutter in his pulse slowed to a heavy, resonant thud; the manufactured softness behind his temples hardened into unforgiving stillness.

Jin-Young pushed himself up from the floorboards and stood on his own feet. The Shiawase jumpsuit was still stained with sweat, and his knuckles were still scraped raw from the firewells, but his spine was locked with sovereign iron. He adjusted the reforged collar against his chest, turned toward the steel door, and stepped back toward the sprawl, carrying teeth at last.
