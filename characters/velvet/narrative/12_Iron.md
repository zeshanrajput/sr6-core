The exhaust fan in the high transom rattled against its rusted security mesh, dragging the damp, diesel-soaked chill of Touristville down across the linoleum. In the far corner of the clinic, an automated sanitizing unit hummed along its ceiling track, misting empty vinyl examination chairs with an acidic fog of synthetic peroxide. Leaded fluorescent tubes buzzed overhead, casting a jaundiced glare over stainless-steel instrument trays, sealed biohazard bins, and rows of unlabelled amber vials.

Velvet leaned hard against the examination counter, her weight braced on locked elbows to keep her trembling knees from giving out. Her body remained trapped inside the compressed, underfed frame of Sun-hee Diaz: clavicles forced tight into a human girl's narrow skeleton, spinal column throbbing where the lumbar vertebrae refused to settle, the salt-stained Shiawase utility jumpsuit stiff with dried sweat. Every inhalation dragged through her trachea like crushed glass, burning with the residual ache of the arcology's concrete dead-zone where ambient mana had thinned to an asphyxiating crawl.

She reached into the breast pocket of the canvas jumpsuit, pulled out a heavy, encrypted optical chit bearing the embossed seal of the Seattle Shadow Chapters, and slid the plastic across the stainless steel toward the wash basin. "Donovan got his clean breach," she said, the syllables scraping raw from Sunny's narrow throat after thirty hours in the intermediate maintenance shafts. "Nobody died in his firewells. We kept our blades sheathed, our guns holstered, and the local gangs took silver instead of lead—though you could have saved me twenty miles of climbing."

Behind the basin, Whiskey turned the heavy brass taps, letting icy municipal water roar over her raw, scarred knuckles before drying them on a coarse linen towel. She looked down at the union chit, the corner of her mouth twitching with a faint, tired smile. "And spoil Donovan's fun?" she asked over the drone of the sanitizer track. "If you knew the guns were loaded with gel, you would have walked through there like an actor on a soundstage. Pyke needed to see if you stayed human when your knees gave out. Besides, I gave you plenty—pickled fish oil in crimson pharmaceutical capsules? I practically branded *red herring* across your forehead."

"I was too busy counting stairs to appreciate the pun," Velvet said, a faint, weary chuckle rattling in her chest. She leaned her weight forward against the counter, meeting the street doc's eyes through the sterile glare. "I need to borrow your back room. I need to ground."

Whiskey leaned against the porcelain rim, studying the salt stains across the jumpsuit and the deep hollows beneath Velvet's cheekbones. Her gaze drifted past Velvet's left ear, pupils dilating behind her spectacles as she shifted her awareness into the astral plane. "You brought company," she murmured, her voice softening into quiet reverence.

"I know," Velvet replied, keeping her palms flat against the steel. "Her weight settled onto my shoulders the moment the chopper lifted off the roof. Sun-hee Diaz was supposed to be a kindred soul to help me carry this disguise. Yet the sun crossed Puget Sound four hours ago, and she remains sentinel over my aura. She needs an anchor."

Whiskey looked at the dense, pressurized radiance shimmering in the doorway, nodding slowly. "That isn't a common shade, Velvet. That's an elder standing guard. Go into the back. The mugwort is fresh, and the earth wire will keep the sprawl's eyes off you."

Reaching beneath the hem of her scrub top, the doc pulled a heavy brass key from a paracord loop around her neck, walked past the glass medication cabinets, and unlocked the steel door set into the concrete back wall. "Solid cedar beams, copper earthing wire woven through the sill, hung mugwort bundles from the ceiling," she said, pulling the heavy barrier open so the scent of dry herbs, cold beeswax candles, and unvarnished timber drifted into the antiseptic triage bay.

Velvet stepped over the brass threshold into the lodge, pulled the steel door shut, and engaged the hydraulic seal. The sudden silence was deep and sheltering. Standing in the center of the cedar floorboards, she bowed her head, brought both hands to her chest over the stenciled numbers of the Shiawase jumpsuit, and whispered in Korean: "Thank you, Sun-hee. I honor your breath and your bones. Rest now. We finished your last climb."

With steady, reverent fingers, she reached behind her neck and pressed both thumbs into the cranial base, releasing the cosmetic lattice. The bone-latches released with a series of soft, wet clicks deep within the mandibular joints. Compressed facial bones eased outward; her jaw widened into its natural elven contour, and the shortened spine lengthened with an audible shudder that sent waves of cool relief down his lumbar vertebrae. His trachea broadened, releasing the tight soprano of the courier as obsidian pigment flooded back across the outer rings of his irises. He knelt on the polished cedar floorboards as Kim Jin-Young.

He rested his palms flat against the cool wood, listening to the ragged rhythm of his own lungs as the courier's lingering terror dissolved into the cedar grain. Beneath the salt-stiffened lapel of the Shiawase jumpsuit, the cool nephrite jade and heavy copper of the *MCI-EXP-884-LUNA* shackle pressed against his collarbone—the weaponized focus Hana had reforged, the debt he had accepted in Amsterdam.

The entity stood in the center of the room, displacing the air until the tallow flames flickered blue and flattened against their wicks. She was draped in soot-blackened lamellar plates and coarse indigo hemp carrying the sharp reek of woodsmoke and old slaughter. Her face was that of an ancestral matriarch carved from weathered oak, her jawline severe as an executioner's block, her eyes dark, flat, and unblinking—the gaze of someone who had watched her settlement burn to ash, who had choked enemy soldiers in the mud with her bare hands to keep her children from starving.

Jin-Young lifted his chin, meeting her pitiless stare. In the vertical slums of the arcology, one hundred and fifty thousand souls lived stacked in concrete pens above the mass graves of the Shutdown. He had crawled through that charnel house for thirty hours, swallowing the agony of the climb and bartering with sacred silver rather than leaving corpses in the dark. He had trusted clean hands as proof of spiritual discipline, mistaking mercy alone for an adequate shield.

The ancestral matriarch stepped forward with the steady, unyielding gravity of stone settling onto bedrock. She reached down, her callous, spectral hand gripping the front of his jumpsuit and the heavy jade collar beneath it. The weight struck his sternum like cold metal. Mitsuhama had spent fifteen years chemically and surgically conditioning his flesh to be soft, compliant, and disarming—an acoustic vessel tuned to yield, smile, and de-escalate under pressure. Hosting ancestral steel inside that frame felt like driving an unyielding spine through porcelain. His back arched off the floorboards and his molars clamped shut against the autonomic reflex to soften, apologize, or charm his way out of the grip, until the polished veneer gave way beneath the quiet pressure of her will.

Through that stillness, Min-seo's parting indictment from the Amsterdam café rang clear in his memory: *She needs teeth, Jin-Young. Stop playing the martyr behind a child's face.*

The ancestor pressed her thumb deep into the hollow of his right shoulder, and his arm snapped upright in a clean, involuntary reflex. His nervous system filled with foreign winter: frozen mud, scorched barley, and children holding their breath behind a wicker screen as a rough-hewn timber door buckled under boots. An intruder’s hand thrust through the splintered latch, clawing for the bolt. Jin-Young’s grip closed around the balance-forward heft of a chipped cleaver, his forearm dropping in one practiced arc to sever the wrist against the sill. A dense shudder jarred his radius as the blade bit cleanly into seasoned oak, ending the breach without a shout.

The vision collapsed, returning him to the cedar planks of the clinic lodge. His right hand remained clenched around empty air, forearm steady as the phantom recoil faded from the tendon. He looked down at his pale, sculpted knuckles: the ancestor had not survived by screaming or chasing enemies down the road, only by holding the latch with quiet steel when the door broke.

He forced his breathing deep into his diaphragm, centering his focus against the lingering ache. Reaching up with his left hand, he closed his palm firmly around the ancestor's spectral wrist while his right caught the heavy nephrite jade and copper band of Hana's collar, flooding the focus with Awakened mana until the stone flared with cold, resolute green light.

"I know what the threshold costs," Jin-Young whispered, his true voice ringing steady against the cedar beams. "Enter and be welcome." He pulled—drawing the ancient, unyielding weight of the matriarch straight into his sternum on his own authority.

The presence locked into his marrow with the permanent, structural quiet of cast steel. The frantic flutter in his pulse slowed to a heavy, measured thud; the manufactured softness behind his temples settled into unforgiving stillness. Pushing himself up from the floorboards onto his own feet, he smoothed the salt-stained Shiawase jumpsuit over the reforged collar at his chest, turned toward the door, and stepped back toward the sprawl—carrying teeth at last.
