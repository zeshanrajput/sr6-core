# Consensus

The last of Travis Fontenot left Jin-Young’s throat with a wet, stubborn click of cartilage.

In the private concourse washroom, he braced both palms against the marble basin and waited for his ribs to finish shortening. The thick-necked roughneck shell, broad across the trapezius and sun-leathered at the nape with a gravel-pit drawl in the vocal cords, dissolved back into fascia and bone memory. Cold tap water beaded along the sharper, narrower jawline of Tanaka Ryo. He blotted his wrists with a linen towel, straightened his charcoal cuffs, and caught the faint, clinging ghost of Tarnished Star solvent near his left wristbone: industrial degreaser and bayou silt that hadn't quite washed clear of the seam.

He didn't scrub it. A man in transit from a storm-struck parish was allowed a trace of the world outside the glass.

The frosted door of the Baton Rouge Metropolitan Airport VIP lounge hissed shut behind him, trading the antiseptic chill of the washroom for climate-controlled stillness. Beyond the floor-to-ceiling windows, Hurricane Lorenzo’s bruised cloud deck had torn apart. Harsh, bleached morning daylight poured across the tarmac, turning puddles into blinding mirrors between nose-to-tail rows of heavy-lift relief tiltrotors, municipal medevac birds, and sleek corporate jets waiting for airspace clearance. Inside, the lounge smelled of ozone filtration and dark roast, with no neon, no booth shadows, and no fixer squinting through synth-smoke.

Aiden Vance sat in one of the pale leather armchairs near the glass, framed against the glare of the runway like a man who had bought the weather. He wore Horizon’s preferred cut: unremarkable slate wool, no visible chrome, and a Dawkins Group lapel pin small enough to miss unless you were trained to look for it. On the low travertine table in front of him sat two white porcelain cups, steam thinning from both rims in the conditioned draft.

Velvet let Tanaka Ryo’s posture settle over his aching collarbones. It was easy, unhurried, and built as the operational armor of a regional logistics consultant with clean SINs and a boarding pass in his pocket. He kept his own presence leashed tight behind his ribs, pulling the engineered gravity inward so the air around him stayed flat and ordinary.

Vance’s aura barely rippled as Ryo approached: no spike of adrenaline, no defensive static, only the polished, unreadable gray of a Senior Field Evaluator who weighed human assets the way actuaries priced flood tables. His eyes rose from a slate tablet, tracking Ryo’s stride with quiet, unblinking precision. For half a beat, his gaze lingered near the left cuff, where the faint chemical ghost of the Tarnished Star yard still clung, before lifting to meet Ryo’s eyes with practiced boardroom warmth.

"Mr. Tanaka," Vance said, his voice pitched just above the hum of the ventilation, smooth as a press release. "I appreciate you making the window. The storm left half the regional grid looking like wet cardboard."

"Flights are clearing faster than the highways," Ryo replied, keeping his cadence clipped and polite with the gravel of Travis Fontenot buried three layers deep. He crossed the last two paces of carpet as Vance gestured toward the second chair, ignored the lingering pull along his sternum, and took his seat across from him.

Vance didn't speak right away. He let three seconds pass, matching his own inhale to the rhythm of Ryo’s ribs in a textbook pacing trick, while a faint, warm veil of synthetic cedar and skin-musk drifted across the travertine table from a micro-diffuser hidden in his watchband or lapel. On an ordinary mark, it would have softened the shoulders and loosened the tongue. To Jin-Young, whose own glands had been sculpted in a Mitsuhama cleanroom to bend human nervous systems, it was like watching an amateur card sharp flash a palmed queen in noon sunlight.

Vance tapped his slate once. The matte glass woke to a clean Dawkins Group sentiment array, quiet ribbons of slate-blue and amber calibrated to read pupil dilation, blink cadence, and vocal micro-tremor. He didn't angle it away. Showing the instrument was half the squeeze.

"You have an interesting footprint, Mr. Tanaka," Vance said, folding his hands with unhurried sympathy. "Clean transit records, impeccable local compliance. Which is why two adjacent anomalies caught our editorial desk's attention this morning." He slid a finger across the glass, pulling up a split feed. "First, a lifestyle story out of the Gulf. Aurelian Design’s star couturier, Belle Moderne, just filed an unscheduled retirement, effective immediately, on the exact night a derelict UniOil platform out past the delta nearly blew its stack under Category 2 winds." Vance’s tone stayed light, almost conversational, though his aura remained flat as wet concrete. "The Coast Guard logs are even stranger. A civilian skiff pulled eight terrified Aurelian security contractors out of an inflatable RIB a mile from that rig. Every storm buoy in the sector clocked eighty-knot gusts, yet the patch of water around their boat was glass-calm. Like someone ironed the Gulf flat for twenty minutes."

Ryo kept his hands still on his knees, his face a polite, attentive mask.

"The second anomaly is a supply-chain echo," Vance continued, his voice dropping half a step into confidential warmth. "Two kilos of uncut Colombian novacoke, chemically tagged to an Aurelian black-account vault, just surfaced in Fernand Amato’s Kozlowski Mafia distribution pipeline. That kind of cross-column jump creates a clear signal in our predictive algorithms. You can bribe a precinct captain to lose a paper file, Mr. Tanaka, or pay a street decker to scrub a local node. You cannot scrub a pattern out of Horizon’s Consensus lattice once the feeds start correlating."

He nudged one of the white porcelain cups an inch closer to Ryo’s side of the table and leaned back, his breathing still synchronized, his eyes tracking the quiet amber bar on his slate. "Consensus doesn't exist to punish independent operators," he said. "It exists to curate outcomes. Horizon can smooth that predictive curve: reclassify the Coast Guard telemetry as sensor drift, bury the Kozlowski batch inside routine hurricane-relief spoilage, and keep your transit records pristine. In exchange, we retain you as an off-book cultural subcontractor. Light touch. Narrative alignment when regional assets get noisy."

Ryo lifted the porcelain, took a single unhurried sip, and set it back on the saucer without a tremor or a chime of ceramic.

Inside his chest, something old and fractured had gone quiet. For years, his sculpted flesh and the ancestral court had shared his ribs like a wary tenant and a demanding landlord, always negotiating rent and always bracing against the seam. Watching Belle Moderne hollow herself out on that rotting Gulf platform, treating her own body like a disposable mannequin and spirits like engines for toxic sludge, had burned the last of that division away. Breath, bone, and the Wise Warrior’s quiet steel now locked into a single, unbroken circuit—structural and unshakeable, even in the harsh glare of a corporate transit lounge.

He didn't lean forward. He let the polite warmth drain out of his voice until only clean geometry remained.

"Ninety-two degrees," Ryo observed, nodding toward the cup. "Hot enough to read as hospitality, cool enough to finish inside a three-minute boarding window. Straight out of the Dawkins field handbook. Along with the cedar diffuser under your lapel, the three-second empathetic tilt before you name the price, and the quarter-second pause while your sub-vocal prompt feeds you the next line."

Vance’s blink cadence didn't falter, though his thumb stilled against the bezel of his slate. "You're reading the room well, Mr. Tanaka," he murmured. "That's precisely why we think you'd be useful."

"You're reading the wrong ledger, Mr. Vance." Ryo kept his tone conversational, stripping every ounce of engineered gravity from the space between them so Vance had nothing to lean against. "Your Consensus lattice flagged the Coast Guard log because your models only understand zero-sum greed. Consensus can price a cartel drop, a precinct scrub, or a corporate hit. It goes blind the moment a crew spends its own margin to flatten eighty-knot water for eight enemy guards who have nothing left on the table. You can't model mercy when your entire predictive engine runs on appetite."

A brief, hair-thin shadow of irritation flickered along the edge of Vance's gray aura. "Anomaly or not, the telemetry still points to you."

"And you aren't going to publish a single frame of it," Ryo said, his voice dropping to a near-whisper that cut beneath the lounge's air scrubbers. "Think through the second-order sequelae. If Horizon leaks Site Laputa or those tagged kilos, you don't burn a regional consultant. You put Aurelian Design’s flagship couturier on every evening feed dumping hazardous waste in Cancer Alley, conducting a Great Form Pollution ritual on a derelict rig, and paying off eco-radicals with corporate-stamped duffel bags of uncut Colombian novacoke."

Ryo rested his forearms on his knees, the faint scent of Tarnished Star solvent rising from his left cuff like a signature.

"Aurelian buys half your luxury lifestyle ad-space on the West Coast," Ryo continued. "Expose them, and your own Gulf relief sponsors eat the shrapnel before the ink dries on the morning brief. You don't have a blackmail file, Mr. Vance. You have a containment problem, and you're hoping I'm polite enough to sign a retainer to help you mop it up."

Across the low travertine table, Vance stopped matching his breathing. His fingers locked motionless over the edge of the slate, his practiced boardroom warmth freezing into hollow, lacquered stillness as the quiet amber bar on his screen flattened to a dead, unreadable gray.

Ryo let the silence hold for two heartbeats, then offered the clean way out.

"Here is what you can file to Los Angeles," Ryo said, his voice returning to the polite, even cadence of a man discussing quarterly logistics. "Belle Moderne’s toxic ritual circle on that rig is permanently dead. It won't reopen under another name. The eight surviving Aurelian guards reached shore intact, and they are terrified enough of the Gulf, and grateful enough to be breathing, to honor their NDAs to the letter. Aurelian’s Q3 valuation holds, Horizon’s Gulf relief sponsors keep their halo, and you look like a field evaluator who neutralized a catastrophic brand leak before noon."

Vance’s eyes didn't leave Ryo’s face. "And the Kozlowski ledger?"

"Fernand Amato’s distribution stays off Horizon’s board," Ryo replied. "No predictive flags, no quiet editorial nudges, no curiosity from the Dawkins desk. We both walk away with what we came for."

For a long second, Vance weighed the ledger in his head: the cost of a bruised ego against a career-making containment report. Dawkins Group men never signed off-book truces in ink, and he didn't offer a handshake. Instead, he inclined his head a fraction of an inch and tapped his slate once, killing the telemetry display until the glass went black.

"A clean consensus, then," Vance said.

"The cleanest kind," Ryo answered. He rose, buttoning his charcoal jacket over the unhurried stillness in his chest, his left cuff brushing the edge of the table with only the faintest memory of Tarnished Star solvent. "Have a safe flight back to the coast, Mr. Vance."

The frosted glass hissed open, and Ryo stepped back into the bright, chaotic concourse. He blended into the stream of storm-delayed travelers, relief coordinators barking into commlinks, and rolling luggage, Tanaka Ryo’s operational armor intact down to the crease in his sleeve.
