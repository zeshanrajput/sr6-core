# Lagniappe

Hurricane Lorenzo's outer rain bands lashed the cypress shutters of *The Gnome's Groove*, rattling the tin awning while Lanyap kept the backroom lamps turned low. Tucked into the corner booth, the team had already broken open their celebration. Runners traded hoarse jokes over flat beer while several leaned in for a razor-thin taste of uncut Colombian, savoring the first pinch before the rest of the haul boxed up for Fernand Amato to move. Laughter and smoky heat spilled across the vinyl bench, their spirit ribbons flaring jagged orange above the table's glare.

Velvet occupied the end chair, holding Travis Fontenot's broad-shouldered frame against the pull of tight fascia and heavy bone. His oil-stained coveralls hung open over a faded Tombstones Urban Brawl shirt, steel-toe boots planted square on the grit floor. Old refinery chemical scars traced his thickened forearms, and a faint ghost of solvent clung near his left cuff. Velvet let the folded bill pass him twice without touching the powder. Instead, he kept one calloused palm wrapped around a heavy, dark-glassed bottle of local stout, lifting it on cue with the booth's toasts so the opaque brown glass hid how little he had actually drunk over the past hour.

Across the wet wood, Roanoke stayed clear of the novacoke too. Unbleached cotton and coarse natural linen hung loosely from her shoulders with the cuffs rolled back, exposing deep, genuine chemical scarring from toxic cleanup sites along both hands and forearms. Before the party peaked, Velvet pulled a fresh cocktail napkin from the brass holder, clicked open a plastic ballpoint, and wrote his commcode in blunt print above three words: *At your service.* He slid the paper past overlapping condensation rings to stop near her elbow, then stood early to slip out.

He stepped into the narrow vestibule, letting the inner door swing shut behind him until the jukebox dropped to a dull throb and rainwater hissed under the street threshold. Roanoke followed him away from the booth and leaned one shoulder against the peeling paint. Her expression spoke of amusement, clearly half-expecting a smooth operator's late-night move.

Velvet let the practiced charm fall away, settling back into the tired roughneck's stance beneath the buzzing doorway bulb. "I didn't mean to pull you away," he said, pitching his voice low against the wind hammering the outer shutters. "There's a dilapidated levee dive back in Destrehan called *Boomerang*, out in Cancer Alley. I've got debts to settle before the storm hits."

Roanoke's crooked smile faded. Her scarred thumb pressed once against the folded napkin tucked into her pocket. "You're running back up the river into Cancer Alley ahead of Lorenzo?"

"Before the roads wash out."

She studied his weathered jaw, sudden curiosity brightening the edge of her aura despite the storm rattling the glass behind him, then reached for her canvas jacket on the wall hook. "Wait up. Can I come with you?"

---

Upriver in Destrehan, *Boomerang* squatted alone on the raised riverbank berm beside the dark silhouettes of the refinery tank farms, a single-story dive leaning into the rising Gulf gusts. Inside, their rain-slick boots left muddy tracks across a stainless-steel dance floor warped like sheet metal from past floods. Sputtering beer promos washed the peeling wallboard in cold blue static while crooked neon buzzed above the bar, filling the low room with the sour-sweet reek of petrochemical rain and stale draft. Roanoke shook water from her canvas jacket inside the doorway, eyes sweeping the vacant booths and upturned barstools stacked for the storm.

Only one corner table held company. Boudreaux and Thibodeaux hunched over dry cardboard coasters, caked to the knees in bayou silt, their auras hollowed out like men who had walked too far after the engines died.

Travis Fontenot's steel-toe boots thudded softly over the buckled floor as Velvet crossed to the counter, oil-stained coveralls darkened at the hem with river rain and tight across his borrowed shoulders. The drowsy barkeep barely glanced up from wiping tarnished brass taps. Velvet pulled folded cash from his coverall pocket and ordered two rounds of decent whiskey. The barkeep eyed the roughneck's grease-smudged knuckles, shrugged, reached below the bar for a corked bottle, and poured four heavy glasses.

Velvet carried the tumblers back two in each fist, keeping the amber liquor level while Roanoke followed a half-step behind his shoulder. At the corner table, Boudreaux's bruised fingers lay slack on the chipped veneer while Thibodeaux stared down into a cracked ashtray, his spirit ribbons frayed pale as dry salt. Velvet set two glasses within reach of the living men, close enough for the warm oak scent to break through the stale malt haze. Then he wiped a ring clean with his sleeve and placed the other two tumblers side by side on the wet wood, facing the empty chairs across from them. He squared the untouched rims along the table's grain, let his knuckles rest beside the glass for one slow breath, and stepped back.

Slowly, Boudreaux raised his heavy chin, lifting bloodshot eyes from the two untouched pours to Travis and Roanoke standing beside the glasses.

Velvet rested one broad palm on the back of a spare chair. "Roanoke, meet Boudreaux and Thibodeaux. They ran Belle Moderne's haul out to the bayou this afternoon."

Roanoke's spine snapped upright. Her volatile GreenWar temper flared sharp green-white across her spirit ribbons as her chemically scarred hands curled onto the table edge. "You're the crew that dumped thirty drums of poison in the marsh?"

Boudreaux dropped his eyes to his bruised knuckles, too hollowed out by grief to trade heat. "Thirty thin drums," he muttered. "Didn't haul them for sport, ma'am."

Velvet stepped one boot-length into the narrow space between Roanoke's corded wrist and the corner table. No seam showed between Travis Fontenot's broad roughneck posture and the shaman's deep stillness anchoring his spine.

"They drew the short straw," Velvet told her, keeping his tone level beneath the buzz of the beer signs. "Moderne paid by the load, held their rent over their heads, and sent them into the swamp with cheap seals. They already paid for that mistake in blood tonight." He shifted his eyes to the two riggers. "I'm sorry about the check you won't be seeing. Aurelian Design won't be cutting another dime after tonight. Belle Moderne is out of business for good."

Thibodeaux let out a ragged breath through his teeth, his jaw clenching once before going slack again. Neither man reached for iron or argued the point.

Velvet angled one shoulder back toward Roanoke, keeping both palms open on the chair back. "Those thirty barrels are still sitting in soft mud ahead of a Category Two surge. If the levees top, every cheap bung leaks straight into the parish water table before dawn. You need drivers who can run those back roads and find the drop point in the dark."

Over a long, weighted minute the anger in Roanoke's posture banked into grim practicality. Her scarred fingers uncurled from the table rim. Earlier that day, Velvet's crew had handed the captured GMC Grizzly and Ares Roadmaster over to her as salvage.

"The Grizzly and the Roadmaster are parked dry under a tarp three miles upriver," Roanoke said, measuring each word. "Pull those thirty drums back out before Lorenzo floods the track, and I'll put your own truck and flatbed rig right back under you tonight as an advance on the cleanup contract."

Gulf rain drumming the tin roof measured the hush between them. Thibodeaux wrapped his silt-stained fingers around one of the full glasses before him. He lifted the whiskey an inch toward the empty chair opposite his own, swallowed it neat in one hard pull, and set the glass down dry.

Boudreaux gave one slow, pragmatic nod over the wet wood. "Send the pin," he muttered, tapping his commlink on the tabletop. "We'll get the drums."

Velvet nudged the spare chair squarely back against the table, leaving the two memorial glasses sitting untouched for the men left in the swamp.

---

Outside *Boomerang*, the screen door slapped shut behind them, leaving the two tired riggers and the four glasses at the corner table. Velvet and Roanoke stood beneath a rusted tin awning on the gravel apron while warm, heavy gusts drove rain across the levee road and rattled the faded beer sign. Roanoke slipped her hands into her pockets, her scarred thumb catching on the corner of the cocktail napkin tucked into the coarse linen as she looked up at him, the earlier amusement from Lanyap's vestibule gone.

A single amber sodium lamp buzzed over the side door, catching the tight, pale chemical scars permanently crosshatching Roanoke's wrists and forearms from real cleanup sites. Velvet paused under the dripping eaves and caught three drops of clean Gulf rainwater on his fingertips, flicking them onto the gravel in a quiet threshold offering before pressing two fingers to the oil-stained breast pocket over his heart. "The drums are coming out of the marsh, Travis," he murmured, his voice pitched low for the man whose weight he carried. "The knot is untied. Go on home."

Velvet reached behind his nape and pressed lightly along the base of his skull to release Travis's bone-latches. Cartilage reset behind his jaw and along his collarbones with quiet clicks. Metallic heat prickled under his tongue as the broad trapezius and sun-leathered neck pulled inward, folding back inside the stiff coveralls. The roughneck rasp drained from his throat in one dry swallow. Along his wrists, the raised chemical ridges smoothed away, leaving clean skin above the grease-darkened cuffs as the coveralls hung a half-size looser across Tanaka Ryo's narrower shoulders and composed features.

Roanoke stood her ground under the awning without flinching, though her spirit ribbons held motionless in the sodium glare.

"Those scars on his arms weren't mine," Ryo said, his voice lighter and unhurried against the drumming tin roof. "They belonged to a Destrehan roughneck who didn't walk out of the last chemical spill. Tonight was his debt to collect. When you dial the number on that napkin, this is the one who picks up, even if I'm less to your taste than he was."

Roanoke's breath caught against the rain. In the astral, the brittle green-and-rust shell around her heart splintered open, flooding her spirit ribbons with a raw, aching swell of deep cypress gold and violet. She stepped across the narrow dry space under the awning and closed her scarred fingers around Ryo's bare wrist, holding tight against the warm, smooth skin above the stained cuff.

Velvet kept his own engineered gravity leashed behind his ribs, leaving the air between them flat and unadorned. The tremor in her grip and the sudden, fierce warmth radiating off her aura belonged entirely to her.

"You brought him back off the river," she said, the brisk Johnson cadence gone, her voice thick and unsteady beneath the drumming eaves. She held on for one long beat, as if anchoring both the dead roughneck and the living man wearing his coveralls, then let go and turned her canvas collar high to shield her wet eyes from the sodium glare. "Stay dry ahead of Lorenzo, Ryo."

She crossed the puddled gravel toward the cab sweeping onto the berm, while Ryo lingered one breath under the humming light before stepping out into the rain.
