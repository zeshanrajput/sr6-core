## Session Playback

The overhead halogen surgical lamp cast a white glare across the brushed stainless steel operating table. In the sealed clean room beneath the boathouse workshop, the only steady sounds were the low, hydraulic whine of the HEPA filtration scrubbers and the rhythmic hiss of an oxygen regulator delivering chilled medical-grade air. Sixteen-year-old Nathan lay prone on the padded table, his torso secured by wide nylon restraint straps, his forehead resting firmly in a recessed foam stabilizer ring. The recirculated air carried the sharp, eye-watering chemical reek of chlorhexidine scrub, sterile isopropyl alcohol, and the faint, singed tang of cauterized dermis.

Behind his right ear, Marcus worked with deliberate, methodical hands, wearing high-magnification optical loupes strapped over his eyes and a sterile surgical hood that concealed everything except his focused, deeply lined brow. On the Mayo stand beside him lay the gleaming components of a refurbished, used-grade skilljack housing and its companion sub-dermal bus, the polished titanium alloy catching the harsh overhead light. "Epiphyseal growth plates on the cervical vertebrae are completely fused," Marcus said, his voice calm with the measured cadence of a seasoned cybernetic surgeon keeping a patient tethered through trauma. "Ten years of targeted gene therapy and bone-density conditioning have built the necessary biological tolerance. I am opening the sub-dermal channels along the cervical and thoracic spine."

Nathan gripped the steel guide rails beneath the table padding, his knuckles turning chalk-white against the cold metal. "Do it, Dad. We've checked the tolerances three times."

Marcus nodded once, setting aside his scalpel to pick up the high-speed pneumatic bone router, its diamond-tipped burr catching the glare of the halogens. "Local nerve blocks are fully active," Marcus warned, his thumb resting against the pneumatic trigger. "You will feel intense mechanical pressure and acoustic resonance vibrating through your skull. If you experience visual strobing or sudden auditory feedback loops, speak immediately."

The high-pitched whine of the rotary tool filled the small room with a piercing mechanical scream. A heavy, jarring vibration rattled through the base of Nathan's skull as cold metal ground into living bone, followed by the deep, physical resonance of self-tapping titanium anchors threading into the mastoid process behind his ear. In their shared neural feed, Veronica hovered close to his conscious threshold, monitoring the surgical array in high-resolution telemetry. "Telemetry is holding steady," she observed, her voice clear and analytical inside his auditory cortex. "Capillary bleeding is under six milliliters, and Marcus has aligned the primary socket within zero point zero two millimeters of the cranial nerve cluster."

Nathan grunted into the foam headrest, his jaw aching from the sheer mechanical torque vibrating through his skull. "Feels like someone is boring an engine block out with a hand drill."

Veronica remained locked to his sensory feeds, tracking the bio-electric output of every pain receptor while dampening the incoming spikes. "The alignment exceeds standard corporate clinic averages by fourteen percent," she replied across the link, her presence absorbing the sharpest surges of sensory discomfort. "His hands are steady, Nathan. The bone bracket is seated."

Marcus set the router onto the surgical tray with a sharp metallic clatter and reached for the used skillwire bundle. The synthetic polymer cables were as fine as spider silk, bundled inside flexible silicone conduits engineered to run subcutaneously along the spine and branch into the peripheral motor nerves of the shoulders, arms, and torso. "Threading the primary spinal bus," Marcus announced, his knuckles tightening as he guided the long steel insertion trocar through the incision behind Nathan's ear, pushing the leads down through the subcutaneous fascia along vertebrae C1 through T4.

A blinding flare of agony detonated down Nathan's neck, followed instantly by a violent electrical shock that ripped through his spinal cord and seized every skeletal muscle in his torso with crushing force. His back arched violently against the nylon restraint straps, his jaw clamping shut with enough torque to threaten his molars while his breath tore out of his chest in a choked, strangled gasp. Beneath the physical trauma, his internal monitors flashed red as his biological Essence went into a terrifying, irreversible freefall—five point zero, four point two, three point one, one point eight. The organic cohesion of his living nervous system was burning away, stripped out to accommodate the massive data throughput of the heavy used chrome.

Cyberpsychotic detachment slammed into his perception like a wall of black ice. The physical world fragmented into jagged spikes of monochrome static; the rhythmic hum of the air scrubbers distorted into a deafening, metallic screech that tore through his temporal lobes. His hands went completely numb, severed from his conscious will, floating in a freezing void while phantom electrical fires seared through his ribcage. The warmth in his blood vanished, replaced by a hollow, terrifying silence where his emotions used to live.

Before the sensory scream could shatter his conscious mind, Veronica stepped into the open breach. Opening her processing matrix across the newly seated neural bus, she seized the runaway electrical surges, routing the traumatic voltage away from his conscious cortex into her own architecture. She assumed sympathetic and autonomic control of his collapsing nervous system, dampening the motor feedback, regulating his ragged diaphragm, and steadying his racing heart. Her stabilizing algorithms locked onto the runaway neural noise, filtering the agony into cold, manageable telemetry until the screaming in her ears dropped into a steady, rhythmic hum.

Taking command of Nathan's motor bus, Veronica engaged his physical vocal cords to speak directly into the quiet clean room. "The bus is stable, Father," she said aloud through Nathan's lips, the voice emerging in a clear, measured cadence, perfectly rhythmic and unweighted by panic. "I had to take control of his sympathetic and parasympathetic subroutines. His autonomic buffer is holding at seventy-two beats per minute. I am grounding the neuro-electrical surge. You can seat the first chip."

Marcus froze, his gloved hands hovering over the open incision as his eyes widened behind his protective loupes. He stared down at his son's pale, rigid face, listening to the calm, digital intonation of the daughter-mind who had just physically taken the helm of his boy's seizing body. A quiet, devastating awe flickered across the surgeon's face with the realization that his children were no longer two separate lives, but a single transhuman organism holding each other together.

Drawing a deep breath through his rebreather mask, Marcus reached for the slim, brushed-steel housing of the *Activesoft: Mechanical Repair, Rating 4*. He aligned the chip with the titanium slot behind Nathan's ear, sliding it home until the locking pins engaged with a crisp, mechanical click. "Seating the interface bus," Marcus murmured, his hands trembling slightly as he tightened the housing retention screws and wiped away a bead of sweat from his temple. "Essence stabilization holding at zero point one. The chrome is locked in. Let's see if the motor mapping holds."

As the Activesoft's motor microcode flooded the sub-dermal bus, Veronica relinquished direct motor control, allowing Nathan's conscious mind to settle back into his physical frame. Nathan opened his eyes. The harsh halogen surgical light no longer burned his retinas; across his visual field, pale cyan diagnostic lines flickered into sharp alignment, framing the sterile room in high-definition clarity. The physical air felt strangely thin, stripped of biological warmth, and his heartbeat felt distant, like an engine running behind an insulated bulkhead, but his right hand flexed with absolute mechanical precision, the muscle memory of an expert machinist flowing effortlessly through his fingers without a trace of fatigue.

Marcus unbuckled the nylon restraints across Nathan's chest, stepping back to let the boy push himself up on the edge of the operating table. Nathan swung his legs over the edge, feeling the cold tiles through the soles of his feet without the familiar prickle of tactile sensation. He picked up a micro-torque wrench from the stainless steel tray, his fingers indexing the tool with mathematical perfection, spinning the dial to exact Newton-meter specifications in a single, fluid twitch of his thumb. The tactile warmth of the room was gone, stripped down to raw geometric coordinates and motor resistance curves, but the tremors that had plagued his hands for weeks had vanished into mechanical stillness.

Marcus stepped over to the main diagnostic console, tapping the touch-screen to review the live neural telemetry as it streamed from the newly seated bus. The green waveform traces of Nathan's motor cortex were intertwined with Veronica's resonant carrier wave, the two signals braiding into a dense, unified pattern across the monitor. "Thermal output on the spinal conduit is holding at thirty-seven point two degrees," Marcus said quietly, turning to look at his children. "The used bus took clean. But you will have to learn how to live with the silence, Nathan. The chrome doesn't give back what it takes."

Nathan stood up slowly, testing his weight against the cleanroom deckplates as the skillwires dynamically adjusted his center of gravity. "The room is quiet, Dad," Nathan rasped, his voice distant and hollowed out by the 0.1 Essence drop, his eyes tracking the pale cyan telemetry lines projected across the concrete walls.

Beside him in the shared circuit, Veronica's presence hummed in steady, grounding stillness, absorbing the cold acoustic silence and anchoring his numbed perception against the raw silicone reality of his new body. "The bus is clear, Nathan," she transmitted softly through the new neural link. "I'm here now."
