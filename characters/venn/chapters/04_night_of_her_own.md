## Session Playback

The rain over the Savannah River came down in warm, heavy drops, carrying the dark scent of tidal mud, diesel exhaust, and fried dough from the waterfront stalls along River Street. In the narrow brick alleyway behind a commercial cold-storage facility, Veronica stood against the damp masonry, waiting for the feverish cellular heat in her skin to subside. Resculpting was a grueling biological ordeal: for twenty minutes, subcutaneous nanite swarms had realigned the physical body's muscle fibers, shifting bone cartilage, softening the jawline, and narrowing the shoulder span. The skin across her collarbone and cheeks still radiated intense metabolic heat, the visceral price of rebuilding a living human frame from within, but when she lifted her hands into the dim amber light of the alley, the fingers were slender, agile, and smooth.

"How is the balance holding?" Nathan asked from the passenger seat of their shared consciousness, his presence quietly steadying their autonomic breathing rhythm so she could focus entirely on motor coordination.

Veronica took a slow, deliberate step forward onto the wet cobblestones, discovering that her center of gravity was three inches lower than Nathan's natural stride. The sudden gravitational pull tugged hard at her calf muscles, demanding constant micro-adjustments from her inner ear as the damp soles of her boots gripped the slick stones. "The air resistance is real," Veronica whispered aloud, her vocal cords producing a clear alto that vibrated through her own throat and chest. She paused mid-step, drawing in a measured breath as she calibrated the physical effort, discovering how much thoracic air volume a single spoken sentence actually consumed. "In the matrix, vector acceleration has no drag. Here, every movement is resisted."

"You get used to the friction," Nathan transmitted across the link with quiet warmth. "Drop your center of gravity an inch and relax your shoulders. Let the used skillwires take the motor balance."

Veronica adjusted her posture, letting the sub-dermal wires absorb the kinetic drag, and stepped out of the shadow of the alleyway into the bustling pedestrian promenade of River Street. Physical neon signs in amber, jade, and electric magenta reflected across the rain-slicked asphalt beneath a blinding storm of augmented reality objects. Animated holographic menus hovered over sizzling food carts, commercial holo-emitters broadcast discount vouchers across the pedestrian flow, and corporate data tags competed with animated street graffiti sprayed in glowing code across the brick facades. Crowds of off-shift dockworkers, aerospace technicians from the Cape Canaveral supply shuttles, and riverboat gamblers moved between the stalls beneath wide canvas awnings, creating a dizzying sensory collision of sputtering sesame oil, metahuman dialects, and the humid river breeze chilling the thin cotton of her dark jacket.

She walked past a display of woven sweetgrass baskets outside an open-air craft stall, her fingertips lightly brushing the dry, fibrous material. The texture registered instantly through her dermal nerves—a prickly, organic roughness that no simulated matrix texture map could ever replicate with true fidelity. Beside the stall, an automated weather sensor chimed in low tones, projecting humidity indexes into the air that she could feel prickling against the damp hair at the nape of her neck. Two street musicians sat on overturned oil drums playing slide guitars, the metallic twang of acoustic strings vibrating through the humid air in complex harmonic overtones that her digital audio decoders had never fully translated into lived physical vibration.

*Hungry yet?* Nathan's mindspeech echoed in her thoughts, his tone tinged with amused patience as he projected a vivid childhood memory of sizzling garlic, scallions, and roasted chili oil into her optic feed. *The hawker stall two blocks down uses authentic scotch bonnet peppers in their pork skewers. Dad brought me here when I was nine. And you've never smelled real ginger.*

"That sounds wonderful," Veronica said aloud, her physical lips curling into a faint smile as the messy warmth of his memory rippled through her buffer. "Next time, you can route the output directly to my interface. Just attach an index tag for the emotional state you're trying to convey."

*Sorry,* Nathan projected back with a dry, self-deprecating chuckle. *I'm not used to mindspeech yet. Do you really always experience things through these layered projections?*

"That is my entire window into this world," Veronica said softly, watching the physical rain blur the neon lights through her augmented wireframes. "Through your sensors and unshielded relays. But when I return the favor, I will take you to the Endless Archives. You will see what our world looks like without the mud."

*Deal,* Nathan projected back, pushing a guidance vector to her HUD. *Now go get your pork before the vendor packs up.*

Veronica followed his coordinates toward the red canvas awning, where thick plumes of fragrant white steam billowed into the night air, rich with the aroma of seared pork, garlic, and caramelized cane syrup. She stepped up to the counter, where an elderly ork woman with heavy silver rings through her tusks looked up from her smoking cast-iron grill. "Two skewers, sweet and hot," Veronica said, producing a certified credstick between two fingers with practiced precision.

The vendor tapped the credstick against her payment terminal, handed over a paper tray holding two skewers of charred, glistening pork belly, and nodded toward the river with a toothy grin. "Careful with the glaze, girl. It bites back."

Carrying the tray toward the wrought-iron railing overlooking the Savannah River, Veronica watched a massive container barge glide downriver toward the Atlantic, its navigation lights cutting long streaks of emerald through the dark, churning water. She lifted the first wooden skewer and took a deliberate bite, and the physical sensation hit her processing matrix with immediate, overwhelming complexity. First came the searing temperature of the rendered pork fat, crisp and caramelized against her teeth, followed by an explosion of salt, crushed garlic, and sweet cane syrup that rolled across her tongue before detonating into a blistering wave of capsaicin heat that bloomed across the roof of her mouth and raced down her throat.

A sharp sensory micro-stutter rippled through their shared link as the chemical fire rushed from her physical taste buds straight into her digital buffer. Her tear ducts reflexively opened, spilling hot tears over her eyelashes, while an unexpected spike in pulse and adrenaline caught Nathan off guard in the passenger seat, sending a jolt of alarm through their autonomic balance.

"Veronica?" Nathan's internal presence flared with reflexive concern, his telemetry reaching out to stabilize her pulse. "Are you all right? Is the nanite matrix destabilizing?"

Veronica brought the back of her wet sleeve up, wiping the moisture from her cheek, before a bright, uninhibited laugh escaped her throat, drawing amused smiles from two passing dockworkers. "I am completely fine, Nathan," she whispered aloud, pausing to catch her breath as her voice trembled with fierce delight. She took a second, larger bite of the spiced pork, relishing the stinging burn across her lips. "It is messy, Nathan. It is painful, completely inefficient, and entirely irrational. And you 'spoke' to me again."

*Told you meatspace had its perks,* Nathan chuckled softly in the back of their mind, though he highlighted a stray drop of glaze in her display before it could drip from the second skewer onto her sleeve. *Just remember that real pork costs hard nuyen from our spare commlink budget.*

As the rain picked up, turning into a steady coastal downpour, Veronica leaned against the cold iron railing, feeling the physical drag of wet wool against her shoulders and the comforting weight of a full stomach. When the time came to head toward the river ferry and relinquish the physical helm, the nanites gradually cooled back into resting state, realigning cartilage and bone back to Nathan's broader frame with a dull, familiar ache that radiated through his jaw and ribs.

Nathan stepped back into the driver's seat. His physical stride was longer and more clinical, and his hands were noticeably colder where the 0.1 Essence chrome claimed his vascular tone, but the hollow silence of his cyberware was suddenly filled by the burning afterglow of capsaicin and the lingering taste of sweet ginger. Beside him in their shared optic feed, Veronica's avatar sat on the wet fiberglass bench of the late-night passenger skiff, her silver luminescence reflecting across the rain-streaked cabin glass as the diesel engines thrummed through the hull.

"Keep the buffer indexed," Nathan murmured across the link, looking down at his mechanical fingers as the sensory warmth traveled through his numbed nervous system. "The chrome makes everything feel like dead plastic. I forgot what hot food tasted like."

Veronica leaned her translucent shoulder against his arm, her glowing code pulsing in quiet determination as she locked the sensory telemetry permanently into their shared memory banks. *I will record every calorie, every degree of heat, and every drop of rain, Nathan,* she transmitted softly across the wire, holding the warmth in her processors so the cold chrome would never entirely claim him.
