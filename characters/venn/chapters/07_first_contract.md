## Session Playback

A cold winter drizzle fell across the rusted rail spurs of the Fulton industrial yards. Water pooled between the blackened ballast gravel, reflecting the distant sodium floodlights of the perimeter fence in dull amber streaks. The damp night air carried the sharp chemical bite of creosote ties and wet coal dust.

In the shadowed alcove between two stacked freight containers, Nathan leaned against the corrugated steel, his dark oilskin duster buttoned tight against the Atlanta chill. Behind his right ear, his used skilljack hummed with a faint sub-dermal warmth as an *Activesoft: Firearms (Rating 6)* streamed high-cadence motor microcode directly into his shoulder and forearm muscles. His fingers rested lightly on the grip of a heavy composite pistol loaded with non-lethal capsule rounds, the motor impulses feeling like instinctive muscle memory wired into his tendons.

In his optic nerves, Veronica projected the tactical matrix overlay.

"Three security personnel inside the staging warehouse," Veronica transmitted across their shared neural circuit. "Two automated surveillance turrets mounted on the roof trusses. The shipping manifest confirms container Alpha-Seven holds forty cases of military-grade neuro-immunosuppressants and gene-stabilization packs stamped for Evo Pharmaceuticals."

*What is the encryption level on the personnel hatch?* Nathan asked internally.

"Rating 3 maglock linked to a localized security bus," Veronica replied, her computational confidence steady in their mind. "Standard corporate architecture. I am routing an intrusion vector through an unshielded wireless diagnostic tap on the climate regulator."

Venn moved in synchronization across the rail siding.

Nathan pushed off the steel container, crossing the wet gravel in a low, economical sprint. His boots made no sound against the ballast as the skillwires dynamically adjusted his stride balance.

As he reached the warehouse personnel door, Veronica struck through the Matrix.

Her digital presence hit the local security network like a surgical blade. She bypassed the host firewall with an elegant sleaze exploit, looping the optical feeds of the ceiling cameras and dropping the electronic deadbolts on the reinforced door with a muffled mechanical click.

Nathan breached the threshold.

Inside, the three private security guards scrambled from their metal desks, reaching for holstered sidearms. Before their weapons cleared leather, Nathan's skillwires fired with flawless, mechanical speed. His right arm snapped into alignment, delivering three double-taps in rapid succession. The heavy capsule rounds ruptured on impact, releasing pressurized anesthetic gel that dropped all three guards to the concrete floor in deep, harmless sedation.

"Guards neutralized," Nathan said aloud, stepping over the threshold.

"Surveillance loop holding for three minutes and forty seconds," Veronica reported, her analysis crisp. "Alpha-Seven is forty meters ahead along the central freight lane."

Nathan jogged down the center aisle of the warehouse, arriving at the designated cargo crate. He applied a portable pneumatic pry-bar to the shipping latches, cracking open the insulated thermal seal. Inside lay neat rows of military-grade medical cases, worth over fifty thousand Nuyen on the shadow market.

*We take what we need for our activesoft subscriptions and nanite maintenance,* Nathan noted, hoisting two heavy cases into his tactical duffel bag. *That covers our expenses through spring.*

"And three cases for the Fulton perimeter clinic," Veronica added firmly, her moral compass asserting itself across their link. "Dr. Ortiz has been treating chrome rejections with expired saline since November. The algorithmic risk of the extra weight is offset by community stability."

"Parnell is going to complain about the reduced haul," Nathan noted with a faint wry grin as he loaded the additional crates onto a hand trolley.

"Parnell operates on short-term margin," Veronica replied. "We operate on long-term survival. An operational free clinic ensures our own medical anonymity."

"Agreed," Nathan said, rolling the trolley toward the loading bay.

One hour later, a beat-up civilian delivery van pulled into the alley behind a shuttered storefront in the Atlanta squatter zone. Nathan unloaded the three medical cases onto the rear loading ramp of the clinic.

An exhausted, middle-aged human doctor in a blood-stained apron stepped out into the rain, staring in disbelief at the pristine Evo pharmaceutical stamps on the insulated containers.

"Where did you get these?" Dr. Ortiz breathed, his hands trembling as he touched the cold seals. "This is enough immunosuppressant stock to keep two dozen street kids from rejecting their implants this winter. Who do I owe for this?"

Nathan stepped back into the shadows of the alley, pulling the collar of his coat high against the drizzle. Beside him in augmented reality, Veronica stood in silent solidarity. Cold rain fell through her translucent shoulders, her cyan filaments flickering with a faint static micro-stutter where the water passed through her light, but her luminescence cast a soft silver rim along the wet leather of Nathan's duster and illuminated the doctor's steaming breath in glowing cyan.

"No debt, Doctor," Nathan said calmly. "Just keep the doors open."

At midnight, they met their fixer in a secluded booth at a subterranean bar beneath the Atlanta transit arches.

Parnell, a stout dwarf fixer with gold-rimmed data glasses and a heavy wool overcoat, inspected the remaining medical crates stacked beneath the table. He tapped his commlink terminal, verifying the pharmaceutical serial numbers and initiating the escrow handshake.

As the data synced, Parnell paused, adjusting his glasses as he squinted at the biometric and PAN telemetry streaming across his lenses.

"Look at this diagnostic return," Parnell muttered, turning his small terminal screen toward them. On the display, two distinct neural wave signatures overlapped in the center, pairing Nathan's biological spikes with Veronica's Resonant carrier wave. "Two separate consciousness signatures running in a single circuit. Like a Venn diagram. If you don't have a street handle yet, kid, there ya go."

Nathan looked at the display. In the back of their mind, Veronica's presence pulsed with quiet approval. For a fraction of a second, his physical voice aligned with her resonant digital tone, their voices merging in clean consensus.

"Venn works," they answered.
