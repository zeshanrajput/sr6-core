# Shadowrun 6e Narrative Standards & Literary Guidelines

This document defines the foundational narrative and literary standards for all Shadowrun 6th Edition character portfolios (`sr6-core`, `sr6yuriko`, `sr6velvet`, `sr6union`, etc.). All narrative drafts, chapter updates, and agentic revisions must comply with these guidelines.

---

## 1. Show-Don't-Tell & Tactical Grounding

### 1.1 Sensory-Mechanical Integration
* **Rule**: Mechanics must never be stated as abstracted meta-text ("She rolled 6 hits on Decoding"), nor should they be ignored. Mechanics translate directly into physical, digital, or astral sensory reality.
* **Meatspace**: Recoil is felt as shoulder bruise and hydraulic dampener hiss; smartlink readouts project wind vector arcs across retinas; wired reflexes feel like the rest of the world drowning in slow-motion syrup.
* **Matrix**: Programs are not abstract files; they are active code construct manifestations. Data processing manifests as ambient heat, encryption appears as geometric armor or shifting cryptographic walls, and IC (Intrusion Countermeasures) operates as predator fauna.
* **Astral**: Essence is color purity and breath; mana flows like currents in water; dark aura flares signal deception or malicious intent; cyberware shows as cold, dead grey voids carved into a metahuman's aura.

### 1.2 Info-Dump Elimination
* **Rule**: Never pause scene momentum to explain Shadowrun lore, corporate history, or weapon specifications in textbook format.
* **Execution**: Weave lore strictly through character interaction, tactical necessity, and environmental observation. If a runner knows Ares Macrotechnology owns the precinct, show the corporate stamp on the drone's chassis or the guard's badge, not a paragraph explaining the corporate court decision of 2059.

---

## 2. Scene Architecture & Pacing

### 2.1 Entry and Exit Rules
* **Arrive Late**: Start every scene as deep into the action or conflict as possible. Cut pre-scene travel, mundane setup, and unnecessary greetings unless they establish immediate tension.
* **Leave Early**: Exit the scene immediately after the narrative turn or emotional climax is reached. Do not linger for post-action recaps, administrative summaries, or polite goodbyes.

### 2.2 Scene Beats & Escalation Curve
Every narrative chapter must follow a clear 4-beat structure:
1. **Inciting Friction (0-20%)**: Immediate tactical, emotional, or environmental obstacle. Establish stakes without throat-clearing.
2. **Rising Tension & Escalation (20-60%)**: Complications stack. The initial plan fails or mutates. Environmental or mechanical friction forces character adaptation.
3. **Climax & Consequential Decision (60-85%)**: High-stakes moment requiring an active, irreversible choice by the protagonist.
4. **Resonance & Aftermath (85-100%)**: Immediate fallout of the choice. Shift in state (physical, resource, or relational) leading directly into the next narrative hook.

### 2.3 Action-to-Exposition Balance
* Maintain an **80/20 balance**: 80% active sensory prose, character dialogue, internal tactical calculation, and environmental friction; 20% contextual grounding.

---

## 3. Dialogue Discipline & Character Voice

### 3.1 Subtext Over Direct Statements
* Characters rarely say exactly what they mean, especially in the shadows where trust is fatal.
* Use body language, micro-expressions, tactical positioning, and clipped cadence to convey fear, authority, or deception.

### 3.2 Shadowrun Jargon & Street Authenticity
* Use SR slang naturally (`Chummer`, `Nuyen`, `Drek`, `Frag`, `Deck`, `Slot`, `Wired`, `Corp-sec`, `SINner`).
* Never explain slang within the prose. Trust the reader's immersion.
* Match jargon density to the character's background:
  * Corporate operatives use clinical acronyms and corporate PR speak.
  * Street runners use dense regional street-slang.
  * Technomancers/AIs frame reality in code, data silt, and Resonance metaphors.
  * Adepts/Mages use tradition-specific metaphysical terminology.

---

## 4. Protagonist Agency & Stakes

### 4.1 Proactive Choice Over Passive Reaction
* The protagonist must drive the scene. Even when ambushed or trapped, their survival depends on deliberate, tactical choices—not luck or external deus ex machina.
* Every major action must carry tangible consequences: resource expenditure (ammo, Edge, drain, nuyen), collateral damage, or compromised stealth.

### 4.2 Mechanical & Physical Realism
* Tracking damage and drain is mandatory. A runner suffering 3 boxes of Physical damage bleeds, moves slower, and feels pain.
* Ammunition counts, matrix mark limits, and spell drain must reflect authentic SR6 rules constraints.

---

## 5. Dystopian Tone & Cyberpunk Aesthetics

### 5.1 The Dystopian Contrast
* Always contrast technological brilliance with human and environmental decay. High-tech AR displays flashing over rain-slicked, garbage-filled alleyways; pristine corporate arcologies towering above neon-drenched, pollution-choked sprawl.

### 5.2 Corporate Omnipresence
* The megacorporations (Ares, Aztechnology, Renraku, Saeder-Krupp, Shiawase, Mitsuhama, etc.) are invisible titans looming over every transaction. Corporate logos, broadcast advertisements, drone surveillance grids, and branded synthetic food products define daily existence.

### 5.3 Sensory Restraint & Cyberpunk Tropes
* **Eliminate Sensory Shortcuts**: Avoid repetitive cliché electrical/cyberpunk descriptors:
  - *Banned Phrases/Shortcuts*: `burnt copper`, `hot solder`, `chemical tang of processing`, `puddles of stale encryption`, `decaying logic in the gutters`, `systems redlining`, `processing at 600%`.
* **Replace with Concrete Grounding**: Use specific acoustic resonance, thermal dissipation shifts, tactile mass/recoil, barometric pressure changes, and structural defamiliarization.
* **Audio Narration & TTS Readability**: When authoring dialogue for portfolios with embedded audio narration, avoid excessive trailing ellipses (`...`) for hesitation; prefer concrete physical beats, micro-expressions, or deliberate word choice. Maintain an ellipses density <= 0.6 per 300 words.
